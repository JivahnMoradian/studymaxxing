The Whole-Course Cheat Sheet

Every must-know formula and rule for intro macro, Units 1–6, on one page. Hit print for a revision sheet.

Unit 1 · Basic Economic Concepts

  • Opportunity Cost = value of the next-best alternative given up. PPC: on the curve = efficient, inside = idle resources, outside = unattainable. Bowed out ⇒ increasing opportunity cost; growth shifts it outward.
  • Comparative Advantage = lower opportunity cost (trade by this, not absolute advantage). Both gain if the terms of trade fall between the two opportunity costs.
  • Market: equilibrium at Qd = Qs. Binding price ceiling (below eq) ⇒ shortage; binding price floor (above eq) ⇒ surplus.

Unit 2 · Indicators & the Business Cycle

  • GDP (Spending): Y = C + I + G + NX, with NX = X − M. Counts final goods produced within borders in a year; excludes used goods, intermediate goods and transfers.
  • Value Added = revenue − intermediate goods. GDP = GNP − net income from abroad (GDP is by location, GNP by residents).
  • Real GDP = Nominal GDP ÷ (Deflator/100); Deflator = (Nominal ÷ Real) × 100. Growth rate = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100.
  • Unemployment Rate = Unemployed ÷ Labor Force × 100; LFPR = Labor Force ÷ Adult Population × 100. Natural rate = frictional + structural; cyclical = actual − natural.
  • CPI = Cost of basket ÷ Cost in base year × 100. Inflation = (CPI2 − CPI1) ÷ CPI1 × 100. Real rate r ≈ i − π.
  • Business Cycle: expansion → peak → recession → trough. Unemployment is cyclical, rising in recessions.

Unit 3 · National Income & Price Determination

  • AD slopes down (wealth, interest-rate, exchange-rate effects). Shifters: C, I, G, NX. SRAS shifts with input costs, productivity, expectations, taxes/regulation; LRAS shifts with resources, technology, institutions.
  • Equilibrium: AD = SRAS. Recessionary gap: Y < Yf (high unemployment); inflationary gap: Y > Yf. Self-correction: wages fall (or rise) and SRAS shifts back to Yf.
  • Multiplier: MPC + MPS = 1; spending multiplier = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS; tax multiplier = −MPC/(1 − MPC); balanced-budget multiplier = 1. ΔY = multiplier × Δspending.
  • Fiscal Policy: expansionary = ↑G or ↓T; contractionary = ↓G or ↑T. Close a gap with ΔG = gap ÷ multiplier. Automatic stabilizers act without new law. Limits: crowding out, lags.
  • Keynesian Cross (college): equilibrium where Y = AE. If Y > AE, unplanned inventories pile up and firms cut output; if Y < AE they raise it. Unplanned Inventory Change = Y − AE.

Unit 4 · The Financial Sector

  • Money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value. M1 (most liquid) ⊂ M2. Required reserves = rr × deposits; excess = reserves − required.
  • Money Multiplier = 1/rr; maximum ΔM = ΔReserves × 1/rr.
  • Time Value: FV = PV(1 + r)n, PV = FV/(1 + r)n. Rule of 70: doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate (%). Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions.
  • Money Market (nominal i): MS vertical, MD downward. Loanable Funds (real r): deficits and lower saving raise r; more saving lowers r.
  • Fed Tools: buy bonds ⇒ ↑reserves, ↑MS, ↓i, ↑AD (expansionary); sell bonds is the reverse. Also the discount rate, reserve requirement and interest on reserves.
  • IS-LM (college): IS (goods market) slopes down; LM (money market) slopes up. Fiscal expansion shifts IS right (↑Y, ↑r, some crowding out); monetary expansion shifts LM right (↑Y, ↓r). A higher price level shifts LM left, which is why AD slopes down.
  • Reserve Market: demand slopes down, flat at the discount rate (top) and at IORB (bottom); supply is vertical. Ample reserves (supply on the flat part): federal funds rate = IORB, OMOs change reserves but not the rate, and the Fed moves the rate by changing IORB. Scarce reserves (supply on the sloping part): OMOs move the rate.

Unit 5 · Long-Run Consequences of Stabilization Policies

  • Phillips Curve: SRPC shows a trade-off between inflation and unemployment; the LRPC is vertical at the natural rate. SRPC shifts with expected inflation and supply shocks.
  • Quantity Theory: MV = PY, so %ΔM + %ΔV ≈ %ΔP + %ΔY. Money is neutral in the long run. Fisher: i ≈ r + πe.
  • Deficit = G − T per year; debt = accumulated deficits. Deficits raise the demand for loanable funds ⇒ crowding out of private investment.
  • Growth: productivity = Y/L. Sources: physical capital, human capital, technology. Per-capita growth ≈ GDP growth − population growth.
  • Supply-Side: policies that raise potential output (LRAS). Taylor Rule: i = π + r* + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5(Output Gap). Rules vs. discretion; time inconsistency.

Unit 6 · The Open Economy

  • Balance of Payments: Current Account + Capital/Financial Account ≈ 0. A current-account deficit is financed by net capital inflows.
  • Identity: NX = S − I = NCO (net capital outflow).
  • Exchange Rate: the price of one currency in another. Appreciation raises the currency's value. Shifters: relative interest rates, inflation, income, tastes, speculation. PPP: P = e·P*.
  • Depreciation ⇒ exports ↑, imports ↓, NX ↑. Flexible rates: expansionary monetary policy ⇒ ↓r ⇒ capital outflow ⇒ depreciation ⇒ NX ↑ (amplifies the effect).
  • Tariff: raises the domestic price, helps domestic producers and the government, hurts consumers, and creates deadweight loss.
  • Impossible Trinity (college): a country cannot have a fixed exchange rate, free capital flows and an independent monetary policy all at once; it must give one up.
