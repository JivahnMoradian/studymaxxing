Scarcity, Opportunity Cost & the PPC

Read the theory → Drill 1.1 →

A. Opportunity Cost from a Table

(Problems 1–5: an economy's production possibilities for laptops and tablets, in thousands of units.)

Combination Laptops Tablets
A 0 70
B 10 66
C 20 58
D 30 44
E 40 26
F 50 0

Problem 1. What is the opportunity cost (in tablets) of each extra laptop when moving from A to B?

Show solution

Tablets given up = 70 − 66 = 4 for 10 more laptops, so the opportunity cost is 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4 tablets per laptop.

Problem 2. What is the opportunity cost per laptop when moving from C to D?

Show solution

Tablets given up = 58 − 44 = 14 for 10 more laptops, so 1.4 tablets per laptop.

Problem 3. Does this PPC show constant or increasing opportunity cost? Support your answer with numbers.

Show solution

Increasing. The cost per laptop rises at each step: 0.4 (A→B), 0.8 (B→C), 1.4 (C→D), 1.8 (D→E), 2.6 (E→F). The curve is bowed outward.

Problem 4. Is producing 20 thousand laptops and 40 thousand tablets attainable? Efficient?

Show solution

At 20 laptops the PPC allows 58 tablets, and 40 < 58. The point is attainable but inefficient (inside the curve): resources are idle or misallocated.

Problem 5. Is producing 30 thousand laptops and 50 thousand tablets attainable?

Show solution

At 30 laptops the maximum is 44 tablets, and 50 > 44. The point is unattainable (outside the curve) with current resources and technology.

B. Positions, Shifts, and Growth

Problem 6. A recession leaves many workers unemployed. Where is the economy relative to its PPC?

Show solution

Inside the PPC: resources are not fully employed, so the economy produces less than it could of both goods. Reemployment moves it back toward the curve; it does not shift the curve.

Problem 7. For each event, say whether the PPC shifts outward, pivots, shifts inward, or does not shift: (a) a new, more productive way to make only wheat; (b) a hurricane destroys half the country's factories; (c) the economy moves from point A to point B on the curve; (d) immigration expands the labor force.

Show solution

(a) Pivots outward along the wheat axis. (b) Shifts inward (capital stock falls). (c) No shift: it is a movement along the curve. (d) Shifts outward (more labor).

For part a), a technology gain in only Wheat pivots the PPC outward along the X-axis; the Y-intercept does not move. Parts b), c) and d) are not graphed.

Problem 8. Economy 1 produces mostly capital goods this year; Economy 2 produces mostly consumer goods. Both start with identical PPCs. Which will have the larger PPC in ten years, and why?

Show solution

Economy 1. Capital goods add to future productive capacity, so its PPC shifts out more. The cost is fewer consumer goods today, which is the opportunity cost of growth.

Problem 9. True or false: "A point inside the PPC is impossible to produce."

Show solution

False. Points inside are attainable but inefficient. Points outside are the impossible ones.

C. Linear PPFs (Computing Opportunity Cost)

Problem 10. In one day a worker can make 12 shirts or 8 pairs of pants. Find the opportunity cost of one shirt, and of one pair of pants.

Show solution

Making 12 shirts means giving up 8 pants, so 1 shirt costs 8/12 = 2/3 pants.
Making 8 pants means giving up 12 shirts, so 1 pair of pants costs 12/8 = 1.5 shirts.
The two costs are reciprocals.

Problem 11. A farm can produce 90 bushels of corn or 45 bushels of soybeans. What is the opportunity cost of one bushel of soybeans?

Show solution

Producing 45 soybeans means giving up 90 corn, so 1 soybean = 90/45 = 2 bushels of corn.

Problem 12. A country moves from 30 to 54 units of good X along a straight-line PPC and gives up 18 units of good Y. What is the opportunity cost of one unit of X, and what is the Y-axis intercept if the X-axis intercept is 90?

Show solution

Extra X = 54 − 30 = 24, so opportunity cost = 18/24 = 0.75 Y per X.
With a constant cost, the full X intercept of 90 means giving up 90 × 0.75 = 67.5 units of Y, so the Y intercept is 67.5.

More Practice — A. Opportunity Cost from a Table

(Problems 13–18: production possibilities for autos and bikes, in thousands.)

Combination Autos (thousands) Bikes (thousands)
A 0 90
B 8 84
C 16 74
D 24 60
E 32 40
F 40 14

Problem 13. Find the opportunity cost (in bikes) of one extra auto when moving from A to B.

Show solution

Bikes given up = 90 − 84 = 6 for 8 more autos: 6 ÷ 8 = 0.75 bikes per auto.

Problem 14. Find the opportunity cost per auto of moving from D to E.

Show solution

Bikes given up = 60 − 40 = 20 for 8 more autos: 2.5 bikes per auto.

Problem 15. Which single step along the table has the highest opportunity cost per auto, and what does that say about the PPC's shape?

Show solution

The costs per step are 0.75, 1.25, 1.75, 2.5, 3.25. The highest is E
→ F (3.25 bikes per auto). Costs keep rising, so the PPC is bowed outward (increasing opportunity cost).

Problem 16. Find the average opportunity cost per auto of moving from C to E.

Show solution

Bikes given up = 74 − 40 = 34 for 32 − 16 = 16 more autos: 34 ÷ 16 = 2.125 bikes per auto.

Problem 17. Classify each point as on the PPC, inside it, or unattainable: (24, 65); (32, 30); (16, 74).

Show solution

At 24 autos the maximum is 60 bikes, so (24, 65) is unattainable. At 32 autos the maximum is 40, so (32, 30) is attainable but inefficient (inside). (16, 74) is point C: on the PPC (efficient).

Problem 18. If a new technology lets the economy produce 20% more of both goods at every point, what are the new maximum autos and maximum bikes?

Show solution

Both intercepts rise by 20%: maximum autos = 40 × 1.2 = 48 thousand; maximum bikes = 90 × 1.2 = 108 thousand.
The whole PPC shifts outward.

More Practice — B. Attainable, Efficient, or Unattainable?

Problem 19. A country can produce up to 40 units of X or 60 units of Y, with constant opportunity cost. Is the combination (20, 30) attainable, and is it efficient?

Show solution

The PPF is the line Y = 60 − 1.5X.
At X = 20, the maximum Y is 30.
The point has Y = 30, so it is on the PPF (efficient).

Problem 20. A country can produce up to 40 units of X or 60 units of Y, with constant opportunity cost. Is the combination (10, 40) attainable, and is it efficient?

Show solution

The PPF is the line Y = 60 − 1.5X.
At X = 10, the maximum Y is 45.
The point has Y = 40, so it is inside the PPF (attainable but inefficient).

The point has Y = 30, so it is outside the PPF (unattainable).

More Practice — C. Linear PPFs: Opportunity Costs

Problem 24. A worker can produce 18 units of X or 45 units of Y per day. Find the opportunity cost of one unit of X and of one unit of Y.

Show solution

Opp. cost of 1 X = max Y ÷ max X = 45/18 = 2.5 Y.
Opp. cost of 1 Y = max X ÷ max Y = 18/45 = 0.4 X.
The two are reciprocals.

Problem 25. A worker can produce 35 units of X or 14 units of Y per day. Find the opportunity cost of one unit of X and of one unit of Y.

Show solution

Opp. cost of 1 X = max Y ÷ max X = 14/35 = 0.4 Y.
Opp. cost of 1 Y = max X ÷ max Y = 35/14 = 2.5 X.
The two are reciprocals.

More Practice — D. Shifts vs. Movements

Problem 29. Does a rise in the unemployment rate move an economy along its PPC, inside it, or shift the PPC?

Show solution

A rise in unemployment moves the economy inside the PPC (idle resources). It does not shift the curve.

Problem 30. A country discovers a large new oil field. What happens to its PPC?

Show solution

More resources: the PPC shifts outward (for goods that use oil, possibly more along that axis, but both intercepts can rise).

Problem 31. A new software tool raises productivity in manufacturing only. Show the effect on a PPC with manufactured goods on the horizontal axis.

Show solution

The curve pivots outward along the manufactured-goods axis; the other intercept does not move.
