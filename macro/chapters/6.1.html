Open Economies and the Balance of Payments

In an open economy, residents trade goods and services with the rest of the world and also buy and sell assets (stocks, bonds, factories, bank deposits) across borders. The Balance of Payments (BOP) is the accounting record of all of a country's international transactions in a period. Follow the money:

  • A transaction that brings money into the country is a credit (+).
  • A transaction that sends money out of the country is a debit (−).

The Two Accounts

Account What It Records Examples (US Perspective)
Current account (CA) Net exports, net income from abroad, net transfers Exports and imports of goods and services (NX); interest, dividends and wages earned abroad minus those paid to foreigners (net primary income); remittances and foreign aid (net transfers)
Capital and financial account (CFA) Purchases and sales of assets between countries Foreigners buying US Treasury bonds, stocks or real estate (inflow, +); a US firm building a plant abroad or a US fund buying foreign bonds (outflow, −)
CA = NX + Net Income from Abroad + Net Transfers
CFA = Foreign Purchases of Domestic Assets - Domestic Purchases of Foreign Assets
CA + CFA = 0

Every international sale of goods is matched by a flow of money, and that money is either spent on goods again or used to buy assets. So the BOP always sums to zero: a current account deficit is exactly offset by a capital and financial account surplus (a net financial inflow), and vice versa. The CA and the CFA individually can be in surplus or deficit; only their sum is zero.

Goods: X = 1,500, M = 1,900
Services: X = 800, M = 600
Net Income = +120
Net Transfers = -60
NX = (1,500 + 800) - (1,900 + 600) = -200 ⟹ CA = -200 + 120 - 60 = -140 ⟹ CFA = +140

The CA deficit of 140 is financed by a CFA surplus of 140: foreigners acquire 140 more in US assets than Americans acquire abroad.

Trade Balance, NX and Net Capital Outflow

  • The Balance of Trade is exports minus imports of goods (sometimes goods and services). Net Exports (NX) = exports − imports of goods and services. A negative NX is a trade deficit.
  • NX is the largest piece of the CA, so a trade deficit usually means a CA deficit.
  • Net Capital Outflow (NCO) = domestic purchases of foreign assets − foreign purchases of domestic assets. It is the negative of the CFA: when the CFA is in surplus, NCO is negative (net capital inflow).

The Saving–Investment Identity

Start from the output identity and rearrange. Total spending on domestic output is Y = C + I + G + NX. National saving is the income not consumed or spent by the government, S = Y − C − G. Then:

S = Y - C - G
NX = S - I = NCO
Y = C + I + G + NX ⟹ NX = Y - C - G - I = S - I

A country that saves more than it invests domestically lends the difference abroad (NCO > 0, trade surplus). A country that invests more than it saves borrows the difference from abroad (NCO < 0, trade deficit, CFA surplus).

GDP vs. GNP and the Current Account

GDP counts production inside the country's borders; GNP counts production by the country's residents wherever it takes place. As in Chapter 2.1, GNP equals GDP plus net income from abroad (income residents earn abroad minus income foreigners earn domestically):

GNP = GDP + Net Income from Abroad

That same net income from abroad is the second component of the current account.

Drawing It Yourself: list every item with its sign (+ for money in, − for money out), add them to get the CA, then write the CFA as the same number with the opposite sign.

Go Deeper: College-Level

Official Reserves and the Statistical Discrepancy: Central bank transactions in foreign reserves are part of the financial account. In practice the recorded credits and debits never match exactly, so a "statistical discrepancy" (errors and omissions) balances the books.

Stocks vs. Flows: The BOP records flows over a year. The Net International Investment Position is the stock: foreign assets owned by residents minus domestic assets owned by foreigners. Persistent CA deficits shrink (make more negative) the position, so a country becomes a net debtor.

Twin Deficits: Since NX = S − I and S = private saving + public saving, a larger government deficit lowers national saving; holding private saving and investment fixed, NX falls one-for-one.
