UNIT 2 — ECONOMIC INDICATORS & THE BUSINESS CYCLE

Real vs. Nominal GDP & the Deflator

Practice these problems →

Why We Need Two Measures

GDP is a dollar total, so it can rise for two very different reasons: firms produce more, or they simply charge more. Only the first makes people better off.

  • Nominal GDP values output at current-year prices. It measures how much is spent on output.
  • Real GDP values output at the prices of a fixed base year. It measures how much is produced, because price changes are removed.
Nominal GDP = (Current Prices) × (Current Quantities)
Real GDP = (Base-Year Prices) × (Current Quantities)

Add these up over every good and service in the economy. Quantities are what change real GDP; both prices and quantities change nominal GDP.

Worked Example: Two Goods, Two Years

Year Price of Books Books Sold Price of Coffee Cups Sold
1 $20 50 $4 200
2 $24 55 $5 220
Nominal GDP1 = 20(50) + 4(200) = 1,800
Nominal GDP2 = 24(55) + 5(220) = 2,420
Real GDP2 (base year 1) = 20(55) + 4(220) = 1,980

Nominal GDP rose 34.4%, but real GDP (base year 1) rose only from 1,800 to 1,980, which is 10%. The rest of the rise was higher prices.

The Base Year

  • In the base year, nominal GDP = real GDP.
  • Choosing a different base year changes the level of real GDP (and slightly changes growth rates), so state the base year every time.
  • To find real GDP in year t with base year b, multiply year-t quantities by year-b prices.

The GDP Deflator

The GDP Deflator is a price index for everything produced domestically. It compares what the output costs at current prices to what it would cost at base-year prices.

GDP Deflator = Nominal GDPReal GDP × 100
Year 2 (base year 1): 2,4201,980 × 100 = 122.2
  • The deflator equals 100 in the base year.
  • A deflator of 122.2 means prices are 22.2% higher than in the base year.
  • Rearranged, this converts nominal values into real ones:
Real GDP = Nominal GDPGDP Deflator × 100

Inflation and Growth Rates

Any percentage change uses the same formula:

% Change = New - OldOld × 100
  • Inflation Rate = % change in the price index (here the deflator).
  • Real GDP Growth = % change in real GDP.
  • Nominal GDP Growth ≈ real growth + inflation, a good approximation for small changes.
Nominal Growth ≈ Real Growth + Inflation

Terms to know: inflation is a rising price level, deflation is a falling price level (negative inflation), and disinflation is a fall in the inflation rate (prices still rise, but more slowly).

Assuming inflation is positive, nominal GDP is above real GDP after the base year and below it before the base year. Nominal GDP can even rise while real GDP falls, whenever prices rise faster than output does.

Deflating a Series

To compare dollar amounts across years, divide each nominal value by that year's price index (base year = 100) and multiply by 100.

Nominal GDP = 11,100, Deflator = 111 ⟹ Real GDP = 11,100111 × 100 = 10,000
Go Deeper: College-Level

Why Multiply Growth Factors: If prices rise by a fraction π and real output by g, nominal GDP is multiplied by (1 + π)(1 + g) = 1 + π + g + πg.
The product πg is tiny for small rates, which is why nominal growth ≈ π + g.

Compound Growth: Real GDP growing at g per year for n years multiplies by (1 + g)n. Growth of 3% for 10 years is about 34.4%, not 30%. This returns in Unit 5.
