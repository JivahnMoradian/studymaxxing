UNIT 2 — ECONOMIC INDICATORS & THE BUSINESS CYCLE

Inflation, Price Indices & the Business Cycle

Practice these problems →

The Consumer Price Index (CPI)

The CPI measures the cost of a fixed market basket of goods and services bought by a typical urban consumer, relative to its cost in a base year.

CPI = Cost of the Basket in the Current YearCost of the Same Basket in the Base Year × 100

The CPI is 100 in the base year. A CPI of 120 means the basket costs 20% more than in the base year.

Basket: 5 pizzas, 4 movie tickets, 30 gallons of gas
Base-year prices: pizza $12, movie ticket $10, gas $3 per gallon
Base-year cost of the basket: 5(12) + 4(10) + 30(3) = 190
Year 2 prices: pizza $15, movie ticket $12, gas $3.50 per gallon
Year 2 cost of the basket: 5(15) + 4(12) + 30(3.50) = 228
CPI2 = 228190 × 100 = 120

Because the basket's quantities stay fixed, only price changes move the index.

Inflation Rate and the Value of Money

The Inflation Rate is the percentage change in a price index from one period to the next.

Inflation Rate = CPIThis Year - CPILast YearCPILast Year × 100

Terms: inflation (price level rising), deflation (price level falling), disinflation (inflation rate falling but still positive).

Inflation erodes purchasing power. If the CPI is 120 (base = 100), a dollar buys only 100 ÷ 120 = 83% of what it bought in the base year.

Real vs. Nominal Variables

A nominal value is measured in current dollars. A real value is adjusted for the price level.

Real Value = Nominal ValueCPI × 100
Hourly wage $18.60 when CPI = 124:
Real Wage = 18.60124 × 100 = $15.00 (in base-year dollars)
% Change in Real Wage ≈ % Change in Nominal Wage - Inflation

A raise that is smaller than inflation is a pay cut in real terms.

To compare dollar amounts across years, multiply by the ratio of price indices. What cost $X when the CPI was A costs $X × (CPIB ÷ A) when the CPI is B.

Real vs. Nominal Interest Rates

The Nominal Interest Rate (i) is the rate stated on a loan. The Real Interest Rate (r) is the rate adjusted for inflation: it measures the true growth of purchasing power.

r = i - π equivalently
i = r + π
  • Ex Ante real rate uses expected inflation (what lenders and borrowers use when they agree to the loan).
  • Ex Post real rate uses actual inflation (what actually happened).
Nominal rate 6%, expected inflation 2.5% ⟹ r = 3.5%
If actual inflation is 4%, the ex post real rate is 6 - 4 = 2%

Biases in the CPI

The CPI tends to overstate true inflation (the true change in the cost of living):

  • Substitution Bias: the fixed basket ignores that consumers switch away from goods whose prices rise.
  • New Products: new goods enter the basket only after a delay, so initial price falls are missed.
  • Quality Improvements: a higher price may reflect a better product, not pure inflation.
  • Outlet Substitution: shoppers move to discount stores.

Costs of Inflation

Cost Meaning Anticipated or Unanticipated?
Shoe-Leather Costs Time and effort spent holding less cash and making more trips to the bank Both (grows with the inflation rate)
Menu Costs Cost of changing prices (reprinting menus, catalogs, repricing) Both
Redistribution of Wealth Unexpected inflation helps borrowers, who repay in cheaper dollars, and hurts lenders and people on fixed nominal incomes Unanticipated
Uncertainty Makes long-term contracts, saving and investment riskier Mostly unanticipated

If inflation is perfectly anticipated, interest rates and wages adjust and there is no arbitrary redistribution. Only the menu and shoe-leather costs remain. Unexpected Deflation redistributes the other way: it helps lenders and hurts borrowers.

Indexing protects against inflation: Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLAs) raise wages or benefits (for example Social Security) with the CPI.

For example, suppose a retiree's monthly Social Security benefit is $1,800 and the CPI rises from 240 to 252 over the year.

Inflation = 252 - 240240 × 100 = 5%
New Benefit = 1,800 × 1.05 = $1,890

The Business Cycle

Real GDP does not grow smoothly. It fluctuates around its long-run upward trend in a business cycle.

  • Expansion: real GDP rises; unemployment falls.
  • Peak: the turning point at the top, where an expansion ends.
  • Recession (Contraction): real GDP falls; unemployment rises. A common rule of thumb is two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP; the official dating is done by the NBER, which looks at broader indicators.
  • Trough: the turning point at the bottom, where a recession ends.

The gap between actual real GDP and potential (full-employment) output is the output gap. In a recession actual output falls below potential and cyclical unemployment appears. Business cycles result from changes in aggregate demand and/or aggregate supply, the subject of Unit 3.

Drawing It Yourself: draw an upward-sloping dashed trend line, then a wavy curve crossing it. Mark each top as a peak and each bottom as a trough. The downhill part from a peak to the next trough is the recession and the uphill part is the expansion.

Go Deeper: College-Level

The Exact Fisher Equation: The approximation r = i − π works for small rates.
Exactly,

1 + i = (1 + r)(1 + π)
i = 8%, π = 5% ⟹ 1 + r = 1.081.05 = 1.0286
r = 2.86% (vs. 3% by approximation)

CPI vs. the GDP deflator vs. PCE: The CPI uses a fixed basket, covers consumer purchases including imports, and is used for COLAs. The GDP deflator uses current-year quantities, covers only domestic production including investment and government output. The Fed targets inflation measured by the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, which updates its basket more often and so shows less substitution bias.

Hyperinflation (conventionally above 50% per month) is caused by money printed to finance government spending; it destroys the usefulness of money. We return to this in Unit 5.

Indicators: Economists classify indicators as leading (stock prices, building permits, new orders), coincident (industrial production, payrolls) and lagging (unemployment duration, inflation). Unemployment typically lags the cycle: it keeps rising for a while after the trough.
