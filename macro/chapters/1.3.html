UNIT 1 — BASIC ECONOMIC CONCEPTS

Supply & Demand Refresher

Practice these problems →

Demand

The Law of Demand: other things equal, a higher price lowers the quantity demanded. The demand curve slopes down.

A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve. Anything else shifts the curve itself. Demand Shifters (remember TIRED):

  • Tastes and preferences
  • Income (normal goods: income up → demand up; inferior goods: income up → demand down)
  • Related goods' prices (substitutes: rival's price up → demand up; complements: partner's price up → demand down)
  • Expectations of future prices
  • Demographics (the number of buyers)

Supply

The Law of Supply: a higher price raises the quantity supplied. The supply curve slopes up. As with demand, a change in the good's own price causes a movement along the supply curve, while anything else shifts the curve itself. Supply Shifters: input prices, technology, the number of sellers, expectations, taxes and subsidies.

Equilibrium

Equilibrium is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Above it there is a surplus (price falls); below it, a shortage (price rises).

Qd = Qs ⟹  solve for P*, then plug back to find Q*
Qd = 180 - 4P, Qs = -30 + 3P ⟹  180 - 4P = -30 + 3P ⟹  210 = 7P ⟹  P* = 30
Q* = 60

Predicting Shifts

Decide which curve moves, and which way.

  • Only Demand Shifts → P and Q move in the same direction as the shift.
  • Only Supply Shifts → P and Q move in opposite directions.
  • Both Shift → one of P or Q is ambiguous; the other is determined.

When demand rises (D1 to D2), the equilibrium price rises from 30 to 34 and the equilibrium quantity rises from 60 to 72.

Price Controls

  • A Price Ceiling (legal maximum) set below equilibrium creates a shortage.
  • A Price Floor (legal minimum) set above equilibrium creates a surplus.

At a controlled price, quantity traded is the smaller of Qd and Qs (the short side of the market).

A binding price ceiling at 24, below the equilibrium price of 30: quantity demanded (84) exceeds quantity supplied (42), a shortage of 42.

A binding price floor at 36, above the equilibrium price of 30: quantity supplied (78) exceeds quantity demanded (36), a surplus of 42.

Why This Matters for Macro: a labor-market price floor is a minimum wage, and sticky wages are the macro version of a price floor above equilibrium. The AD-AS model in Unit 3 is built from these same tools applied to the whole economy.

Go Deeper: College-Level

Solving a Shift Algebraically: Start from the market above, with demand Qd = 180 − 4P and supply Qs = −30 + 3P, which gave P* = 30 and Q* = 60.
Now suppose demand rises (for example, incomes increase) so that only the intercept changes, to Qd = 208 − 4P, while supply is unchanged.
Re-solve for the new equilibrium:

208 - 4P = -30 + 3P ⟹ 238 = 7P ⟹ P* = 34
Q* = -30 + 3(34) = 72

The price rises from 30 to 34 and the quantity from 60 to 72, matching the shift graph above.

Elasticity in One Line: The price elasticity of demand is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price. When demand is inelastic, a price rise increases total revenue.
