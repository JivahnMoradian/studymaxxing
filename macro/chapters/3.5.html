UNIT 3 — NATIONAL INCOME & PRICE DETERMINATION

Fiscal Policy

Practice these problems →

What Fiscal Policy Is

Fiscal Policy is the use of government spending (G) and taxes (T) to influence real GDP, employment and the price level. It works by shifting aggregate demand.

Expansionary Fiscal Policy Contractionary Fiscal Policy
Actions Raise G, cut T (or raise transfers) Cut G, raise T (or cut transfers)
Effect on AD Shifts right Shifts left
Used for Recessionary gap (high unemployment) Inflationary gap (overheating)
Budget Deficit rises (or surplus falls) Deficit falls (or surplus rises)

Expansionary policy in a recessionary gap: AD shifts right from AD1 to AD2, restoring output to Yf, at a higher price level.

Contractionary policy in an inflationary gap: AD shifts left, reducing the price level and restoring Yf.

Drawing It Yourself: draw the short-run equilibrium (E1) to the left of LRAS for a recession (or to the right for an inflationary gap), then draw the shifted AD2 so that it crosses SRAS exactly on LRAS. Label E2, Yf, and the new price level.

Discretionary Policy vs. Automatic Stabilizers

  • Discretionary Fiscal Policy: deliberate changes in G (government purchases), T (taxes), or TR (government transfer payments) that require new legislation (for example, a stimulus package, an infrastructure bill, a tax-cut law).
  • Automatic Stabilizers: spending and tax rules already in place that change automatically with the business cycle, with no new law.
Automatic Stabilizer In a Recession In an Expansion
Progressive income tax Tax revenue falls faster than income Revenue rises faster than income
Unemployment insurance Transfers rise Transfers fall
Means-tested transfers (food assistance, Medicaid) Rise Fall
Corporate and payroll taxes Revenue falls Revenue rises

Automatic stabilizers push the budget toward deficit in recessions and toward surplus in booms, cushioning swings in disposable income and AD without any political delay.

Budget Deficits, Surpluses and the Debt

Budget Balance = T - G - TR
(deficit if negative, surplus if positive)
G = 2,400,  TR = 800,  T = 2,900 ⟹  Balance = 2,900 - 2,400 - 800 = -300 ⟹  deficit of 300
  • A Deficit (G + TR > T) adds to the national debt; a surplus reduces it.
  • The Debt is the accumulated stock of past deficits (a stock); the deficit is the annual flow.
  • Cyclical Deficit: the part of the deficit caused by the business cycle (lower tax revenue, higher transfers in a recession). Structural Deficit: the deficit that would remain even at full employment.
Structural Deficit = deficit if GDP were at Yf
Cyclical Deficit = Actual Deficit - Structural Deficit
T = 0.25Y,  G + TR = 2,700,  Yf = 10,000,  Y = 9,200:
Actual Deficit = 2,700 - 2,300 = 400
Structural = 2,700 - 2,500 = 200

Sizing the Policy

Use the multipliers from 3.4 to find the change in G or T that shifts AD enough to close the gap:

Δ G = GapSpending Multiplier
Δ T = GapTax Multiplier
MPC = 0.7,  Yf - Y = 210:
Δ G = 2103.33 = +63 or
Δ T = 210-2.33 = -90

Because a tax cut is partly saved, closing a gap with taxes takes a larger change (90) than with government purchases (63).

Costs and Limits of Fiscal Policy

  • Policy Lags: stimulus takes time and can arrive too late (see Policy Lags below).
  • Crowding Out: Deficit-financed spending means the government borrows more, raising the demand for loanable funds, so the real interest rate rises and private investment falls. This offsets part of the stimulus.
  • Other Limits: temporary tax cuts may be saved rather than spent; spending targeted poorly may not raise output; higher debt means future taxes.

Policy Lags

Stabilization policy takes time to work, and may arrive after the problem is gone.

  • Recognition Lag: time to realize the economy has changed (data are delayed and noisy).
  • Decision Lag: time to decide on an action (long for fiscal policy because Congress must act).
  • Implementation Lag: time to put the decision into effect.
  • Impact (Effect) Lag: time for the policy to affect spending and output.

Fiscal policy has long inside lags (recognition, decision, implementation). Monetary policy has a short inside lag (the FOMC meets frequently) but a long impact lag. If lags are long and the economy would self-correct, policy can arrive at the wrong time and make fluctuations worse (procyclical). Automatic Stabilizers have no lags.

Crowding out: higher government borrowing shifts the demand for loanable funds right (D1 to D2), raising the real interest rate from r1 to r2 and reducing the funds available for private investment.

Δ Y = Multiplier × (Δ G + Δ I)
where Δ I < 0 is the crowded-out investment
Δ G = +50,  Multiplier = 4
Crowded-Out Investment = -18 ⟹  Δ Y = 4 × (50 - 18) = 128  (instead of 200)
Go Deeper: College-Level

Cyclically Adjusted Budget: Economists measure the stance of fiscal policy using the cyclically adjusted (structural) balance, computed at potential output. A rising actual deficit during a recession may simply reflect automatic stabilizers, not a change in policy. Policy is "expansionary" only if the structural balance worsens.

Evidence on Multipliers: Estimates for government purchases are typically in the range of about 0.5 to 2, depending on how much slack the economy has, whether the central bank offsets the stimulus, and whether the stimulus is financed with debt. They tend to be larger in deep recessions and when interest rates are near zero (no crowding out).
