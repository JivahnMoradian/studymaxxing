Unemployment

Who Counts? The BLS Categories

Each month the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) surveys households and places every adult (age 16 or older, civilian, not in an institution such as a prison or nursing home) into one of three groups:

  • Employed: worked for pay at least one hour in the survey week (full-time or part-time), or has a job but is temporarily absent.
  • Unemployed: not working, available for work, and actively looked for work in the past four weeks (or is waiting to be recalled from a layoff).
  • Not in the Labor Force: everyone else (full-time students, retirees, stay-at-home parents, and discouraged workers who have given up looking).

The Labor Force is the sum of all employed and unemployed people. Children under 16 and institutionalized adults are not part of the adult population.

Labor Force = Employed + Unemployed
Unemployment Rate = UnemployedLabor Force × 100
Labor Force Participation Rate = Labor ForceAdult Population × 100
Employed = 150, Unemployed = 10, Adult Population = 240 ⟹ Labor Force = 160
Unemployment Rate = 10160 = 6.25%
Labor Force Participation Rate = 160240 = 66.7%

A related measure is the employment-population ratio = employed ÷ adult population = 150 ÷ 240 = 62.5%.

What Happens when People Change Status

The unemployment rate and the participation rate respond differently when people move between groups.

Event Employed Unemployed Labor Force Unemployment Rate Participation Rate
An unemployed person finds a job Up Down Same Down Same
An employed person loses a job Down Up Same Up Same
An unemployed person gives up looking (discouraged) Same Down Down Down Down
A non-participant starts looking (not yet hired) Same Up Up Up Up
A non-participant takes a job at once Up Same Up Down Up
An employed person retires Down Same Down Up Down

The key lesson: the unemployment rate can fall for a bad reason (e.g. people drop out of the labor force).

Three Types of Unemployment

  • Frictional: short-term, caused by the normal time it takes workers and jobs to match (new graduates, people between jobs, people who quit to search). Always present.
  • Structural: a mismatch between workers' skills or location and the jobs available, caused by technological change, shifts in industries, or wages held above equilibrium (minimum wage, unions). Typically lasts longer.
  • Cyclical: caused by downturns in the business cycle, when weak demand means too few jobs. It is the only type that is zero at full employment.

Seasonal unemployment (for example, ski instructors in summer) is a regular pattern that the BLS removes with "seasonal adjustment."

The Natural Rate

The Natural Rate of Unemployment is the rate that exists when the economy is at full employment (producing at potential output). It includes the frictional and structural types but no cyclical unemployment.

Natural Rate = Frictional + Structural
Cyclical Unemployment = Actual Rate - Natural Rate
  • Actual > natural: cyclical unemployment is positive; real GDP is below potential (recessionary gap).
  • Actual < natural: cyclical unemployment is negative; real GDP is above potential (inflationary gap).
  • Full employment does not mean 0% unemployment. It means zero cyclical unemployment.

The natural rate drifts over time: better job-matching technology lowers frictional unemployment, while a mismatch of skills raises structural unemployment.

Limits of the Unemployment Rate

The measured rate is a rough guide to joblessness, and is often criticized for understating it:

  • Discouraged Workers are out of the labor force, so they are not counted as unemployed.
  • Underemployed workers (part-time but wanting full-time, or overqualified for their jobs) count as fully employed.
  • It says nothing about the quality or length of jobs or wage levels.

It can also overstate unemployment, because some people counted as unemployed work in the underground economy or are not seriously searching.

Go Deeper: College-Level

Okun's Law: Unemployment and output move in opposite directions. Okun's rule of thumb is that when the unemployment rate is 1 percentage point above the natural rate, real GDP is about 2% below its potential:

Y - YfYf × 100 = -2(u - un)
u = 7%, un = 5% ⟹ Output ≈ -2(7 - 5) = -4%, i.e. 4% below potential

Why a Natural Rate Above Zero? Search theory (workers and firms take time to find a good match), efficiency wages (firms pay above the market-clearing wage to raise productivity, which creates a surplus of labor), unions and minimum wages all raise structural unemployment.

Hysteresis: A long recession can raise the natural rate itself: long-term unemployed workers lose skills and contacts, so the actual rate feeds back into the natural rate.

Broader BLS Measures: The headline rate is "U-3." The broader "U-6" also counts discouraged and other marginally attached workers and part-timers who want full-time work, so it is always higher.
