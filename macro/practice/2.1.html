2.1

Circular Flow & GDP

Read the theory → Drill 2.1 →

A. Computing GDP (Expenditure Approach)

Problem 1. An economy has consumption of 540, investment of 130, government purchases of 210, exports of 80 and imports of 95 (billions of dollars). Find GDP.

Show solution
Y = C + I + G + (X - M) = 540 + 130 + 210 + (80 - 95) = 865

Net exports are −15 (a trade deficit), so GDP is 865.

Problem 2. In a recent year, C = 3,350, I = 910, G = 1,270 and NX = −240 (billions). Find GDP.

Show solution
Y = 3,350 + 910 + 1,270 - 240 = 5,290

GDP is 5,290 billion. The negative NX is subtracted (imports exceed exports).

Problem 3. GDP is 1,420. Consumption is 910, government purchases are 285 and investment is 170. What are net exports, and does the country run a surplus or a deficit?

Show solution
NX = Y - C - I - G = 1,420 - 910 - 170 - 285 = 55

NX = +55, so the country has a trade surplus (exports exceed imports).

Problem 4. Use the data (billions of dollars) to compute GDP.

Item Value
Household spending on services 600
Household spending on durable goods 140
Household spending on nondurable goods 210
Business purchases of equipment 120
New housing construction 70
Increase in business inventories 15
Federal government purchases 160
State and local government purchases 240
Social Security payments 230
Exports 150
Imports 190
Households' purchases of stock 80
Show solution
  • C = 600 + 140 + 210 = 950
  • I = 120 + 70 + 15 = 205 (new housing and inventories are investment)
  • G = 160 + 240 = 400
  • NX = 150 − 190 = −40
  • Excluded: Social Security (transfer) and stock purchases (financial transaction).
Y = 950 + 205 + 400 - 40 = 1,515

GDP = 1,515 billion.

Problem 5. GDP is 2,080, consumption is 1,310, government purchases are 455, exports are 240 and imports are 275. Find investment.

Show solution

NX = 240 − 275 = −35.

I = Y - C - G - NX = 2,080 - 1,310 - 455 - (-35) = 350

Investment = 350.

Problem 6. A carmaker produces 400 cars this year, valued at $30,000 each, and sells only 340 of them to households. How much does this firm add to GDP, and in which components?

Show solution

GDP counts what is produced: 400 × $30,000 = $12,000,000.

  • Sold cars are consumption: 340 × $30,000 = $10,200,000 (C).
  • The 60 unsold cars are added to inventories, which is investment: 60 × $30,000 = $1,800,000 (I).

10.2 million + 1.8 million = 12.0 million.
Unsold output is not lost from GDP; it is counted as inventory investment.

Problem 7. Last year an economy had C = 600, I = 150, G = 180 and NX = −30. This year C = 630, I = 135, G = 189 and NX = −24. What was the percentage growth of (nominal) GDP?

Show solution
  • Last year: 600 + 150 + 180 − 30 = 900.
  • This year: 630 + 135 + 189 − 24 = 930.
Growth = 930 - 900900 × 100 = 3.33%

Even though investment fell, higher C, G and NX lifted GDP by about 3.3%.

B. Does It Count Toward GDP?

Problem 8. Maria buys a used 2015 sedan from a dealer for $9,000. The dealer's profit and sales commission on the car was $1,100. How much of this transaction is counted in this year's GDP?

Show solution

Only the $1,100 counts, as consumption of the dealer's service. The sedan itself was counted in GDP in 2015 when it was produced. Counting it again would double count.

Problem 9. A household buys 100 shares of stock for $4,000. Is this part of GDP?

Show solution

No. It is a financial transaction that transfers ownership of an existing asset. No new good or service is produced. (Only the broker's fee would count as a service.)

Problem 10. The government pays $800 per month in unemployment benefits to a jobless worker. Does this count in G?

Show solution

No. It is a transfer payment: no good or service is produced in exchange. G includes only government purchases. If the worker then spends the benefit on groceries, that spending counts in C.

Problem 11. A bakery buys $2,000 of flour and then sells bread made from it for $5,000. How much of this is counted in GDP?

Show solution

Flour is an intermediate good, so only the final sale of $5,000 of bread counts. The $2,000 is already embedded in that price. Counting both would give $7,000 and double count.

Problem 12. A family pays $350,000 for a newly built house. Does it count, and in which component?

Show solution

Yes, as investment (I), residential construction. New houses are treated as capital goods, not consumer goods. (Resale of an existing house would not count, only the agent's commission.)

Problem 13. Compare (a) a parent who stays home and cares for her children and (b) a parent who pays a licensed day-care center $9,000 a year. What does each do to GDP?

Show solution

(a) Home care is a non-market activity, so it is not counted. (b) The fee is payment for a service and is counted in C: +$9,000. The real activity is similar in both cases, which is a limitation of GDP.

Problem 14. An American buys a car built in Japan for $28,000. What happens to US GDP?

Show solution

C rises by $28,000, but M rises by the same amount, so NX falls by $28,000. Net effect on US GDP is zero: the car was not produced in the US. (It does raise Japan's exports and GDP.)

Problem 15. A Japanese company builds a car factory in Ohio worth $600 million and the plant produces cars. Do the plant's output and investment count in US GDP?

Show solution

Yes. GDP counts production within the borders, whoever owns it. The factory is US investment (I) and its output is US GDP. The profits sent back to Japan are a payment to foreign owners, which is why US GNP is smaller than US GDP by that amount.

C. Which Component of Spending?

Problem 16. Classify as C, I, G or NX (and the direction for NX): (a) a household buys a new refrigerator; (b) a firm buys a new delivery truck; (c) a state pays its teachers' salaries; (d) a Brazilian airline buys a US-made jet.

Show solution

(a) C. (b) I (business capital). (c) G (purchase of teaching services). (d) NX rises through exports (X).

Problem 17. A US bookstore adds 5,000 unsold, US-printed books to its stockroom. Which component rises?

Show solution

Investment (I) through inventory accumulation. Output has been produced but not sold to an end user.

Problem 18. A family buys a newly built home; a student buys a new laptop; a firm builds a new warehouse. How many of these are investment?

Show solution

Two. The new home (residential investment) and the warehouse (business investment). The laptop bought by a student is consumption.

Problem 19. The government buys a new fleet of buses for $15 million and also sends $15 million in Social Security checks. What is the total effect on G?

Show solution

G rises by $15 million only: the bus purchase. The Social Security checks are transfers, not purchases.

Problem 20. Which is not part of investment in the national accounts: (a) a firm buying software; (b) a household buying corporate bonds; (c) construction of a new apartment building; (d) a rise in a retailer's inventories?

Show solution

(b). Buying bonds is a financial transaction, which is "investment" in everyday language but not in GDP accounting. (a), (c) and (d) are all investment.

D. Value Added

Problem 21. A rancher sells cattle to a packer for $360. The packer sells beef to a grocer for $540. The grocer sells the beef to consumers for $700. Find the value added at each stage and the contribution to GDP.

Show solution
Stage Sales Intermediate input Value added
Rancher 360 0 360
Packer 540 360 180
Grocer 700 540 160

Total value added = 360 + 180 + 160 = 700, equal to the final price.
GDP rises by $700.

Problem 22. In Problem 21, what would be the (wrong) total if you added up every stage's sales, and by how much does it overstate GDP?

Show solution

Adding sales: 360 + 540 + 700 = 1,600.
That overstates GDP by 1,600 − 700 = 900, because the rancher's cattle and packer's beef are counted again inside later prices (double counting).

Problem 23. Iron ore is sold for $55, steel for $140, auto parts for $310, and a finished car for $690. Find the value added by the assembler and the total value added in the chain.

Show solution
  • Assembler: 690 − 310 = 380.
  • Other stages: ore 55, steel 140 − 55 = 85, parts 310 − 140 = 170.
  • Total: 55 + 85 + 170 + 380 = 690, the price of the final car.

Problem 24. A US firm imports $250 of parts and assembles them into products that it sells to US households for $600. What is its value added, and what does the sale do to US GDP?

Show solution

Value added = 600 − 250 = $350.
In the expenditure approach, C rises by $600 and M rises by $250 (NX falls by 250), so GDP rises by $350, the same thing.

Problem 25. A bakery sells 40,000 loaves at $12 each ($480,000 of revenue). It buys $130,000 of flour and $40,000 of electricity. It pays $190,000 in wages, $35,000 in rent and $15,000 in interest. Find (a) its value added and (b) its profit.

Show solution

(a) Value added = 480,000 − (130,000 + 40,000) = $310,000.

(b) Value added is split among the factors of production, so profit = 310,000 − 190,000 − 35,000 − 15,000 = $70,000.

This shows why the value-added and income approaches agree: the value a firm adds becomes wages, rent, interest and profit.

E. Income Approach, GDP and GNP

Problem 26. An economy has wages of 3,860, rent of 240, interest of 310 and profit of 890. Depreciation is 640 and indirect business taxes are 410. Find GDP.

Show solution
Factor income = 3,860 + 240 + 310 + 890 = 5,300
GDP = 5,300 + 640 + 410 = 6,350

Problem 27. Spending on GDP totals 8,125. Wages are 4,410, rent 365, interest 520, depreciation 880 and indirect taxes 570. What must profit be?

Show solution

Expenditure and income measures must be equal, so profit is the residual:

Profit = 8,125 - 4,410 - 365 - 520 - 880 - 570 = 1,380

Problem 28. A country has GDP of 4,250. Its residents earn 190 abroad and foreigners earn 145 within the country. Find GNP.

Show solution
GNP = 4,250 + 190 - 145 = 4,295

GNP exceeds GDP by the net foreign income of +45.

Problem 29. Country Z has GDP of 610. Its residents earn 20 abroad, while foreign firms and workers earn 55 inside Z. Find GNP and say which is larger and why.

Show solution
GNP = 610 + 20 - 55 = 575

GDP is larger. Foreigners earn more in Z than Z's residents earn abroad, as in a country that hosts many foreign-owned factories.

Problem 30. A country's GNP is 2,840 and its net income from abroad is −62. Find GDP.

Show solution

GNP = GDP + net foreign income, so GDP = 2,840 − (−62) = 2,902.

Problem 31. For each, say whether it is in US GDP, in US GNP, both, or neither: (a) profit earned by a Toyota plant in Kentucky; (b) wages of a US engineer working in Saudi Arabia; (c) wages of a US teacher in Texas.

Show solution

(a) US GDP only: produced in the US by a foreign-owned firm. (b) US GNP only: earned by a US resident, but produced in Saudi Arabia. (c) Both: produced in the US by a US resident.

F. Circular Flow and Leakages

Problem 32. In the circular flow, classify each as a money flow or a real flow, and name the market: (a) a household pays for groceries; (b) a firm pays wages; (c) workers supply labor.

Show solution

(a) Money flow from households to firms in the product market (consumption). (The groceries themselves are the matching real flow, from firms to households, in the same market.) (b) Money flow from firms to households in the factor market. (c) Real flow from households to firms in the factor market.

Problem 33. Identify each as a leakage or injection: (a) a household deposits money in a savings account; (b) a firm sells bonds to finance a new plant; (c) the government collects income taxes; (d) a foreign buyer purchases US wheat.

Show solution

(a) Leakage: saving (S). (b) Injection: borrowing for investment (I). (c) Leakage: taxes (T). (d) Injection: exports (X).

Problem 34. In a simple economy with no government or foreign sector, household income is 1,260 and households spend 1,070 on consumption. What is saving? If the circular flow is in balance, how much must firms invest?

Show solution

Saving S = 1,260 − 1,070 = 190.
For the flow to be stable, the leakage must be offset by an equal injection, so I = 190: firms borrow the saved funds through financial markets and spend them on capital.

Problem 35. Saving is 140, taxes are 210 and imports are 95. Investment is 100 and government purchases are 230. What level of exports keeps the circular flow in balance?

Show solution

Leakages = S + T + M = 140 + 210 + 95 = 445.
Injections must equal this: I + G + X = 100 + 230 + X = 445, so X = 115.

Problem 36. True or false: "Total household income always equals total spending on final goods." Explain using the circular flow.

Show solution

True in the circular flow, as an accounting identity: every dollar firms receive from the sale of output is paid out as income to someone (wages, rent, interest or profit, with profit being the residual).
So output = income = expenditure.

G. GDP per Capita and Limitations

Problem 37. GDP is $2.4 trillion and the population is 80 million. Find GDP per capita.

Show solution
2.4 × 101280 × 106 = $30,000 per person

Problem 38. A country's GDP grows by 5% in a year while its population grows by 3%. Approximately what is the growth rate of GDP per capita?

Show solution

Approximately 5% − 3% = 2%.
(Exactly: 1.05 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = 1.94%.) Growth only raises average living standards if output grows faster than the population.

Problem 39. A hurricane destroys many homes. Over the next year, spending on rebuilding is very high. What happens to GDP, and what does this say about GDP as a measure of well-being?

Show solution

GDP rises because rebuilding is new production (I and C). But people are not better off than before the storm: they are only restoring what was lost. GDP does not subtract the destroyed wealth. This is a limitation of GDP as a welfare measure.

Problem 40. Country A has higher GDP per capita than Country B. Give two reasons it might nonetheless have lower well-being.

Show solution

Any two of: income may be unequally distributed in A; A's citizens may work longer hours with less leisure; non-market work (home production) and the underground economy may be larger in B; A may suffer greater pollution and environmental damage that GDP ignores; differences in health, safety or life expectancy.
