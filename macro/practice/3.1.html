3.1

Aggregate Demand

Read the theory → Drill 3.1 →

A. Classifying Events: Does AD Shift, Which Way, and Through Which Component?

Problem 1. A sustained stock-market boom raises household wealth. What happens to AD, and through which component?

Show solution

Higher wealth increases consumption, so C rises and AD shifts right.

Problem 2. The real interest rate rises. What happens to AD, and through which component?

Show solution

Borrowing is more expensive, so firms undertake fewer projects: I falls and AD shifts left.

Problem 3. Congress cuts spending on defense and highway construction. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Both are government purchases, so G falls and AD shifts left.

Problem 4. Several of the country's major trading partners fall into recession. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Foreign income falls, so foreigners buy fewer of our goods: exports fall, NX falls, and AD shifts left.

Problem 5. The government cuts the income tax rate. What happens to AD, and through which component?

Show solution

Disposable income rises, so C rises and AD shifts right.

Problem 6. Firms become optimistic about future sales and expected profits. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Expected profitability of new capital rises, so I rises and AD shifts right.

Problem 7. The domestic currency appreciates against foreign currencies. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Domestic goods become more expensive abroad and foreign goods cheaper at home, so exports fall, imports rise, NX falls and AD shifts left.

Problem 8. Consumer confidence falls sharply after a wave of layoffs in the news. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Households expect lower future income and cut back: C falls and AD shifts left.

Problem 9. The government increases unemployment benefits (a transfer payment). What happens to AD, and is this a change in G?

Show solution

Transfers are not G (no good or service is purchased). They raise household disposable income, so C rises and AD shifts right, by less than a purchase of the same size would.

Problem 10. A new investment tax credit lowers the after-tax cost of buying equipment. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Investment becomes more profitable: I rises and AD shifts right.

Problem 11. The central bank sells government bonds, which pushes up the real interest rate. Separately, the government raises purchases. For each action alone, say how AD shifts. What happens overall?

Show solution

Higher real interest rate: I falls, AD left. Higher purchases: G rises, AD right. The net shift is ambiguous without knowing which effect is larger.

B. Movement Along AD vs. a Shift of AD

Problem 12. The price level falls from 120 to 110 with nothing else changing. Does AD shift?

Show solution

No. A change in the price level is a movement along AD (to a larger quantity of real GDP demanded).

Problem 13. Real GDP demanded rises because households and firms become more optimistic. Is this a movement along AD or a shift?

Show solution

A shift to the right: the cause (optimism) is not a change in the price level, and planned spending rises at every price level.

Problem 14. The price level rises 4%. Describe the movement along AD and name the three effects that explain it.

Show solution

Move up and to the left along AD, so the quantity of real GDP demanded falls. Wealth effect (real balances fall, C falls), interest-rate effect (money demand rises, interest rates rise, I falls), and exchange-rate effect (domestic goods more expensive, NX falls).

Problem 15. True or false: "A higher price level shifts AD to the left because everything costs more."

Show solution

False. A higher price level is a movement up along AD, not a shift. Only non-price-level determinants shift AD.

Problem 16. The Fed lowers interest rates, which raises investment and consumer durable purchases. Movement along AD or a shift?

Show solution

A shift to the right. The Fed's action changes spending at every price level; it is not a response to a change in the price level.

Problem 17. The exchange rate does two different things in the AD story. Distinguish the "exchange-rate effect" that explains AD's slope from an exchange-rate change that shifts AD.

Show solution

Slope: a change in the domestic price level makes domestic goods relatively cheaper or more expensive, so NX moves along AD. Shift: an independent change in the exchange rate (for example, the currency depreciates because of foreign investor sentiment) changes NX at every price level, shifting AD.

C. The Three Effects Behind the Slope

Problem 18. The price level falls, so the dollars in people's savings accounts buy more. Households respond by spending more. Which effect is this?

Show solution

The wealth (real-balances) effect.

Problem 19. The price level falls, so people need to hold less money for transactions, and interest rates fall. Firms invest more. Which effect, and what happens to the quantity of real GDP demanded?

Show solution

The interest-rate effect. Investment rises, so the quantity of real GDP demanded rises (movement down along AD).

Problem 20. The US price level falls relative to prices abroad. Foreigners buy more US goods and Americans buy fewer imports. Which effect is this?

Show solution

The exchange-rate (net-export) effect: NX rises, so the quantity of real GDP demanded rises.

Problem 21. Explain the chain of events behind the AD slope when the price level rises (interest-rate effect).

Show solution

Price level up → people need more money to buy the same goods → money demand rises
→ interest rates rise
→ investment (and interest-sensitive consumption) falls
→ quantity of real GDP demanded falls.

Problem 22. A student says AD slopes downward "because of the income and substitution effects." Is this correct?

Show solution

No. Those explain a single market's demand curve. The whole economy's AD slopes down because of the wealth, interest-rate, and exchange-rate effects.

Problem 23. Which of the three effects would you expect to be weakest in a nearly closed economy with few imports and exports?

Show solution

The exchange-rate (net-export) effect, because NX is a very small share of spending.

D. Component Arithmetic

Problem 24. An economy has C = 1,240, I = 310, G = 420, exports = 190, and imports = 235 (all in billions). Find NX and total planned spending.

Show solution

NX = 190 − 235 = −45.
AD = 1,240 + 310 + 420 − 45 = 1,925 billion.

Problem 25. At the current price level total planned spending is 2,310. C = 1,480, I = 360, and NX = −70. Find G.

Show solution

2,310 = 1,480 + 360 + G − 70, so G = 2,310 − 1,480 − 360 + 70 = 540.

Problem 26. Initially C = 5,200, I = 1,100, G = 1,450, X = 820, M = 1,010. Then I falls by 120 and X rises by 60 (nothing else changes). Find AD before and after at the same price level, and the direction of the shift.

Show solution

Before: 5,200 + 1,100 + 1,450 + (820 − 1,010) = 7,560.
After: 5,200 + 980 + 1,450 + (880 − 1,010) = 7,500.
Planned spending falls by 60, so AD shifts left (before any multiplier effect).

Problem 27. Exports are 640 and imports are 710. Then the currency depreciates, so exports rise to 700 and imports fall to 650. Find NX before and after, and say what happens to AD.

Show solution

Before: NX = 640 − 710 = −70.
After: NX = 700 − 650 = +50, a change of +120.
NX rises, so AD shifts right.

Problem 28. The spending multiplier is 2.5 and government purchases rise by 14. By how much does AD shift horizontally?

Show solution

Shift = 2.5 × 14 = 35 to the right.

Problem 29. The multiplier is 5 and AD needs to move left by 60 to offset an inflationary gap. What decrease in G would do it?

Show solution

ΔG = 60 ÷ 5 = −12, a decrease in government purchases of 12.

Problem 30. Government purchases rise by 25 while exports fall by 25. What is the initial change in total planned spending, and what does it imply for AD?

Show solution

+25 (G) − 25 (X) = 0.
The two changes cancel, so AD does not shift (to a first approximation).

E. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 31. The economy is in long-run equilibrium. Firms revise their forecasts down and cut planned investment. (a) Which curve shifts, and in which direction? (b) What happens to the short-run price level and real GDP? (c) Which component of AD changed?

Show solution

(a) AD shifts left because planned investment falls at every price level. (b) The economy moves along SRAS to a lower price level and lower real GDP (below potential output). (c) Investment (I).

Problem 32. A foreign recession cuts US exports. At the same time, the Fed lowers the interest rate, raising US investment. Assume the effects are the same size. What happens to AD?

Show solution

Lower exports reduce NX (AD left). Higher investment raises I (AD right). With equal size, AD does not shift. If either were larger, AD would move in the direction of the larger one.

Problem 33. Households in an economy pay off debt faster, and the stock market falls. (a) Which component of AD is affected? (b) How does AD shift? (c) Draw the shift on a graph with SRAS and LRAS, starting from long-run equilibrium.

Show solution

(a) Consumption: less debt capacity and lower wealth. (b) AD shifts left. (c) The economy moves to a lower price level and real GDP below potential.

Problem 34. A country's currency depreciates sharply. Trace the effect through the components of AD and graph the AD shift.

Show solution

Exports become cheaper for foreigners (X rises), imports become more expensive for domestic buyers (M falls), so NX rises and AD shifts right. In the short run the price level and real GDP both rise.

Problem 35. Explain why a tax cut for households shifts AD by less than a government spending increase of the same dollar size.

Show solution

All of the spending increase is first-round demand. A tax cut raises disposable income, and households save part of it, so the first-round spending increase is only MPC times the tax cut (details in 3.4 and 3.5). Hence a smaller AD shift.
