A. Finding Equilibrium

Problem 1. Demand is Qd = 140 − 5P and supply is Qs = −20 + 3P. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.

Show solution

Set Qd = Qs: 140 − 5P = −20 + 3P, so 160 = 8P and P* = 20.
Then Q* = 140 − 5(20) = 40.

Problem 2. Demand is Qd = 96 − 2P and supply is Qs = 4P. Find P* and Q*.

Show solution

96 − 2P = 4P, so 96 = 6P and P* = 16, Q* = 4(16) = 64.

Problem 3. At a price of 12 in Problem 1's market, is there a shortage or surplus, and how large?

Show solution

Qd = 140 − 5(12) = 80 and Qs = −20 + 3(12) = 16.
Qd >
Qs, so there is a shortage of 64.
Price will rise toward 20.

B. Predicting Shifts

Problem 4. The price of coffee rises. What happens to the market for tea (a substitute)?

Show solution

Demand for tea shifts right, so the equilibrium price and quantity of tea both rise.

Problem 5. A new technology lowers the cost of making solar panels. What happens to their price and quantity?

Show solution

Supply shifts right. Equilibrium price falls and quantity rises.

Problem 6. Income rises and the good is inferior (for example, instant noodles). What happens to equilibrium price and quantity?

Show solution

Demand for an inferior good falls when income rises, so the curve shifts left: price and quantity both fall.

Problem 7. Demand rises and supply rises at the same time. What happens to P* and Q*?

Show solution

Both increases push quantity up, so Q* rises for sure. Demand pushes price up and supply pushes it down, so the effect on P* is ambiguous (it depends on which shift is larger).

C. Price Controls

(Problems 8–10: Qd = 180 − 4P, Qs = −30 + 3P. The equilibrium is P* = 30, Q* = 60.)

Problem 8. A ceiling of 24 is imposed. Find the shortage and the quantity traded.

Show solution

At P = 24: Qd = 180 − 96 = 84 and Qs = −30 + 72 = 42.
Shortage = 84 − 42 = 42.
Quantity traded is the smaller amount, 42.

Problem 9. A floor of 38 is imposed. Find the surplus and the quantity traded.

Show solution

At P = 38: Qd = 180 − 152 = 28 and Qs = −30 + 114 = 84.
Surplus = 84 − 28 = 56.
Quantity traded is the smaller amount, 28.

Problem 10. A ceiling of 35 is imposed. What happens?

Show solution

35 is above the equilibrium price of 30, so the ceiling is non-binding: the market stays at P* = 30 and Q* = 60.

Supply shifts left. Equilibrium price rises (from 14 to 17) and quantity falls (from 94 to 82).

More Practice — C. Price Ceilings and Floors

Problem 21. In the market Qd = 210 − 3P, Qs = −30 + 5P (equilibrium P* = 30), a ceiling of 25 is imposed. Find the shortage and the quantity traded.

Show solution

At P = 25: Qd = 135 and Qs = 95.
Shortage of 40; quantity traded = 95.

Problem 22. Same market, but a floor of 36 is imposed. Find the surplus and the quantity traded.

Show solution

Surplus of 48; quantity traded = 102.

Shortage of 30; quantity traded = 97.

Problem 24. Same market, but a floor of 34 is imposed. Find the surplus and the quantity traded.

Show solution

Surplus of 20; quantity traded = 107.

Problem 25. In the market Qd = 332 − 8P, Qs = −60 + 6P, a ceiling of 30 is imposed. What happens?

Show solution

The equilibrium price is 28 (Q* = 108).
A ceiling of 30 is above equilibrium, so it is non-binding: price stays at 28 and quantity at 108.
