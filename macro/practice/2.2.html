2.2

Real vs. Nominal GDP & the Deflator

Read the theory → Drill 2.2 →

A. Nominal and Real GDP from a Table

(Problems 1–3: an economy produces only books and coffee. Year 1 is the base year.)

Year Price of books Books Price of coffee Cups of coffee
1 $20 50 $4 200
2 $24 55 $5 220

Problem 1. Compute nominal GDP in each year.

Show solution
Year 1: 20(50) + 4(200) = 1,000 + 800 = 1,800
Year 2: 24(55) + 5(220) = 1,320 + 1,100 = 2,420

Problem 2. Compute real GDP in year 2 using year 1 as the base year.

Show solution

Use year-1 prices with year-2 quantities:

20(55) + 4(220) = 1,100 + 880 = 1,980

Real GDP in year 2 is 1,980 (in year-1 dollars).

Problem 3. Now use year 2 as the base year. Find real GDP in year 1, and say what real GDP growth is under this base year.

Show solution

Year-2 prices with year-1 quantities: 24(50) + 5(200) = 1,200 + 1,000 = 2,200.
Real GDP in year 2 is its own nominal GDP, 2,420.

Growth = (2,420 − 2,200) ÷ 2,200 = 10%, the same as with base year 1 in this particular example.
In general, the two base years can give different growth rates (see Problem 7).

(Problems 4–6: a small economy with pizza and laptops.)

Year Price of pizza Pizzas Price of laptop Laptops
2022 $8 100 $500 20
2023 $10 120 $550 25
2024 $11 130 $600 24

Problem 4. Find nominal GDP in each year.

Show solution
  • 2022: 8(100) + 500(20) = 800 + 10,000 = 10,800
  • 2023: 10(120) + 550(25) = 1,200 + 13,750 = 14,950
  • 2024: 11(130) + 600(24) = 1,430 + 14,400 = 15,830

Problem 5. Find real GDP in each year using 2022 as the base year.

Show solution

Use 2022 prices ($8 and $500):

  • 2022: 10,800 (base year, equals nominal)
  • 2023: 8(120) + 500(25) = 960 + 12,500 = 13,460
  • 2024: 8(130) + 500(24) = 1,040 + 12,000 = 13,040

Problem 6. Find real GDP in each year using 2024 as the base year.

Show solution

Use 2024 prices ($11 and $600):

  • 2022: 11(100) + 600(20) = 1,100 + 12,000 = 13,100
  • 2023: 11(120) + 600(25) = 1,320 + 15,000 = 16,320
  • 2024: 15,830 (base year, equals nominal)

The pattern is the same (real GDP rises, then falls in 2024), but the levels differ from Problem 5 because the base year changed.

Problem 7. An economy produces solar panels and hospital visits.

Year Price of panel Panels Price of visit Visits
1 $50 40 $30 60
2 $35 70 $39 66

Compute real GDP growth from year 1 to year 2 using (a) year 1 as the base year and (b) year 2 as the base year. Why do the answers differ?

Show solution

Nominal GDP: year 1 = 50(40) + 30(60) = 3,800; year 2 = 35(70) + 39(66) = 2,450 + 2,574 = 5,024.

(a) Base year 1: real GDP in year 2 = 50(70) + 30(66) = 3,500 + 1,980 = 5,480.
Growth = 5,480 ÷ 3,800 − 1 = 44.2%.

(b) Base year 2: real GDP in year 1 = 35(40) + 39(60) = 1,400 + 2,340 = 3,740.
Growth = 5,024 ÷ 3,740 − 1 = 34.3%.

They differ because panels (the good with the big quantity increase) were expensive in year 1 and cheap in year 2. Valuing that quantity gain at year-1 prices makes growth look larger.

Problem 8. In the pizza and laptop economy (Problems 4–5), nominal GDP rose from 2023 to 2024, yet real GDP (base 2022) fell. Explain how.

Show solution

Nominal GDP rose from 14,950 to 15,830 (+5.9%), but real GDP fell from 13,460 to 13,040 (−3.1%). Prices rose enough to outweigh the fall in output: laptop production fell from 25 to 24, and laptops are most of this economy's output. A rise in nominal GDP does not mean more is being produced.

Problem 9. In an economy with one good, the price doubles and the quantity produced stays the same. What happens to nominal GDP and real GDP?

Show solution

Nominal GDP doubles (price × quantity). Real GDP is unchanged, because it uses base-year prices with the same quantity. All of the increase in nominal GDP is inflation.

B. The GDP Deflator

Problem 10. In year 2 of the books and coffee economy (Problems 1–2), nominal GDP is 2,420 and real GDP (base year 1) is 1,980. Find the GDP deflator.

Show solution
Deflator = 2,4201,980 × 100 = 122.2

Prices are 22.2% higher than in year 1.

Problem 11. Find the GDP deflator for 2022, 2023 and 2024 in the pizza and laptop economy (base year 2022; use Problems 4 and 5).

Show solution
  • 2022: 10,800 ÷ 10,800 × 100 = 100.0
  • 2023: 14,950 ÷ 13,460 × 100 = 111.1
  • 2024: 15,830 ÷ 13,040 × 100 = 121.4

Problem 12. Nominal GDP is 18,900 and the GDP deflator is 126. Find real GDP.

Show solution
Real GDP = 18,900126 × 100 = 15,000

Problem 13. Real GDP is 6,400 and the GDP deflator is 112.5. Find nominal GDP.

Show solution

Nominal GDP = real GDP × deflator ÷ 100 = 6,400 × 1.125 = 7,200.

Problem 14. In the base year nominal GDP is 540. What are real GDP and the deflator?

Show solution

In the base year, real GDP equals nominal GDP, so real GDP = 540 and the deflator = 540 ÷ 540 × 100 = 100.

Problem 15. Using the data in Problem 7 with year 2 as the base year, the deflator for year 1 is nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100 = 3,800 ÷ 3,740 × 100. Is this above or below 100 and why? Compute it.

Show solution

3,800 ÷ 3,740 × 100 = 101.6.
It is above 100 because year 1 comes before the base year (year 2) and prices at that time, on average, were higher than year-2 prices for this mix of goods (panels fell a lot).
A deflator below 100 means prices were lower than in the base year.

Problem 16. A GDP deflator rises from 108 in one year to 117 in the next. By how much did the price level change?

Show solution

(117 − 108) ÷ 108 = 8.3%.
A rise of 9 index points is not 9%: the percentage depends on the starting value.

C. Inflation from the Deflator

Problem 17. The deflator is 100 in year 1 and 122.2 in year 2. What is the inflation rate?

Show solution
122.2 - 100100 × 100 = 22.2%

Problem 18. GDP deflators are 104 (2021), 109.2 (2022) and 112.5 (2023). Find the inflation rate in 2022 and in 2023. Is this inflation, deflation or disinflation?

Show solution
  • 2022: (109.2 − 104) ÷ 104 = 5.0%
  • 2023: (112.5 − 109.2) ÷ 109.2 = 3.0%

Prices are still rising, but more slowly: disinflation (a falling inflation rate), not deflation.

Problem 19. A deflator falls from 118 to 115.64. Find the inflation rate and name the phenomenon.

Show solution

(115.64 − 118) ÷ 118 = −2.0%.
A negative inflation rate is deflation: the overall price level is falling.

Problem 20. Using Problem 11, find the inflation rate in 2023 and in 2024 in the pizza and laptop economy.

Show solution
  • 2023: (111.07 − 100) ÷ 100 ≈ 11.1%
  • 2024: (121.40 − 111.07) ÷ 111.07 ≈ 9.3%

Inflation slowed but remained positive, so this is disinflation.

Problem 21. The deflator is 126.0 and inflation next year is 3.5%. What will the deflator be?

Show solution

126.0 × 1.035 = 130.4.

Problem 22. Classify: (a) inflation falls from 6% to 4%; (b) the price level falls 1.5%; (c) inflation rises from 2% to 5%.

Show solution

(a) Disinflation (prices rising more slowly). (b) Deflation. (c) Higher inflation (accelerating inflation).

D. Growth Rates

Problem 23. In the pizza and laptop economy, find the growth rate of real GDP (base 2022) from 2023 to 2024.

Show solution

(13,040 − 13,460) ÷ 13,460 = −3.1%.
Real output fell.

Problem 24. Real GDP rises from 14,200 to 14,910. Find the growth rate.

Show solution

(14,910 − 14,200) ÷ 14,200 = 710 ÷ 14,200 = 5.0%.

Problem 25. Nominal GDP rises from 18,000 to 19,800 while the GDP deflator rises from 100 to 104. Find (a) the nominal growth rate, (b) the inflation rate, and (c) real GDP growth, exactly and by approximation.

Show solution

(a) 1,800 ÷ 18,000 = 10%.
(b) 4%.

(c) Real GDP at the start = 18,000 (deflator 100); at the end = 19,800 ÷ 104 × 100 = 19,038.
Exact growth = 19,038 ÷ 18,000 − 1 = 5.8%.
Approximation: 10% − 4% = 6%.

Problem 26. Nominal GDP grows 7% in a year while inflation is 2.5%. Approximately how fast does real GDP grow?

Show solution

Real growth ≈ 7% − 2.5% = 4.5%.

Problem 27. The price level rises 6% and real output falls 2%. What happens to nominal GDP?

Show solution

Approximately +6% − 2% = +4%.
Exactly: 1.06 × 0.98 = 1.0388, so nominal GDP rises 3.9%.

Problem 28. Real GDP was 8,000 in 2020 and 9,000 in 2023. What is the total growth over the three years, and approximately what is the average annual rate?

Show solution

Total = 1,000 ÷ 8,000 = 12.5%.
The annual rate g solves (1 + g)3 = 1.125, so g ≈ 3.9% per year (a rough check: 12.5 ÷ 3 ≈ 4.2%, a bit high because of compounding).

Problem 29. Nominal GDP is 4.5 trillion dollars, the deflator is 120 and the population is 150 million. Find real GDP per capita.

Show solution

Real GDP = 4.5 trillion ÷ 120 × 100 = 3.75 trillion.
Per person: 3.75 × 1012 ÷ (150 × 106) = $25,000.

E. Deflating a Series

(Problems 30–31: an economy's nominal GDP and price index, with 2020 as the base year.)

Year Nominal GDP Price index (2020 = 100)
2020 9,600 100
2021 10,140 104
2022 10,989 111
2023 11,716 116

Problem 30. Compute real GDP for each year (in 2020 dollars).

Show solution

Real GDP = nominal ÷ index × 100:

  • 2020: 9,600
  • 2021: 10,140 ÷ 104 × 100 = 9,750
  • 2022: 10,989 ÷ 111 × 100 = 9,900
  • 2023: 11,716 ÷ 116 × 100 = 10,100

Problem 31. Using Problem 30, compare nominal GDP growth with real GDP growth between 2020 and 2023.

Show solution

Nominal: 11,716 ÷ 9,600 − 1 = 22.0%.
Real: 10,100 ÷ 9,600 − 1 = 5.2%.
Most of the nominal growth was inflation (the price index rose 16%).

Problem 32. A price index has 2018 = 100, 2020 = 108 and 2023 = 126. Rebase it so that 2020 = 100. What are the new values for 2018 and 2023?

Show solution

Divide every value by the new base value (108) and multiply by 100:

  • 2018: 100 ÷ 108 × 100 = 92.6
  • 2020: 100
  • 2023: 126 ÷ 108 × 100 = 116.7

Rebasing changes the index numbers but not the inflation rates: 2020 to 2023 is still +16.7%.

Problem 33. Between 2015 and 2023 nominal GDP grew 30% and the GDP deflator grew 22%. What was real GDP growth over the period?

Show solution

Real GDP grows by the ratio of the two growth factors: 1.30 ÷ 1.22 = 1.0656, so real GDP grew 6.6% (the quick approximation, 30 − 22 = 8%, overstates it for large changes).

F. Conceptual Checks

Problem 34. True or false: "If nominal GDP rises, the economy must have produced more."

Show solution

False. Nominal GDP can rise purely because prices rose. Only real GDP isolates changes in output.

Problem 35. Why does the GDP deflator equal 100 in the base year, and why does this make the choice of base year somewhat arbitrary?

Show solution

In the base year, current prices are base-year prices, so nominal GDP = real GDP and the ratio is 1 (× 100).
Moving the base year rescales the index and the real GDP levels, but the direction of changes in real GDP and the price level is unchanged, and growth rates change only slightly.

Problem 36. Which is better for comparing living standards across two decades, nominal GDP or real GDP per capita? Why?

Show solution

Real GDP per capita: it removes inflation (real) and adjusts for population growth (per capita), so it tracks goods and services available per person.

Problem 37. True or false: "A deflator of 95 means prices are 5% lower than in the previous year."

Show solution

False. A deflator of 95 means prices are 5% lower than in the base year (100), not necessarily than in the previous year. Inflation compares the deflator to the previous year's deflator.
