AD-AS Equilibrium & Self-Correction

Read the theory → Drill 3.3 →

A. Short-Run Shift Analysis (Start from Long-Run Equilibrium)

(For Problems 1–8, assume the economy starts in long-run equilibrium. State which curve shifts, and the short-run effect on price level, real GDP, and unemployment.)

Problem 1. Consumers become more confident about future income and increase spending.

Show solution

AD shifts right. Price level rises, real GDP rises above Yf, unemployment falls below the natural rate (inflationary gap).

Problem 2. The government cuts spending on infrastructure.

Show solution

AD shifts left. Price level falls, real GDP falls below Yf, unemployment rises (recessionary gap).

Problem 3. The price of oil falls sharply and the decline is expected to last.

Show solution

SRAS shifts right. Price level falls, real GDP rises, unemployment falls.

Problem 4. Union contracts raise nominal wages across the economy.

Show solution

SRAS shifts left. Price level rises, real GDP falls, unemployment rises (stagflation).

Problem 5. Foreign economies enter a recession and buy fewer exports.

Show solution

NX falls, so AD shifts left. Price level falls, real GDP falls, unemployment rises.

Problem 6. The central bank cuts interest rates, boosting investment spending.

Show solution

I rises, so AD shifts right. Price level rises, real GDP rises, unemployment falls.

Problem 7. A frost destroys much of the year's crop, raising food and input costs (the economy's capacity is unchanged).

Show solution

Adverse supply shock: SRAS shifts left. Price level rises, real GDP falls, unemployment rises.

Problem 8. The domestic currency depreciates sharply.

Show solution

Exports rise and imports fall, so NX rises: AD shifts right. Price level rises, real GDP rises, unemployment falls. (Imported inputs also get more expensive, which can shift SRAS left a little.)

When the two shifts push a variable in the same direction, that variable is determinate; when they push it in opposite directions, it depends on the sizes of the shifts.

Problem 10. A major hurricane destroys a large share of an economy's factories and roads. Show the effect on a graph and describe the long-run outcome for output.

Show solution

Capacity falls, so LRAS shifts left (and SRAS shifts left). Price level rises and potential output (and actual output) falls permanently: no self-correction can return output to the old Yf until the capital is rebuilt.

B. Identifying and Sizing Gaps

Problem 11. The unemployment rate is 8% and the natural rate is 5%. Is the economy in a recessionary or inflationary gap? Where is real GDP relative to potential?

Show solution

Unemployment is above the natural rate: recessionary gap. Real GDP is below potential (Y < Yf).

Problem 12. Real GDP is 5,630 and potential output is 5,500. Find the output gap in dollars and as a percent. Which type of gap is it?

Show solution

Gap = 5,630 − 5,500 = +130.
Percent = 130 ÷ 5,500 × 100 ≈ +2.4%.
Inflationary gap.

Problem 13. AD: Y = 5,800 − 8P. SRAS: Y = 2,300 + 20P. Potential output is 5,000. Find the short-run equilibrium and classify the gap.

Show solution

5,800 − 8P = 2,300 + 20P → 3,500 = 28P → P = 125, Y = 5,800 − 1,000 = 4,800.
Gap = 4,800 − 5,000 = −200 (−4.0%): recessionary gap.

Problem 14. AD: Y = 5,300 − 20P. SRAS: Y = 2,300 + 4P. Yf = 2,700. Find the equilibrium and classify the gap.

Show solution

5,300 − 20P = 2,300 + 4P → 3,000 = 24P → P = 125, Y = 5,300 − 2,500 = 2,800.
Gap = 2,800 − 2,700 = +100 (≈ +3.7%): inflationary gap.

Problem 15. AD: Y = 4,700 − 12P. SRAS: Y = 2,800 + 8P. Yf = 3,800. Find the equilibrium, the gap, and the percent gap.

Show solution

4,700 − 12P = 2,800 + 8P → 1,900 = 20P → P = 95, Y = 4,700 − 1,140 = 3,560.
Gap = −240 (−6.3%): recessionary.

Problem 16. AD: Y = 7,500 − 20P. SRAS: Y = 4,800 + 10P. Yf = 5,500. Find the equilibrium and the gap.

Show solution

7,500 − 20P = 4,800 + 10P → 2,700 = 30P → P = 90, Y = 7,500 − 1,800 = 5,700.
Gap = +200 (≈ +3.6%): inflationary.

C. Self-Correction

Problem 17. The economy is in a recessionary gap. Describe the self-correction process step by step.

Show solution

High unemployment puts downward pressure on nominal wages and input prices. Production costs fall, so SRAS shifts right. The price level falls and real GDP rises along AD until it reaches Yf. Unemployment returns to the natural rate.

Problem 18. The economy is in an inflationary gap. Describe the self-correction process, and say what happens to the price level.

Show solution

Low unemployment lets workers bargain for higher wages, so costs rise and SRAS shifts left. The price level rises further while real GDP falls back to Yf. Unemployment returns to the natural rate.

Problem 19. A recessionary gap is described by AD: Y = 3,000 − 6P and SRAS: Y = 1,000 + 10P, with Yf = 2,400. Find the short-run equilibrium, the long-run price level after self-correction, and the new SRAS intercept.

Show solution

Short run: 3,000 − 6P = 1,000 + 10P → P = 125, Y = 2,250 (gap −150).
Long run: AD at Yf: 2,400 = 3,000 − 6P → P = 100.
New SRAS: Y = c′ + 10P passes through (2,400; 100), so c′ = 2,400 − 1,000 = 1,400.
SRAS has shifted right by 1,400 − 1,000 = 400.
The price level falls from 125 to 100.

Problem 20. AD: Y = 4,700 − 12P, SRAS: Y = 2,800 + 8P, Yf = 3,800 (the economy starts at P = 95 and Y = 3,560). Find the long-run price level, new SRAS intercept, and how much SRAS shifts.

Show solution

Long-run price level: 3,800 = 4,700 − 12P → P = 75.
New SRAS: 3,800 = c′ + 8(75) → c′ = 3,200, a rightward shift of 3,200 − 2,800 = 400.
The price level falls from 95 to 75, and real GDP rises from 3,560 to 3,800.

Problem 21. AD: Y = 7,500 − 20P, SRAS: Y = 4,800 + 10P, Yf = 5,500 (starts at P = 90, Y = 5,700). Find the long-run price level and new SRAS intercept. Does SRAS shift left or right?

Show solution

Long run: 5,500 = 7,500 − 20P → P = 100.
New SRAS: 5,500 = c′ + 1,000 → c′ = 4,500, which is lower than 4,800: SRAS shifts left by 300.
The price level rises from 90 to 100; real GDP falls from 5,700 to 5,500.

Problem 22. True or false: "After an inflationary gap self-corrects, the economy's price level returns to where it started."

Show solution

False. The price level ends higher than where it started (SRAS shifted left along a fixed AD), while output returns to Yf.

Problem 23. An increase in AD (for example, more consumer optimism) occurs in an economy at full employment, and the economy is then left alone. Compare the short-run and long-run effects on real GDP and the price level.

Show solution

Short run: price level and real GDP both rise (inflationary gap). Long run: real GDP returns to Yf, and the price level rises even more. The only permanent effect of a demand increase is a higher price level.

Problem 24. Why can self-correction take a long time in a deep recession?

Show solution

Nominal wages are downward sticky: contracts, minimum wages, and worker resistance to pay cuts slow the fall in wages. Until wages and costs fall enough, SRAS barely shifts and unemployment stays high.

D. Policy vs. Self-Correction

Problem 25. The economy is in a recessionary gap. Which type of policy would be used, which curve shifts, and in which direction? What happens to the price level compared with self-correction?

Show solution

Expansionary policy (raise G, cut taxes, or lower interest rates). AD shifts right until it intersects LRAS. The price level rises (compared with self-correction, where it falls). Output returns to Yf faster.

Problem 26. The economy is in an inflationary gap. What policy is appropriate and what does it do to the price level and output?

Show solution

Contractionary policy (cut G, raise taxes, raise interest rates). AD shifts left: the price level falls from its high short-run level, and real GDP falls to Yf.

Problem 27. AD: Y = 5,800 − 8P and SRAS: Y = 2,300 + 20P, with Yf = 5,000 and an initial price level of 125. By how much must AD shift horizontally to close the gap? If the spending multiplier is 4, what change in government purchases is needed?

Show solution

Price level on SRAS at Yf: 5,000 = 2,300 + 20P → P = 135.
AD must pass through (5,000; 135): 5,000 = a′ − 8(135) → a′ = 6,080.
Shift = 6,080 − 5,800 = 280.
ΔG = 280 ÷ 4 = +70.

Problem 28. AD: Y = 3,000 − 6P and SRAS: Y = 1,000 + 10P, with Yf = 2,400. The multiplier is 5. Find the horizontal AD shift needed and the required ΔG. What is the price level after policy?

Show solution

SRAS at Yf: 2,400 = 1,000 + 10P → P = 140.
New AD: 2,400 = a′ − 6(140) → a′ = 3,240.
Shift = 3,240 − 3,000 = 240.
ΔG = 240 ÷ 5 = +48.
The price level rises from 125 to 140.

Problem 29. AD: Y = 7,500 − 20P and SRAS: Y = 4,800 + 10P, with Yf = 5,500 (current P = 90, Y = 5,700). The multiplier is 5. Find the AD shift and ΔG needed.

Show solution

SRAS at Yf: 5,500 = 4,800 + 10P → P = 70.
New AD: 5,500 = a′ − 20(70) → a′ = 6,900.
Shift = 6,900 − 7,500 = −600.
ΔG = −600 ÷ 5 = −120.
The price level falls from 90 to 70.

Problem 30. A recessionary gap exists. Policymakers disagree: one says "wait for wages to fall," the other says "use stimulus now." Give one argument for each.

Show solution

Wait: self-correction needs no government action, avoids policy lags and overshoot, and reduces the price level. Stimulus: wages are sticky so waiting may take years, costing output and jobs; stimulus restores Yf faster.

Problem 31. After a recession, policymakers enact fiscal stimulus but the economy had already begun self-correcting. What may happen?

Show solution

Stimulus pushes AD right while SRAS is also shifting right, so output overshoots Yf, opening an inflationary gap and raising the price level.

E. Tracking the Long Run

Problem 32. An economy at long-run equilibrium (P = 100, Y = 3,000, U = 5%) experiences a fall in AD. In the short run P = 96, Y = 2,880, U = 7%. Describe what happens next, and give the long-run values of P, Y, and U if AD stays at its lower level.

Show solution

Recessionary gap: wages fall and SRAS shifts right. In the long run Y returns to 3,000 and U to 5%, while the price level falls further, to a value below 96 (e.g., 92). Output and unemployment return to their initial values; only the price level is permanently different.

Problem 33. A permanent increase in AD occurs from long-run equilibrium. Fill in "higher / lower / unchanged" for the long-run effect on (a) real GDP; (b) price level; (c) unemployment; (d) nominal wages.

Show solution

(a) Unchanged (Yf). (b) Higher. (c) Unchanged (natural rate). (d) Higher (wages catch up to prices; real wages return to their original level).

Problem 34. Compare the long-run effect on the price level of (a) a one-time adverse supply shock (temporary), and (b) a permanent rise in AD, assuming the economy self-corrects each time.

Show solution

(a) After the cost shock fades, SRAS shifts back, so the price level returns to its original level. (b) A permanent rise in AD leaves the price level permanently higher.

Problem 35. In a long-run equilibrium, SRAS shifts left because of an oil shock. The central bank responds by raising AD so output returns to Yf. Compare the price level with the alternative of doing nothing and letting wages fall.

Show solution

Policy: AD shifts right to Yf and the price level ends higher than after the shock. Doing nothing: unemployment pushes wages down, SRAS shifts back right, and the price level returns to its original level. Stimulus is faster but permanently raises the price level.

F. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 36. An economy is in long-run equilibrium. Firms reduce investment because of pessimism. (a) Draw a correctly labeled AD-AS graph showing the short-run effect. (b) State the effect on price level, real GDP, and unemployment. (c) If the government does nothing, describe what happens to wages and the price level in the long run. (d) Draw the long-run adjustment.

Show solution

(a)–(b) AD shifts left: price level falls, real GDP falls below Yf, unemployment rises above the natural rate. (c) Wages fall, SRAS shifts right, and the price level falls further. (d) Output returns to Yf.

Problem 37. The economy is in long-run equilibrium. A major oil-exporting region cuts production, raising oil prices. (a) Show the short-run effect. (b) What is this called? (c) The central bank wants to restore full employment: show how, and say what happens to the price level.

Show solution

(a) SRAS shifts left: price level up, output down. (b) Stagflation. (c) Expansionary policy shifts AD right to Yf; the price level rises even further than after the shock.

In the graph, the shock moves the economy from E1 to E2 (SRAS1 to SRAS2); the stimulus then shifts AD from AD1 to AD2, taking it to E3 at Yf with the price level at PL3.

Problem 38. An economy has an unemployment rate of 4% when the natural rate is 5.5%. (a) What type of gap is this? (b) Show it on a graph. (c) Explain two ways the economy can return to potential output, and what happens to the price level in each.

Show solution

(a) Inflationary gap. (b) Short-run equilibrium to the right of LRAS. (c) Self-correction: wages rise, SRAS shifts left, price level rises further. Contractionary policy: AD shifts left, price level falls.

Problem 39. The economy is in a recessionary gap. The government increases its purchases of goods and services, and consumers become more optimistic at the same time. Explain what happens to AD and how the price level and the output gap are affected.

Show solution

Both G and C rise, so AD shifts right, by more than from the government action alone. The price level rises, real GDP rises, and the recessionary gap shrinks (it closes if AD reaches LRAS; if the shift is larger than needed, an inflationary gap results).
