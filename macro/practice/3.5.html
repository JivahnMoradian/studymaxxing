Fiscal Policy

Read the theory → Drill 3.5 →

A. Choosing a Policy for a Gap

Problem 1. The unemployment rate is 9% and the natural rate is 5%. Which type of fiscal policy is appropriate? Name two specific actions and show the effect on AD.

Show solution

Recessionary gap, so expansionary fiscal policy: for example, increase government purchases (infrastructure) and cut income taxes. AD shifts right, raising real GDP toward Yf and the price level.

Problem 2. The economy is growing so fast that unemployment is 3% (natural rate 5%) and inflation is accelerating. What policy would be appropriate?

Show solution

Inflationary gap, so contractionary fiscal policy: cut government purchases and/or raise taxes. AD shifts left, lowering the price level and bringing real GDP back to Yf.

Problem 3. Real GDP is 4% below potential. Policymakers choose between cutting income taxes and increasing infrastructure spending of the same size. Which has the larger effect on AD, and why?

Show solution

Infrastructure spending (a government purchase) has the larger effect: the entire amount is spent in round one. A tax cut is partly saved (the MPS share), so its first-round effect is smaller.

Problem 4. For each situation, state whether fiscal policy should be expansionary or contractionary: (a) real GDP exceeds potential; (b) unemployment is above the natural rate; (c) the economy is at long-run equilibrium; (d) a sharp fall in consumer confidence has opened a recessionary gap.

Show solution

(a) Contractionary. (b) Expansionary. (c) Neither (no gap, keep the policy neutral). (d) Expansionary.

Problem 5. Describe how expansionary fiscal policy affects the price level, real GDP, and unemployment, starting from a recessionary gap.

Show solution

AD shifts right: price level rises, real GDP rises toward Yf, unemployment falls toward the natural rate.

Problem 6. Contractionary fiscal policy is used to fight an inflationary gap. Describe the short-run effect on the price level, real GDP, and unemployment.

Show solution

AD shifts left: price level falls, real GDP falls to Yf, unemployment rises back to the natural rate.

Problem 7. The economy is in a recessionary gap. Policymakers choose to do nothing and wait. Compare the final price level with the outcome of expansionary fiscal policy.

Show solution

Self-correction (SRAS shifts right) ends at a lower price level; expansionary policy (AD shifts right) ends at a higher price level. Output is Yf in both.

Problem 8. An economy has a recessionary gap, but inflation is already high from a prior supply shock. Why is policy difficult?

Show solution

Expansionary policy fixes unemployment but pushes the price level up further (inflation worsens); doing nothing leaves unemployment high. Fiscal policy can address only one of the two problems, so policymakers must prioritize.

B. Discretionary vs. Automatic

Problem 9. Classify each as discretionary or automatic: (a) Congress passes a $500 billion infrastructure act; (b) unemployment insurance payments rise as layoffs increase; (c) tax revenue falls as incomes decline under a progressive income tax; (d) Congress votes to send $1,200 rebate checks to households.

Show solution

(a) Discretionary (new law). (b) Automatic. (c) Automatic. (d) Discretionary.

Problem 10. Explain how a progressive income tax acts as an automatic stabilizer in a recession.

Show solution

As incomes fall, households move into lower tax brackets and pay a smaller share of income in tax. Tax revenue falls faster than income, so disposable income falls less than income, cushioning consumption and AD, with no new legislation.

Problem 11. Real GDP falls by 500, the average tax rate on income is 20%, and unemployment benefits rise by 35. By how much does the deficit change automatically?

Show solution

Tax revenue falls by 0.2 × 500 = 100.
Transfers rise by 35.
The deficit rises by 100 + 35 = 135, with no change in law.

Problem 12. In an expansion, the same stabilizers work in reverse. Describe the effects on tax revenue, transfers, and the budget balance.

Show solution

Tax revenue rises (faster than income under progressive taxes), transfers fall, and the budget moves toward surplus. This restrains spending and cools the expansion.

Problem 13. True or false: "Automatic stabilizers eliminate recessions."

Show solution

False. They reduce the size of fluctuations and act without delay, but they do not eliminate recessions; discretionary policy or monetary policy may still be needed.

Problem 14. A president signs a law cutting corporate tax rates to encourage business spending. Is this discretionary or automatic? Which component of AD is it meant to change?

Show solution

Discretionary (requires a new law). It is meant to raise investment (I), shifting AD right.

Problem 15. Why might a government prefer automatic stabilizers to discretionary stimulus? Give one disadvantage of automatic stabilizers.

Show solution

Advantage: no recognition or legislative lag; they respond immediately and proportionally. Disadvantage: they only soften downturns, are not tailored to the specific shock, and cannot fully close a large gap.

C. Deficits, Surpluses and the Debt

Problem 16. G = 2,400, transfers = 800, and tax revenue = 2,900. Find the budget balance.

Show solution

Balance = T − G − TR = 2,900 − 2,400 − 800 = −300, a deficit of 300.

Problem 17. The national debt is 20,000 at the start of the year. The government runs a deficit of 300. What is the debt at the end of the year? What if there had been a surplus of 150 instead?

Show solution

Deficit: debt = 20,000 + 300 = 20,300.
Surplus: debt = 20,000 − 150 = 19,850.

Problem 18. Tax revenue is T = 0.25Y. Spending (G plus transfers) is 2,700. Find the budget balance when Y = 10,000, Y = 9,000, and Y = 11,000.

Show solution

Y = 10,000: T = 2,500, balance = −200.
Y = 9,000: T = 2,250, balance = −450.
Y = 11,000: T = 2,750, balance = +50.
The deficit grows in recession automatically.

Problem 19. Using T = 0.25Y, G + TR = 2,700, potential output of 10,000, and actual GDP of 9,200, find the actual deficit, the structural deficit, and the cyclical deficit.

Show solution

Actual: T = 2,300, deficit = 2,700 − 2,300 = 400.
Structural (at Yf): T = 2,500, deficit = 200.
Cyclical = 400 − 200 = 200.

Problem 20. A government with T = 0.2Y and G + TR = 1,500 has potential output 7,000. In a boom Y = 7,600. Find the actual and structural budget balance and say how much of the balance is cyclical.

Show solution

Actual: T = 1,520, balance = 1,520 − 1,500 = +20.
Structural: T = 1,400, balance = −100 (a structural deficit).
Cyclical = +20 − (−100) = +120 (boom revenue).
The apparent surplus hides a structural deficit.

Problem 21. True or false: "A budget deficit and the national debt are the same thing."

Show solution

False. The deficit is the annual flow (T < G + TR in one year). The debt is the accumulated stock of all past deficits (net of surpluses).

D. Sizing ΔG and ΔT

(Assume a fixed price level so that the horizontal AD shift equals the change in real GDP, and ignore crowding out.)

Problem 22. MPC = 0.7 and the recessionary gap is 210. Find ΔG alone, and ΔT alone, that closes it.

Show solution

Spending multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.3 = 3.33, so ΔG = 210 ÷ 3.33 = +63.
Tax multiplier = −0.7 ÷ 0.3 = −2.33, so ΔT = 210 ÷ (−2.33) = −90.

Problem 23. MPC = 0.9 and real GDP is 360 below potential. Find ΔG and ΔT.

Show solution

Multiplier 10: ΔG = +36.
Tax multiplier −9: ΔT = 360 ÷ (−9) = −40.

Problem 24. MPC = 0.75 and the recessionary gap is 260. Congress approves a 20 increase in G. How large a tax cut is needed in addition to close the gap?

Show solution

The G increase raises Y by 4 × 20 = 80.
Remaining gap = 260 − 80 = 180.
Tax multiplier = −3, so ΔT = 180 ÷ (−3) = −60.

Problem 25. MPC = 0.5. Policymakers want to close a 90 recessionary gap without changing the budget balance (ΔG = ΔT). What ΔG = ΔT is needed?

Show solution

The balanced-budget multiplier = 1, so ΔG = ΔT = +90 (a 90 increase in both).
Check: 2 × 90 − 1 × 90 = 90.

Problem 26. An economy has MPC = 0.8 and a recessionary gap of 100. How much must AD shift to close the gap, and what ΔG does that require? Draw it.

Show solution

AD must shift right by 100.
ΔG = 100 ÷ 5 = +20.

Problem 27. Government purchases rise by 40 and MPC = 0.75. By how much does real GDP rise at a fixed price level? If the gap was 200, is it closed?

Show solution

ΔY = 4 × 40 = 160.
The gap of 200 is not closed; 40 of the gap remains (increasing G by an additional 10 would finish the job).

E. Crowding Out and Lags

Problem 28. Explain how a deficit-financed increase in government spending can "crowd out" private investment. Show the effect on the loanable funds market.

Show solution

Government borrowing raises the demand for loanable funds; the real interest rate rises, so private borrowers cut investment. The net stimulus is smaller than the spending increase.

Problem 29. ΔG = +50, multiplier = 4, and crowded-out investment is 18. Find ΔY with and without crowding out.

Show solution

Without: 4 × 50 = 200.
With: 4 × (50 − 18) = 128.

Problem 30. In which circumstances is crowding out smallest?

Show solution

When the economy has a lot of idle resources (deep recession), when the central bank keeps interest rates low, or when the economy has high saving. Then borrowing does not push interest rates up much.

Problem 31. Identify each lag. (a) Statisticians need months to confirm a recession began. (b) Congress debates a stimulus bill for three months. (c) The Fed lowers rates, but firms take a year to change investment plans. (d) The agency takes weeks to start paying benefits.

Show solution

(a) Recognition lag. (b) Decision lag. (c) Impact lag. (d) Implementation lag.

Problem 32. Explain how policy lags can make fiscal stimulus destabilizing.

Show solution

If the stimulus is spent after the economy has already started to recover or self-correct, AD shifts right on top of a recovering economy, which creates an inflationary gap, so policy worsens the swings.

Problem 33. A recession starts in month 0, is recognized in month 5, a fiscal bill passes in month 11, spending begins in month 14, and the full effect takes 6 more months. How long is the total lag, and why might it cause a problem?

Show solution

Recognition 5 + decision 6 + implementation 3 + impact 6 = 20 months.
If the economy self-corrects within a year or so, the stimulus can arrive when the economy is already recovering, causing overheating (a procyclical effect).

Problem 34. Why does monetary policy usually have a shorter inside lag than fiscal policy but a longer impact lag?

Show solution

The FOMC meets regularly and can change rates immediately (short inside lag). Fiscal policy needs legislation (long inside lag). But changes in interest rates take time to affect investment, housing and consumption (long impact lag), while tax and spending changes affect spending sooner once enacted.

F. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 35. The economy is in long-run equilibrium. Firms cut investment sharply. (a) Show the effect on a correctly labeled AD-AS graph. (b) The government increases purchases to return to full employment; show on the graph. (c) Compare the final price level with the initial one.

Show solution

(a) AD shifts left: price level and real GDP fall (recessionary gap). (b) A rise in G shifts AD right, back to the original position. (c) Price level and output return to their initial levels.

Problem 36. The economy is in an inflationary gap. MPC = 0.6 and real GDP exceeds potential by 180. (a) Which policy would you choose? (b) Find the change in G needed. (c) Find the change in taxes needed instead. (d) State the effect on the price level.

Show solution

(a) Contractionary fiscal policy.
(b) ΔG = −180 ÷ 2.5 = −72.
(c) ΔT = −180 ÷ (−1.5) = +120.
(d) AD shifts left, so the price level falls.

Problem 37. A government runs a deficit of 400 during a recession, of which 200 is cyclical. (a) What happens to the cyclical part when the economy returns to potential? (b) What is the deficit after recovery, with no policy change?

Show solution

(a) The cyclical deficit disappears (taxes recover, transfers fall). (b) The remaining deficit is the structural one: 200.

Problem 38. Congress passes a one-time tax rebate of 150 billion. MPC = 0.8. (a) What is the maximum ΔY by the formula? (b) Why might the actual effect be smaller?

Show solution

(a) Tax multiplier = −4, so ΔY = −4 × (−150) = +600.
(b) Households may save a temporary rebate (the effective MPC is lower), some spending goes to imports, higher interest rates crowd out investment, and the price level rises.

Problem 39. The government cuts spending to reduce its deficit while the economy is in a recession. What happens to AD, real GDP, and unemployment? Is this advisable?

Show solution

AD shifts left, real GDP falls, unemployment rises, and the recessionary gap widens. Many economists advise delaying deficit reduction until the economy recovers (it is contractionary at the wrong time).
