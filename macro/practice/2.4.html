Inflation, Price Indices & the Business Cycle

A. CPI from a Market Basket

Problem 1. A basket contains 5 pizzas, 4 movie tickets and 30 gallons of gas. Prices: base year pizza $12, ticket $10, gas $3; year 2 pizza $15, ticket $12, gas $3.50. Find the CPI in year 2.

Show solution
  • Base year cost: 5(12) + 4(10) + 30(3) = 60 + 40 + 90 = 190.
  • Year 2 cost: 5(15) + 4(12) + 30(3.50) = 75 + 48 + 105 = 228.
CPI2 = 228190 × 100 = 120

The basket costs 20% more than in the base year.

Problem 2. Find the cost of the basket and the CPI in each year.

Show solution
  • 2020: 10(6) + 3(20) + 8(3) = 60 + 60 + 24 = 144, so CPI = 100.
  • 2021: 10(6.5) + 3(21) + 8(3.5) = 65 + 63 + 28 = 156, so CPI = 156 ÷ 144 × 100 = 108.3.
  • 2022: 10(7) + 3(22) + 8(4) = 70 + 66 + 32 = 168, so CPI = 168 ÷ 144 × 100 = 116.7.

Problem 3. Use Problem 2 to find the inflation rate in 2021 and in 2022. What is happening to inflation?

Show solution
  • 2021: (108.3 − 100) ÷ 100 = 8.3% (equivalently 156 ÷ 144 − 1).
  • 2022: (116.7 − 108.3) ÷ 108.3 = 168 ÷ 156 − 1 = 7.7%.

Prices are still rising but at a slower rate, so this is disinflation.

Problem 4. The basket cost 250 in the base year and the CPI is now 132. What does the same basket cost today?

Show solution

Cost = 250 × 132 ÷ 100 = 330.

Problem 5. A basket of 8 apples and 6 loaves of bread costs $1.25 per apple and $3.00 per loaf in the base year. This year apples cost $1.40 and the CPI is 115. What must a loaf cost?

Show solution

Base cost = 8(1.25) + 6(3) = 10 + 18 = 28.
Current cost = 28 × 1.15 = 32.20.

Apples cost 8(1.40) = 11.20, so bread costs 32.20 − 11.20 = 21.00 for 6 loaves, or $3.50 per loaf.

Problem 6. In Problem 1, which good's price rise contributed the most dollars to the increase in the basket's cost?

Show solution

Change in cost: pizza 75 − 60 = +15; tickets 48 − 40 = +8; gas 105 − 90 = +15.
Pizza and gas each contributed $15 of the $38 increase (38 ÷ 190 = 20%).

B. The Inflation Rate from a Price Index

Problem 7. The CPI rises from 238.0 to 245.1. Find the inflation rate.

Show solution
245.1 - 238.0238.0 × 100 = 3.0%

Problem 8. CPIs are 236.0 (2018), 242.1 (2019) and 247.0 (2020). Find inflation in 2019 and 2020 and describe the trend.

Show solution
  • 2019: (242.1 − 236.0) ÷ 236.0 = 2.6%
  • 2020: (247.0 − 242.1) ÷ 242.1 = 2.0%

Inflation fell but remained positive: disinflation.

Problem 9. The CPI is 240 this year and inflation is 2.5%. What will the CPI be next year?

Show solution

240 × 1.025 = 246.0.

Problem 10. The CPI falls from 100 to 98.5. Find the rate of change and name it.

Show solution

(98.5 − 100) ÷ 100 = −1.5%: deflation.

Problem 11. The CPI is 140 in year 1 and 154 in year 2. Is inflation 14%? Compute it.

Show solution

No. Inflation = (154 − 140) ÷ 140 = 10%.
A rise of 14 index points is not 14%, because the percentage is measured against last year's level.

C. Real Wages and Comparing Dollars Over Time

Problem 12. A worker earns $18.60 an hour and the CPI is 124 (base year = 100). Find the real wage.

Show solution

Real wage = 18.60 ÷ 124 × 100 = $15.00 in base-year dollars.

Problem 13. Nominal wages rise 4% while the CPI rises 6%. What happens to real wages?

Show solution

Approximately 4% − 6% = −2%.
Exactly: 1.04 ÷ 1.06 − 1 = −1.9%.
The worker can buy less than before despite the raise.

Problem 14. In 2015 the wage was $22.00 (CPI = 100). In 2020 it is $26.40 (CPI = 115). Find the real wage in 2020 (in 2015 dollars) and the percentage change in the real wage.

Show solution

Real wage in 2020 = 26.40 ÷ 115 × 100 = $22.96.
Change = 22.96 ÷ 22.00 − 1 = +4.3%.
The nominal wage rose 20% but prices rose 15%, so the real wage rose only about 4%.

Problem 15. A salary of $50,000 was earned when the CPI was 218. What salary has the same purchasing power when the CPI is 258?

Show solution

50,000 × 258 ÷ 218 = $59,174.

Problem 16. An annual salary of $62,000 is earned when the CPI is 121 (base = 100). What is it worth in base-year dollars?

Show solution

62,000 ÷ 121 × 100 = $51,240 (rounded).

Problem 17. A retiree receives a fixed pension of $2,400 per month. Over a decade the price level rises 25%. What is the pension worth in today's purchasing power relative to its starting value?

Show solution

Real value = 2,400 ÷ 1.25 = $1,920, a 20% loss in purchasing power.
Fixed nominal incomes lose when inflation is higher than expected.

D. Purchasing Power

Problem 18. With the CPI at 120 (base year = 100), how much does $20 of base-year purchasing power cost today?

Show solution

$20 × 120 ÷ 100 = $24.

Problem 19. With the CPI at 125 (base year = 100), what fraction of its base-year purchasing power does $1 retain?

Show solution

100 ÷ 125 = 0.80, so a dollar buys 80% of what it bought in the base year (a 20% loss of value).

Problem 20. The CPI was 60 in 1985 and 240 in 2023. About how many times as many dollars does it take to buy the same basket?

Show solution

240 ÷ 60 = 4 times.
A basket costing $100 in 1985 costs about $400 in 2023.

E. Real and Nominal Interest Rates

Problem 21. A loan has a nominal interest rate of 7% and inflation of 3%. Find the real interest rate.

Show solution

r = i − π = 7 − 3 = 4%.

Problem 22. The nominal rate is 6% and borrowers and lenders expect inflation of 2.5%. Actual inflation turns out to be 4%. Find the ex ante and ex post real rates.

Show solution

Ex ante: 6 − 2.5 = 3.5%.
Ex post: 6 − 4 = 2%.
The lender received less than expected in real terms; the borrower paid less.

Problem 23. A bank wants a 2.5% real return and expects 3% inflation. What nominal rate should it charge?

Show solution

i = r + π = 2.5 + 3 = 5.5%.

Problem 24. A savings account pays 3% while inflation is 5%. Find the real rate and interpret it.

Show solution

r = 3 − 5 = −2%.
The saver's money grows in dollars but loses purchasing power: the real return is negative.

Problem 25. A one-year loan is made at 8% when inflation is expected to be 3%. Inflation turns out to be 6%. Who gains, the lender or the borrower, and by how many percentage points is the real rate lower than expected?

Show solution

Expected real rate = 8 − 3 = 5%;
actual = 8 − 6 = 2%.
Unexpectedly high inflation means the borrower repays in cheaper dollars, so the borrower gains and the lender loses.
The real rate is 3 percentage points lower than expected.

Problem 26. If the nominal rate is 10% and inflation is 10%, what is the real rate? What does this say about a borrower's cost?

Show solution

r = 10 − 10 = 0%.
The borrower repays, in purchasing power, only what was borrowed.
Interest compensates exactly for lost value of money.

F. Costs of Inflation and Indexing

Problem 27. Classify each as a shoe-leather cost or a menu cost: (a) a restaurant reprints its menus; (b) people make several trips to the bank each week to keep little cash on hand; (c) a retailer pays workers to relabel shelf prices; (d) a company spends time managing cash balances rather than producing.

Show solution

(a) Menu. (b) Shoe-leather. (c) Menu. (d) Shoe-leather.

Problem 28. Inflation is higher than anyone expected. Who is likely to gain and who to lose: (a) a homeowner with a fixed-rate mortgage; (b) a bank that made that mortgage; (c) a retiree on a fixed nominal pension; (d) a worker whose contract is indexed to the CPI?

Show solution

(a) Gains: pays back in cheaper dollars. (b) Loses: gets back less purchasing power. (c) Loses: the pension buys less. (d) Unaffected: indexing keeps real pay constant.

Problem 29. Inflation is perfectly anticipated at 4%, and everyone builds it into contracts. Is there still a cost of inflation?

Show solution

Yes, but a smaller one. Anticipated inflation avoids arbitrary redistribution because interest rates and wages adjust. The menu costs and shoe-leather costs (and tax distortions) remain.

Problem 30. Social Security benefits of $1,800 a month are indexed to the CPI, which rises from 240 to 252. Find the new benefit.

Show solution

Inflation = (252 − 240) ÷ 240 = 5%.
New benefit = 1,800 × 1.05 = $1,890.

Problem 31. A union contract gives a $22 hourly wage with a cost-of-living adjustment tied to the CPI. If the CPI rises 3.5% over the year, what is the wage after the adjustment?

Show solution

22 × 1.035 = $22.77.

Problem 32. Unexpected deflation occurs. Who gains and who loses between borrowers and lenders?

Show solution

Lenders gain: they are repaid in dollars that buy more. Borrowers lose: the real value of their debt rises. This is the opposite of unexpected inflation.

Problem 33. Why does uncertainty about future inflation harm the economy even if the average inflation rate is moderate?

Show solution

Unpredictable inflation makes long-term contracts, saving and investment riskier: lenders demand a risk premium, and some beneficial long-term projects are not undertaken.

G. Biases in the CPI

Problem 34. Gas prices double and consumers switch to public transit, but the CPI basket still contains the same quantity of gas. Which bias is this, and does the CPI overstate or understate the rise in the cost of living?

Show solution

Substitution bias. The fixed basket ignores the switch, so the CPI overstates the true increase in the cost of living.

Problem 35. A new smartphone costs 10% more than last year's model but is twice as fast. How does this affect the CPI as a measure of inflation?

Show solution

The CPI records a 10% price rise, but part of it reflects higher quality, not pure inflation. Quality improvements mean the CPI overstates inflation.

Problem 36. True or false: "Because of its biases, the CPI tends to understate inflation."

Show solution

False. The standard biases (substitution, new goods, quality, outlet substitution) cause the CPI to overstate inflation.

H. Business-Cycle Phases

Problem 37. Label each phase and turning point: (a) real GDP is rising and unemployment is falling; (b) the highest point before real GDP starts to fall; (c) real GDP is falling and unemployment is rising; (d) the lowest point before real GDP begins to rise.

Show solution

(a) Expansion. (b) Peak. (c) Recession (contraction). (d) Trough.

Problem 38. Identify the peak and the trough.

Show solution

Real GDP rises until Q3 (10,020), then falls until Q6 (9,790), then rises again. The peak is Q3 and the trough is Q6.

Problem 39. Over which quarters was the economy in a recession? Does it meet the "two consecutive quarters of decline" rule of thumb?

Show solution

Real GDP fell in Q4, Q5 and Q6 (from the Q3 peak to the Q6 trough). That is three consecutive quarters, so it meets the rule of thumb. Q2, Q3, Q7 and Q8 are expansion.

Problem 40. Find the percentage change in real GDP from the peak to the trough.

Show solution

(9,790 − 10,020) ÷ 10,020 = −2.3%.

Problem 41. Potential output is 10,000. Find the output gap at the trough (Q6) and at the peak (Q3), as a percentage of potential.

Show solution
  • Trough: (9,790 − 10,000) ÷ 10,000 = −2.1% (output below potential: recessionary gap, cyclical unemployment positive).
  • Peak: (10,020 − 10,000) ÷ 10,000 = +0.2% (slightly above potential).

Problem 42. In a typical recession, what happens to (a) real GDP, (b) the unemployment rate, (c) cyclical unemployment, (d) inflation pressure?

Show solution

(a) Falls. (b) Rises. (c) Rises (becomes positive). (d) Inflation pressure eases (disinflation), because weak demand slows price increases.
