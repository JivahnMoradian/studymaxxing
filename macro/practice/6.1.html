6.1

Balance of Payments

Read the theory → Drill 6.1 →

A. Classifying Transactions (US Perspective)

For Problems 1–8, say whether the transaction appears in the current account or the capital and financial account, whether it is a credit or a debit, and which component it belongs to.

Problem 1. A US farm exports $8 million of soybeans to China.

Show solution

Current account, credit (export of goods, part of NX). Money flows into the US.

Problem 2. A US family buys a German-made car.

Show solution

Current account, debit (import of goods, lowers NX). Money flows out of the US.

Problem 3. A British tourist spends a week at a Florida hotel.

Show solution

Current account, credit: a tourist's spending is an export of a service (US residents sell hospitality to a foreigner).

Problem 4. A US resident receives $5,000 in dividends from shares of a Swiss company.

Show solution

Current account, credit: dividends are income from abroad (net primary income). The purchase of the shares earlier would have been a CFA debit, but the dividend payment is a CA credit.

Problem 5. An immigrant working in the US sends $300 per month to family in Honduras.

Show solution

Current account, debit: a remittance is a unilateral transfer (net transfers). Money flows out and no good or asset is received in return.

Problem 6. A Japanese pension fund buys US Treasury bonds.

Show solution

Capital and financial account, credit: a foreign purchase of a US asset is a financial inflow.

Problem 7. A US automaker builds a new factory in Mexico.

Show solution

Capital and financial account, debit: a US purchase of a foreign asset (foreign direct investment) is a financial outflow.

Problem 8. A US bank lends $50 million to a Brazilian firm, and the US government sends $2 billion in foreign aid to another country.

Show solution

The bank loan is a purchase of a foreign asset: CFA, debit. The foreign aid is a unilateral transfer: current account, debit. Both send money out of the US.

B. Computing the CA, CFA and BOP

Problem 9. A country exports 640 of goods and 310 of services, and imports 790 of goods and 250 of services (billions). Find the goods trade balance and net exports.

Show solution

Goods balance = 640 − 790 = −150.
NX = (640 + 310) − (790 + 250) = 950 − 1,040 = −90 (a trade deficit).

Problem 10. In Problem 9, net income from abroad is +35 and net transfers are −25. Find the CA and CFA.

Show solution

CA = −90 + 35 − 25 = −80.
Since CA + CFA = 0, CFA = +80: a net financial inflow of 80.

Problem 11. Foreigners bought 410 of this country's assets in a year, and residents bought 290 of foreign assets. Net income from abroad was +20 and net transfers were −30. Find the CFA, the CA and net exports.

Show solution

CFA = 410 − 290 = +120.
So CA = −120.
Then −120 = NX + 20 − 30, which gives NX = −110.

Problem 12. A country's CA is +45. Its exports are 500 and imports 470 (goods and services), and net transfers are −10. What is net income from abroad?

Show solution

NX = 500 − 470 = 30.
CA = 30 + income − 10 = 45, so net income = +25.

Problem 13. (Billions.) Goods exports 1,240; goods imports 1,515; services exports 505; services imports 310; income received from abroad 410; income paid abroad 385; transfers received 20; transfers sent 75. Foreigners bought 640 of the country's assets. Find the goods balance, the services balance, NX, net income, net transfers, the CA, the CFA, and the country's purchases of foreign assets.

Show solution
  • Goods: 1,240 − 1,515 = −275. Services: 505 − 310 = +195. NX = −80.
  • Net income = 410 − 385 = +25. Net transfers = 20 − 75 = −55.
  • CA = −80 + 25 − 55 = −110. CFA = +110.
  • CFA = 640 − (domestic purchases of foreign assets) = 110, so residents bought 530 of foreign assets.

Problem 14. A country exports 930 and imports 780 (goods and services), with net income of −15 and net transfers of −20. Find the CA and the CFA. Is the country a net lender or borrower?

Show solution

NX = 150.
CA = 150 − 15 − 20 = +115 (surplus).
CFA = −115: a net financial outflow.
The country is a net lender to the rest of the world.

C. The Identity NX = S − I = NCO

Problem 15. Y = 12,000, C = 7,800, G = 2,100 and I = 1,950. Find national saving, NX and NCO.

Show solution

S = 12,000 − 7,800 − 2,100 = 2,100.
NX = 12,000 − 7,800 − 2,100 − 1,950 = 150.
S − I = 2,100 − 1,950 = 150 = NCO.
The country lends 150 abroad (trade surplus).

Problem 16. National saving is 640 and domestic investment is 710. Find NCO and NX, and say what happens in the CFA.

Show solution

NCO = S − I = −70, so NX = −70 (trade deficit).
The CFA is +70: the country borrows 70 from abroad to fund investment.

Problem 17. An economy has Y = 5,000, taxes T = 900, C = 3,200, G = 1,000 and I = 650. Find private saving, public saving, national saving and NX.

Show solution

Private saving = Y − T − C = 900.
Public saving = T − G = −100 (budget deficit).
National saving = 900 − 100 = 800 (check: Y − C − G = 800).
NX = S − I = 800 − 650 = +150.

Problem 18. A country has NCO = +60 and I = 420. Find national saving.

Show solution

S = I + NCO = 420 + 60 = 480.

Problem 19. Holding investment fixed, a country's national saving rises by 50. What happens to NCO and NX?

Show solution

NX = S − I, so NX and NCO both rise by 50.
The extra saving is lent abroad (or the borrowing from abroad falls).

Problem 20. The government's deficit grows by 80 (public saving falls by 80) while private saving rises by 30. Investment is unchanged. Find the change in NX.

Show solution

National saving changes by −80 + 30 = −50, so NX falls by 50.
This is the "twin deficits" effect: a larger budget deficit tends to come with a larger trade deficit.

D. Reasoning with the Accounts

Problem 21. A country runs a trade deficit year after year. What must be true of its CFA, and what happens to foreign ownership of its assets?

Show solution

A trade deficit means a CA deficit, so the CFA is in surplus: foreigners buy more of its assets than residents buy abroad. Foreign ownership of the country's stocks, bonds, land and firms grows, and the country becomes a net debtor.

Problem 22. True or false: "If the CA is in deficit, the balance of payments is in deficit."

Show solution

False. The BOP (CA + CFA) always sums to zero. A CA deficit is simply offset by a CFA surplus.

Problem 23. For each event, say whether the US current account moves toward deficit or surplus: (a) a recession abroad lowers foreign incomes; (b) a US boom raises household spending; (c) the US increases foreign aid; (d) US firms earn higher profits from their foreign subsidiaries.

Show solution

(a) Foreigners buy fewer US exports: NX falls, toward deficit. (b) Imports rise: toward deficit. (c) A larger transfer outflow: toward deficit. (d) Higher income from abroad: toward surplus.

Problem 24. Foreign investors suddenly want more US stocks and bonds. What happens to the CFA, and what must happen to the CA (in the end)?

Show solution

The CFA rises.
Since CA + CFA = 0, the CA must fall by the same amount.
The mechanism: foreigners need dollars to buy US assets, so the dollar appreciates, US exports fall and imports rise, which lowers NX (see Chapter 6.3).

E. GDP, GNP and the Current Account

Problem 25. A country's GDP is 9,500 and it receives net income from abroad of +120. Find its GNP.

Show solution

GNP = 9,500 + 120 = 9,620.

Problem 26. A country produces a GDP of 600. Its residents earn 15 from foreign investments, and foreign owners of local firms take 40 of profits home. Find GNP and say which is larger, GDP or GNP.

Show solution

Net income from abroad = 15 − 40 = −25.
GNP = 600 − 25 = 575.
GDP is larger.

Problem 27. An economy has GDP = 4,000, net income from abroad = −60, net transfers = +10, C = 2,600, G = 800 and I = 600. Find NX, the CA, national saving (measured from national income plus transfers) and S − I.

Show solution

NX = 4,000 − 2,600 − 800 − 600 = 0.
CA = 0 − 60 + 10 = −50.
National saving = (4,000 − 60 + 10) − 2,600 − 800 = 550.
S − I = 550 − 600 = −50 = CA, so the identity holds.

F. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 28. An economy has Y = 8,000, C = 5,100, G = 1,700, exports of 1,200 and imports of 1,400. Net income and transfers are zero. (a) Find NX. (b) Find investment. (c) Find national saving and NCO. (d) State the CFA and what it means.

Show solution

(a) NX = 1,200 − 1,400 = −200.
(b) I = Y − C − G − NX = 8,000 − 5,100 − 1,700 + 200 = 1,400.
(c) S = Y − C − G = 1,200, and NCO = S − I = 1,200 − 1,400 = −200 (matches NX).
(d) CFA = +200: net financial inflow; foreigners finance 200 of the country's investment.

Problem 29. A country has a CA surplus. Explain, using the BOP identity and the saving–investment identity, what this says about its CFA, its saving relative to its investment, and its ownership of foreign assets.

Show solution

CA > 0 means CFA < 0 (net financial outflow).
With NX = S − I > 0, national saving exceeds domestic investment, so the surplus saving is used to buy foreign assets.
The country's ownership of foreign assets rises relative to foreign ownership of its assets.
