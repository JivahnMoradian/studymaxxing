6.2

The Foreign Exchange Market

Read the theory → Drill 6.2 →

Throughout, e is measured in foreign currency per dollar unless stated otherwise.

A. Conversions and Cost Comparisons

Problem 1. The exchange rate is 0.80 euros per dollar. How many dollars does one euro cost?

Show solution

Dollars per euro = 1 / 0.80 = 1.25.

Problem 2. The exchange rate is 150 yen per dollar. A meal in Tokyo costs ¥12,000. What is the price in dollars, and how many dollars is one yen worth?

Show solution

Price = 12,000 / 150 = $80.
One yen is worth 1/150 ≈ $0.0067.

Problem 3. The exchange rate is £0.75 per dollar. A London hat costs £45. What is the dollar price?

Show solution

45 / 0.75 = $60.

Problem 4. A pair of US shoes costs $120. The exchange rate is 0.90 euros per dollar. What does a French buyer pay in euros?

Show solution

120 × 0.90 = €108.

Problem 5. A laptop costs C$1,560 in Canada and $1,150 in the US. The exchange rate is 1.30 Canadian dollars per US dollar. Where is it cheaper, and by how much in US dollars?

Show solution

Canadian price in US dollars = 1,560 / 1.30 = $1,200.
The US laptop at $1,150 is cheaper by $50.

Problem 6. One dollar buys 0.90 euros and also 120 yen. How many yen does one euro buy?

Show solution

Euro = 1/0.90 dollars, so yen per euro = 120 / 0.90 ≈ 133.3.

B. Appreciation, Depreciation and Percent Changes

Problem 7. The exchange rate rises from 0.80 to 0.88 euros per dollar. State which currency appreciated and compute the percent change in the dollar's value in euros, and the percent change in the euro's value in dollars.

Show solution

The dollar appreciates: (0.88 − 0.80) / 0.80 = +10%.
The euro's value in dollars goes from 1/0.80 = 1.25 to 1/0.88 ≈ 1.136, a change of (1.136 − 1.25) / 1.25 ≈ −9.1%: the euro depreciates.

Problem 8. The yen–dollar rate moves from 110 to 125 yen per dollar. Find the percent changes in the dollar's value (in yen) and in the yen's value (in dollars).

Show solution

Dollar: (125 − 110) / 110 ≈ +13.6% (appreciates).
Yen: from 1/110 = 0.009091 to 1/125 = 0.008, which is −12.0% (depreciates).

Problem 9. The pound goes from $1.50 to $1.35. Which currency appreciated? Give the percent change in the pound's value (in dollars) and in the dollar's value (in pounds).

Show solution

Pound: (1.35 − 1.50) / 1.50 = −10% (depreciates).
In pounds per dollar, the rate goes from 1/1.50 = 0.667 to 1/1.35 = 0.741, so the dollar appreciates by about 11.1%.

Problem 10. The peso goes from 17 to 20 pesos per dollar. Which currency depreciated, and by what percent (in each currency's own value)?

Show solution

The peso depreciates: its dollar value falls from 1/17 = 0.0588 to 1/20 = 0.05, a drop of 15%.
The dollar appreciates by (20 − 17)/17 ≈ 17.6%.

Problem 11. A hotel night in Rome costs €200. The exchange rate rises from 0.90 to 0.95 euros per dollar. How does the dollar price change for an American traveler?

Show solution

Before: 200 / 0.90 ≈ $222.22. After: 200 / 0.95 ≈ $210.53. The dollar appreciated, so the trip is about $11.70 cheaper.

C. Currency-Market Shift Graphs

For each event, draw the market for dollars (vertical axis: euros per dollar), state which curve(s) shift and which way, and say whether the dollar appreciates or depreciates.

Problem 12. Foreign consumers develop a stronger taste for US products.

Show solution

The demand for dollars shifts right; the dollar appreciates (e rises); the quantity of dollars traded rises.

Problem 13. Americans' tastes shift toward imported goods.

Show solution

Americans supply more dollars to buy foreign currency: supply of dollars shifts right; the dollar depreciates.

Problem 14. Real interest rates in the US rise relative to those in Europe.

Show solution

Foreigners want more US assets: demand for dollars shifts right. Americans buy fewer foreign assets: supply of dollars shifts left. The dollar appreciates; the quantity traded is ambiguous.

Problem 15. US inflation rises while European inflation stays low.

Show solution

US goods become relatively expensive: exports fall (demand for dollars shifts left) and imports rise (supply of dollars shifts right). The dollar depreciates; the quantity is ambiguous.

Problem 16. US real GDP grows faster than that of its trading partners.

Show solution

Higher US income raises US imports, so Americans supply more dollars: supply shifts right; the dollar depreciates.

Problem 17. A recession in Europe lowers European incomes.

Show solution

Europeans buy fewer US goods: demand for dollars shifts left; the dollar depreciates.

Problem 18. Speculators expect the dollar to appreciate next year.

Show solution

Investors buy dollars now (demand right) and Americans hold dollars rather than sell them (supply left). The dollar appreciates now; the expectation can be self-fulfilling.

Problem 19. The European Central Bank raises European interest rates.

Show solution

European assets become more attractive: foreigners demand fewer dollars (demand left) and Americans supply more dollars to buy European assets (supply right). The dollar depreciates.

Problem 20. Foreign tourism to the US booms. Show it in the euro market (vertical axis: dollars per euro).

Show solution

Tourists must sell euros to buy dollars, so the supply of euros shifts right. The euro depreciates (dollars per euro fall), mirroring the dollar's appreciation in the dollar market.

Problem 21. Classify each: does the dollar appreciate or depreciate? (a) Americans invest heavily in European stocks. (b) A US export subsidy lowers US export prices abroad. (c) Foreign investors lose confidence in euro bonds and buy US Treasuries.

Show solution

(a) Americans sell dollars to buy euros: supply of dollars right, depreciates. (b) Foreign demand for US goods rises: demand for dollars right, appreciates. (c) Demand for dollars rises: appreciates.

D. Who Gains and Who Loses from an Exchange Rate Change?

Problem 22. The dollar depreciates. Who benefits and who is hurt: US exporters, US importers, American tourists in Europe, European tourists in the US?

Show solution

US exporters gain (their goods are cheaper abroad). US importers lose (foreign goods cost more dollars). American tourists abroad lose (their dollars buy less). European tourists in the US gain.

Problem 23. Italian wine costs €30 a bottle. The exchange rate rises from 0.75 to 0.80 euros per dollar. Find the dollar price before and after and describe the effect on US imports.

Show solution

Before: 30 / 0.75 = $40.
After: 30 / 0.80 = $37.50.
The dollar appreciated, so imports get cheaper and US imports rise.

Problem 24. A US firm sells a machine for $60,000. The exchange rate rises from 0.90 to 1.05 euros per dollar. Find the euro price to a German buyer before and after, and the effect on exports.

Show solution

Before: 60,000 × 0.90 = €54,000.
After: 60,000 × 1.05 = €63,000, an increase of about 16.7%.
A stronger dollar makes US exports more expensive, so exports fall.

Problem 25. A Swiss watch costs CHF 2,400; a comparable US watch costs $2,500. At what exchange rate (CHF per dollar) are they equally priced? Which is cheaper for an American when e is above that rate?

Show solution

The Swiss watch costs 2,400 / e dollars.
Set 2,400 / e = 2,500, giving e = 0.96.
For e > 0.96, 2,400 / e < 2,500, so the Swiss watch is cheaper.

E. PPP and the Real Exchange Rate (College Extension)

Problem 26. A Big Mac costs $5.00 in the US and ¥450 in Japan. What exchange rate does PPP imply? If the actual rate is 140 yen per dollar, is the dollar over- or undervalued, and by how much?

Show solution

PPP: e = 450 / 5.00 = 90 yen per dollar.
The actual rate is higher: the dollar buys more yen than PPP suggests, so it is overvalued by 140/90 − 1 ≈ 55.6% (the yen is undervalued).

Problem 27. A standard basket costs $200 in the US and 1,500 pesos in Mexico. Find the PPP exchange rate.

Show solution

e = P* / P = 1,500 / 200 = 7.5 pesos per dollar.

Problem 28. US inflation is 2% and UK inflation is 6%. Using relative PPP, what happens to the pound–dollar exchange rate (pounds per dollar)?

Show solution

%Δe ≈ π* − π = 6% − 2% = +4%.
The dollar appreciates about 4% (the pound, with higher inflation, depreciates).

Problem 29. e = 0.90 euros per dollar. A US basket costs $50 and the same basket costs €36 in the euro area. Find the real exchange rate and interpret it.

Show solution

ε = e × P / P* = 0.90 × 50 / 36 = 1.25.
The US basket costs €45, compared with €36 in the euro area, so US goods are 25% more expensive.

Problem 30. The dollar depreciates 5% (e falls 5%), US prices rise 3% and foreign prices rise 1%. Approximately how does the real exchange rate change, and what does it imply for NX?

Show solution

%Δε ≈ −5% + 3% − 1% = −3%.
US goods become relatively cheaper, so NX rises.

F. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 31. The real interest rate in the US rises while the real interest rate in the euro area falls. (a) Draw the dollar market and show the effect. (b) What happens to the exchange rate? (c) Show the effect in the euro market. (d) What happens to US net exports?

Show solution

(a) Financial capital flows toward the US: demand for dollars right, supply of dollars left. (b) The dollar appreciates. (c) In the euro market, supply of euros right (Europeans sell euros to buy dollar assets) and demand for euros left: the euro depreciates. (d) US goods are more expensive abroad and foreign goods cheaper at home, so exports fall, imports rise and NX falls.

Problem 32. A trip to Europe costs €2,400. The exchange rate rises from 0.80 to 1.00 euros per dollar. (a) Find the dollar cost of the trip before and after. (b) A French family's trip to the US costs $3,000; find its euro cost before and after. (c) Who gains in each case?

Show solution

(a) Before: 2,400 / 0.80 = $3,000.
After: 2,400 / 1.00 = $2,400.
(b) Before: 3,000 × 0.80 = €2,400.
After: 3,000 × 1.00 = €3,000 (a 25% increase).
(c) The dollar appreciated: American travelers gain;
French travelers to the US lose, so US tourism exports fall.
