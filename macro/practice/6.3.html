6.3

Net Exports & Capital Flows

Read the theory → Drill 6.3 →

A. Exchange Rate Changes, NX and AD

Problem 1. The US dollar appreciates against most currencies. State what happens to US exports, imports, net exports, AD, real GDP, the price level and unemployment (short run, starting from long-run equilibrium).

Show solution

Exports fall, imports rise, NX falls. AD shifts left: real GDP falls, the price level falls, unemployment rises.

Problem 2. The dollar depreciates. Give the same effects.

Show solution

Exports rise, imports fall, NX rises. AD shifts right: real GDP rises, the price level rises, unemployment falls.

Problem 3. The economy is in a recessionary gap. Explain how a depreciating dollar could help, and draw it.

Show solution

Depreciation raises NX, shifting AD right. A large enough shift restores output to Yf, closing the gap and lowering unemployment to the natural rate.

Problem 4. The economy is in an inflationary gap and the dollar appreciates strongly. What happens?

Show solution

Higher US prices abroad and cheaper imports lower NX, shifting AD left. Output falls back toward Yf and the price level falls, easing the inflationary pressure.

Problem 5. A trading partner's income rises, holding the exchange rate fixed. What happens to US exports, NX and AD?

Show solution

Foreigners buy more US goods: exports rise, NX rises, and AD shifts right. (In the forex market, the higher demand for dollars would also tend to appreciate the dollar, which would partly offset the NX gain.)

Problem 6. US consumers' incomes rise strongly. What happens to NX and to the dollar's value?

Show solution

Americans buy more imports, so NX falls. They supply more dollars to buy foreign currency, so the dollar depreciates, which partly offsets the fall in NX.

Problem 7. For each event, say whether AD shifts left or right and which component of AD changes: (a) the euro depreciates against the dollar; (b) the yen appreciates against the dollar; (c) Mexico's inflation rises sharply relative to US inflation.

Show solution

(a) A weaker euro means a stronger dollar: NX falls, AD left. (b) A stronger yen means a weaker dollar: NX rises, AD right. (c) Mexican goods become relatively more expensive: US exports to Mexico rise and US imports from Mexico fall (and the peso depreciates): NX rises, AD right (the NX component).

Problem 8. NX falls by 30 and MPC = 0.75. By how much does AD shift (horizontally)?

Show solution

Multiplier = 1 / (1 − 0.75) = 4, so AD shifts left by 30 × 4 = 120.

B. Computing NX and NCO

Problem 9. Exports are 500 and imports are 600. After the dollar depreciates, exports rise 8% and imports fall 5%. Find NX before and after.

Show solution

Before: 500 − 600 = −100.
After: 500 × 1.08 − 600 × 0.95 = 540 − 570 = −30.
NX improves by 70 but remains in deficit.

Problem 10. National saving is 1,150 and domestic investment is 980. Find NCO, NX and the CFA.

Show solution

NCO = S − I = 170, NX = 170, CFA = −170 (net financial outflow).

Problem 11. Y = 9,000, C = 5,700, G = 1,800 and I = 1,700. Find NX, national saving and NCO.

Show solution

NX = 9,000 − 5,700 − 1,800 − 1,700 = −200.
S = 9,000 − 5,700 − 1,800 = 1,500.
NCO = 1,500 − 1,700 = −200.

Problem 12. NCO is −120 and investment is 760. Find national saving.

Show solution

S = I + NCO = 760 − 120 = 640.

Problem 13. MPC = 0.6 and NX rises by 24 as the dollar depreciates. How far does AD shift?

Show solution

Multiplier = 1 / (1 − 0.6) = 2.5, so AD shifts right by 24 × 2.5 = 60.

Problem 14. A country exports 830 and imports 790, saves 940 and invests 900. Check the identity NX = S − I.

Show solution

NX = 830 − 790 = 40;
S − I = 940 − 900 = 40.
They match.

C. Open-Economy Loanable Funds Graphs

For each event, use the three-panel model and state the direction of r, I, NCO, the exchange rate and NX.

Problem 15. The government runs a larger budget deficit, lowering national saving.

Show solution

S shifts left: r rises, investment falls (crowding out), NCO falls, the supply of dollars shifts left, the dollar appreciates, and NX falls.

Problem 16. Households save more (S rises).

Show solution

S shifts right: r falls, investment rises, NCO rises, the dollar depreciates, and NX rises.

Problem 17. A new tax credit boosts domestic investment demand.

Show solution

Demand for loanable funds (I + NCO) shifts right: r rises, investment rises (the shift outweighs the higher r), NCO falls, the dollar appreciates, and NX falls.

Problem 18. Political instability at home causes residents to move their wealth abroad (capital flight).

Show solution

NCO is higher at every r: the NCO schedule shifts right. (Residents still save the same amount; they now use more of it to buy foreign assets, which adds to the demand for loanable funds. Another way to see it: fewer funds are left for domestic borrowers, which would be a leftward shift in the supply of funds available at home. Either way r rises.) r rises, investment falls, NCO rises, the supply of dollars shifts right, the dollar depreciates sharply, and NX rises.

Problem 19. The US imposes an import quota. Describe the effects on r, NCO, the exchange rate and NX.

Show solution

The quota reduces imports, so NX demand shifts right in the foreign-exchange panel and NX rises at first;
AD shifts right.
The dollar appreciates, which makes exports more expensive and partly offsets the gain. r, investment and NCO are unchanged (determined by S, I and capital flows). (In the long run NX = S − I returns to its original value: see the blue button in the lesson.)

Problem 20. A change in foreign tastes reduces demand for US goods at every exchange rate.

Show solution

NX demand shifts left: the real exchange rate falls (the dollar depreciates). r, investment, NCO and NX are unchanged.

Problem 21. Foreign real interest rates rise. Describe the effects.

Show solution

Foreign assets become more attractive, so NCO is higher at every domestic r: the NCO schedule shifts right. Same result as capital flight: r rises, investment falls, NCO rises, the dollar depreciates, NX rises.

D. Real Interest Rates and Capital Flows

Problem 22. The US real interest rate is 4% and the euro-area real interest rate is 2%. Which way does financial capital flow, and what happens to the dollar?

Show solution

Capital flows into the US. The demand for dollars rises and the supply falls: the dollar appreciates.

Problem 23. The Fed lowers interest rates (the US real rate falls below foreign rates). Describe the effects on capital flows, the dollar and NX.

Show solution

Capital flows out of the US. The demand for dollars falls and the supply rises: the dollar depreciates, so NX rises.

Problem 24. US nominal rates are 6% with inflation of 4%. Euro-area nominal rates are 5% with inflation of 1%. Where will capital flow?

Show solution

Real rates: US = 6 − 4 = 2%; euro area = 5 − 1 = 4%.
Capital flows toward the euro area, despite the higher US nominal rate.
The dollar depreciates.

Problem 25. Rank three countries by their attractiveness to foreign investors: A (nominal 9%, inflation 6%), B (nominal 5%, inflation 1%), C (nominal 7%, inflation 2%).

Show solution

Real rates: A = 3%, B = 4%, C = 5%.
Ranking: C, B, A.

Problem 26. Capital inflows rise strongly into the US. What happens to the supply of loanable funds in the US, and to the US real interest rate?

Show solution

Foreign savers add to the pool of loanable funds: the effective supply of funds rises, which tends to lower the US real interest rate (relative to what it would be) and allows more domestic investment.

E. Trade Deficits and Surpluses

Problem 27. A country has a persistent trade deficit. What does this imply for national saving relative to investment, and for foreign ownership of its assets?

Show solution

NX < 0 means S < I: the country invests more than it saves. The gap is financed by a net capital inflow, so foreign ownership of domestic assets rises.

Problem 28. Is a trade deficit necessarily harmful? Give an argument on each side.

Show solution

Not necessarily: if borrowed funds finance productive investment, future output and income rise enough to repay. Potential harm: if the borrowing funds consumption, future generations owe interest and dividend payments abroad, and a sudden stop in inflows can cause a crisis.

Problem 29. A country has a trade surplus. State the signs of NX, NCO and the CFA, and say whether it is a net lender or borrower.

Show solution

NX > 0, NCO > 0, CFA < 0. It is a net lender: S > I.

F. Exam-Style Problems

Problem 30. Foreign investors increase their demand for US financial assets. (a) Show the effect in the dollar market. (b) What happens to NX? (c) Starting from long-run equilibrium, show the effect on output and the price level.

Show solution

(a) Demand for dollars shifts right; the dollar appreciates. (b) US exports fall and imports rise: NX falls. (c) AD shifts left: real GDP and the price level fall in the short run; unemployment rises.

Problem 31. A country's government increases its budget deficit, so national saving falls by 140, and the higher real interest rate causes investment to fall by 60. (a) Find the change in NCO and NX. (b) Describe the effect on the exchange rate, and on AD if the economy starts at full employment.

Show solution

(a) NCO = S − I = −140 − (−60) = −80, so NCO and NX both fall by 80.
(b) The supply of dollars (NCO) shifts left and the dollar appreciates.
NX falls, so AD shifts left, partly offsetting the stimulus from higher government spending or lower taxes.

Note: in the AP-style loanable funds graph (Chapter 4.3) you would show the deficit as demand shifting right, because government borrowing adds to demand. This three-panel model instead counts the deficit as lower public saving, so national saving (supply) shifts left. Either way r rises and NCO falls.

Problem 32. National saving rises by 90 and the lower real interest rate causes investment to rise by 30. Find the change in NCO. With MPC = 0.75, find the horizontal shift in AD from the change in NX alone.

Show solution

ΔNCO = ΔS − ΔI = 90 − 30 = +60, so ΔNX = +60.
Multiplier = 4, so AD shifts right by 60 × 4 = 240.
