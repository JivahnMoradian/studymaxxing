UNIT 2 — SUPPLY AND DEMAND

Surplus & Deadweight Loss

Practice these problems →

The Four Surpluses

Consumer Surplus (CS) is the gain to buyers: the difference between what they were willing to pay (the demand curve) and what they actually pay (the price). Graphically it's the area below the demand curve and above the price, out to the quantity traded.

Producer Surplus (PS) is the gain to sellers: the difference between the price they receive and the minimum they were willing to accept (the supply curve). Graphically it's the area above the supply curve and below the price, out to the quantity traded.

Government Surplus (GS) appears only when there's a tax or subsidy: it's the tax revenue (T × Q, a positive rectangle) or the subsidy cost (a negative amount, since the government pays out).

Total Surplus (TS) is the whole pie: TS = CS + PS + GS. (With no government involvement, TS = CS + PS.)

Computing Surplus for Linear Curves

CS and PS are triangles, so:

Area = ½ × Base × Height
  • Base = the equilibrium quantity (how far the triangle extends along the horizontal axis).
  • CS Height = (demand's vertical intercept − equilibrium price).
  • PS Height = (equilibrium price − supply's vertical intercept).

To find an intercept, set Q = 0 in that curve and solve for P.

Why Equilibrium Maximizes Total Surplus

At the free-market equilibrium, every trade where a buyer values the good more than it costs a seller actually happens, and no trade happens where cost exceeds value. No mutually beneficial trade is missed and no wasteful trade occurs, so CS + PS is as large as possible. This is allocative efficiency.

Deadweight Loss

Deadweight Loss (DWL) is the reduction in total surplus when a market is pushed away from the efficient equilibrium: by a tax, subsidy (beyond correcting an externality), price control, quota, or monopoly. It represents mutually beneficial trades that no longer happen.

For a linear market, DWL is the triangle between the demand and supply curves over the lost quantity: DWL = ½ × (reduction in quantity) × (wedge between the demand price and supply price at the new quantity).

For a per-unit tax, since the tax itself is the vertical wedge, this simplifies to

DWL = ½ × Tax × (Q* - Qtax)

Surplus as Integrals
For general (non-linear) curves, CS = ∫0Q [Pd(q) − P*] dq and PS = ∫0Q [P* − Ps(q)] dq.
The triangle formulas are just the linear special case.

The Efficiency Theorems
The result that competitive equilibrium maximizes total surplus is the intuition behind the First Welfare Theorem: under competition with no externalities, public goods, or market power, equilibrium is Pareto efficient.
The surplus-maximizing quantity is exactly where marginal social benefit = marginal social cost — the hook into Unit 6's market-failure analysis, where a wedge between private and social curves reopens a DWL even without government action.
