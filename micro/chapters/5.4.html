UNIT 5 — FACTOR MARKETS

Monopsony

Practice these problems →

A Single Buyer of Labor

A Monopsony is a market with a single buyer of a resource: for example, the only major employer in a town, or a specialized employer of one kind of worker. Just as a monopoly has power over the price it sells at, a monopsony has power over the wage it pays.

Why Marginal Factor Cost Exceeds the Wage

A monopsonist faces the upward-sloping market labor-supply curve: to hire one more worker it must offer a higher wage — and (without wage discrimination) it must pay that higher wage to every worker, not just the new one. So the cost of an extra worker — the Marginal Factor Cost (MFC) — is the new wage plus the raises given to all existing workers. That makes MFC lie above the labor-supply (wage) curve.

For a linear supply curve, MFC has the same intercept and twice the slope (the same "twice the slope" trick as a monopolist's MR).

The Monopsony Outcome

The monopsonist hires where MRP = MFC (not MRP = wage), then pays the wage read off the labor-supply curve at that quantity — which is below the workers' MRP. Compared with a competitive labor market (where wage = MRP = supply), a monopsony:

  • Hires Fewer workers, and
  • Pays a Lower Wage.
How to get to Marginal Factor Cost

To get to MFC, first calculate the total factor cost — the wage times the number of workers, TFC = W × L.
Since the labor-supply curve is upward-sloping, the wage rises with hiring, so write it as a linear function W = a + bL.
Substituting gives TFC = (a + bL)L = aL + bL².

The Marginal Factor Cost is then the derivative of total factor cost:

dTFCdL = a + 2bL

So MFC = a + 2bL — the same intercept as supply but twice the slope, which is why MFC lies above the labor-supply curve.

The Minimum-Wage Twist

Because a monopsony holds wages below MRP, a well-set minimum wage can raise both the wage and employment: it removes the firm's incentive to restrict hiring to hold wages down (the mirror image of how a price floor behaves, but with the counterintuitive employment result). This is a key reason economists don't treat the minimum-wage/employment question as settled.

Go Deeper: College-Level

The Exploitation Gap
In equilibrium a monopsonist pays W < MRP; the gap MRP − W is sometimes called monopsonistic exploitation: workers are paid less than the value of their marginal product. The degree of the gap depends on how inelastic labor supply is (how hard it is for workers to go elsewhere), which is why monopsony power is greater in company towns and for highly specialized skills.
