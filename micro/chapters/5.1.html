UNIT 5 — FACTOR MARKETS

The Demand for Labor (MRP)

Practice these problems →

Factor Markets and Derived Demand

In factor (resource) markets the roles flip: households are the sellers (of labor, land, capital) and firms are the buyers. The demand for a factor is a derived demand — a firm wants workers only because of the output they produce and the revenue that output earns. If demand for the product rises, so does demand for the labor that makes it.

Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)

A firm values an extra worker by the extra revenue that worker generates — the marginal revenue product:

MRP = MP × MR

For a firm selling in a perfectly competitive product market, price = marginal revenue, so MRP = MP × P. MRP falls as more workers are hired, because marginal product falls (diminishing returns from 3.1). The MRP curve is the firm's labor-demand curve.

Marginal Resource Cost (MRC) and the Hiring Rule

The Marginal Resource Cost (or marginal factor cost, MFC) is the extra cost of hiring one more unit of the factor. In a competitive labor market the firm is a wage taker, so each extra worker costs the same market wage: MRC = wage.

The profit-maximizing firm hires up to the point where the last worker's added revenue equals their added cost:

MRP = MRC

(In a competitive labor market: hire until MRP = wage.)

Go Deeper: College-Level

Why the hiring rule is the same idea as MR = MC
Hiring one more worker adds MRP to revenue and MRC to cost.
Profit rises as long as MRP >
MRC and is maximized at MRP = MRC — the input-market version of the output rule MR = MC.
The two are consistent: choosing the profit-maximizing output and choosing the profit-maximizing labor are the same decision seen from two sides.

Market Power Lowers MRP
If the firm has market power in the product market, MR <
P, so MRP = MP × MR lies below MP × P.
Such a firm hires fewer workers than a price-taking firm with the same productivity, because each worker's output must be sold at a falling price.
