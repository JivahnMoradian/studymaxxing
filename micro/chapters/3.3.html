UNIT 3 — PRODUCTION, COST & PERFECT COMPETITION

Profit Maximization

Practice these problems →

Revenue and the Two Kinds of Profit

Total Revenue is

TR = P × Q

Profit is what's left after costs — but economists and accountants count costs differently:

  • Explicit Costs are out-of-pocket payments (wages, rent, materials).
  • Implicit Costs are the opportunity costs of resources the owner already owns (the salary the owner gives up, interest forgone on invested funds).
Accounting Profit = TR - Explicit Costs
Economic Profit = TR - Explicit Costs - Implicit Costs

Because economic profit also subtracts implicit costs, it is smaller than accounting profit. A firm earning zero economic profit is said to earn a normal profit: it's covering all its costs including opportunity cost, so the owner is doing exactly as well as in their next-best alternative.

The Profit-Maximizing Rule: MR = MC

Produce each unit whose marginal revenue MR = ΔTR/ΔQ is at least its marginal cost, and stop where:

MR = MC

(with MC rising). Beyond that point, extra units cost more than they bring in and shrink profit.

Measuring the Profit

Once you have the profit-maximizing quantity:

π = TR - TC = PQ - ATC · Q = (P - ATC) × Q

If P > ATC the firm earns a profit; if P < ATC it takes a loss; if P = ATC it breaks even.

The Shutdown Decision (Short Run)

Earning a profit isn't guaranteed — and when a firm is losing money, it still has to decide whether producing beats not producing. In the short run, fixed costs are sunk (paid no matter what), so they drop out of the choice; all that matters is whether revenue covers the variable cost. Comparing price to average variable cost:

  • P ≥ AVC → keep producing (revenue covers variable costs and puts something toward fixed costs).
  • P < AVC → shut down (producing would lose more than the fixed cost alone). The Shutdown Point is minimum AVC.

This rule applies to any firm's short-run output decision. In 3.4 we use it to build the perfectly competitive firm's supply curve.

Go Deeper: College-Level

Why MR = MC, from calculus
Profit is the gap between total revenue and total cost:

π(Q) = TR(Q) - TC(Q)

It is maximized where the derivative with respect to output is zero:

dπdQ = dTRdQ - dTCdQ = 0 ⟹ dTRdQ = dTCdQ

and since marginal revenue is dTR/dQ and marginal cost is dTC/dQ, this is exactly MR = MC.
The second-order condition requires MC to be rising through the intersection (cutting MR from below): otherwise you've found a profit minimum.
This is why we always take the MR = MC crossing where MC slopes up.
