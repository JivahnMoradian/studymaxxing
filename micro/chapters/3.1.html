UNIT 3 — PRODUCTION, COST & PERFECT COMPETITION

Production in the Short Run

Practice these problems →

The Production Function and the Two Time Horizons

A Production Function shows the maximum output a firm can produce from given inputs. Two horizons matter:

  • Short Run: at least one input is fixed (usually capital — the factory size). The firm varies output only by changing variable inputs (usually labor).
  • Long Run: all inputs are variable — the firm can change everything, including its scale.

Total, Marginal, and Average Product

Holding capital fixed and adding labor (L):

  • Total Product (TP): total output produced.
  • Marginal Product (MP): the extra output from one more worker.
MP = Δ TPΔ L
  • Average Product (AP): output per worker.
AP = TPL

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns

As more of a variable input (labor) is added to a fixed input (capital), the marginal product eventually falls. Early workers may raise MP (specialization), but beyond some point each additional worker adds less than the one before, because they share a fixed amount of capital. This is the law of diminishing marginal returns, and it's what ultimately makes marginal cost rise.

The MP–AP Relationship

Marginal pulls the average toward it:

  • When MP > AP, average product is rising.
  • When MP < AP, average product is falling.
  • MP = AP exactly at maximum AP.

(The same "marginal vs. average" logic reappears with MC and ATC in 3.2.)

Go Deeper: College-Level

Marginal Product as a Derivative
With continuous labor, MP = dTP/dL: the slope of the total-product curve.
TP is maximized where MP = 0; beyond that, extra workers reduce output (negative marginal product).

The Long Run: Isoquants and Isocost
When all inputs vary, a firm chooses the cheapest input mix to hit a target output.
An Isoquant shows all (labor, capital) combinations yielding the same output; an isocost line shows combinations of equal total cost.
These are exactly the production-side twins of the indifference curves and budget line from consumer theory (1.4): the isoquant plays the role of an indifference curve, and the isocost plays the role of the budget line.
Cost is minimized where an isoquant is tangent to an isocost — where the marginal rate of technical substitution, MRTS = MPL/MPK, equals the input-price ratio w/r.
It is the direct analogue of the consumer's MRS = Px/Py tangency.
