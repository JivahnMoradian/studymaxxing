The Whole-Course Cheat Sheet

Every must-know formula and rule for intro micro, Units 1–6, on one page. Hit print for a revision sheet.

Unit 1 · Basic Concepts

  • Opportunity Cost = value of the next-best alternative given up. Per-unit: what you give up ÷ what you get.
  • PPF: on the curve = efficient, inside = idle resources, outside = unattainable. Bowed out ⇒ increasing opportunity cost; slope = opportunity cost.
  • Comparative Advantage = lower opportunity cost (trade by this, not absolute advantage). Gains if terms of trade fall between the two parties' opportunity costs.
  • Absolute Advantage = more output from the same resources.
  • Marginal Analysis: act while MB ≥ MC; optimum at MB = MC.
  • Consumer Choice (Utility Max): spend all income where MUx/Px = MUy/Py. Diminishing marginal utility: each extra unit adds less.

Unit 2 · Supply & Demand

  • Equilibrium: set Qd = Qs → P*, then Q*.
  • Demand Shifters: income (normal/inferior), tastes, prices of substitutes/complements, expectations, # buyers. Supply Shifters: input prices, technology, taxes/subsidies, expectations, # sellers.
  • PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP (abs). >1 elastic, <1 inelastic, =1 unit. Midpoint: divide by the average. Point: PED = (dQ/dP)(P/Q) = −b·P/Q for Q=a−bP.
  • Total-Revenue Test: inelastic ⇒ P and TR move together; elastic ⇒ opposite; unit ⇒ TR maxed.
  • Other Elasticities: YED (normal >0, inferior <0, luxury >1); XED (substitutes >0, complements <0); PES.
  • Surplus: CS = under demand, above price; PS = above supply, below price; total surplus maxed at equilibrium.
  • DWL = ½ × (lost Q) × (price wedge). Per-unit tax: ½ · t · (Q* − Qt). More inelastic side bears more of a tax.
  • Controls: binding price ceiling (below eq) ⇒ shortage; binding price floor (above eq) ⇒ surplus.

Unit 3 · Production & Cost

  • Product: MP = ΔTP/ΔL, AP = TP/L. Diminishing returns = MP falling.
  • Costs: TC = FC + VC; ATC=TC/Q, AVC=VC/Q, AFC=FC/Q, MC = ΔTC/ΔQ. MC cuts ATC & AVC at their minimums.
  • Profit: accounting = TR − explicit; Economic = TR − explicit − implicit (opportunity) costs; Normal Profit = zero economic profit.
  • Profit Max (Any Firm): MR = MC.
  • Perfect Competition: price taker, P = MR = AR; produce where P = MC.
  • Shutdown (SR): stop if P < min AVC. Break-even at P = min ATC. Long Run: entry/exit ⇒ economic profit → 0, P = min ATC.

Unit 4 · Imperfect Competition

  • Monopoly: MR < P. Set MR = MC for Qm, read Pm off demand. For P=a−bQ, MR = a − 2bQ. DWL = ½(Pm−MC)(Qc−Qm).
  • Natural Monopoly: declining ATC. MC-pricing (P=MC) ⇒ loss = −FC (needs subsidy); fair-return (P=ATC) ⇒ zero profit.
  • Price Discrimination needs: market power, segmentable buyers, no resale. 1st = personalized, 2nd = menu/quantity, 3rd = groups (higher price to less-elastic group).
  • Monopolistic Competition: differentiated, free entry; SR profit → LR zero economic profit; excess capacity, P > MC.
  • Oligopoly / Game Theory: Nash Eq = no one gains by deviating alone; prisoner's dilemma ⇒ both defect. Cournot: q = (a−MC)/3b each. Stackelberg: leader (a−MC)/2b, follower (a−MC)/4b. Bertrand: P = MC.

Unit 5 · Factor Markets

  • Derived Demand. MRP = MP × MR (= MP × P for a competitive seller) = most you'd pay a worker.
  • Hiring Rule: hire where MRP = MFC. Competitive (wage-taker) firm: MFC = wage.
  • Least-Cost Inputs: MPL/PL = MPK/PK. Profit-max inputs: MRP/price = 1 for each input.
  • Competitive Labor Market: W* where labor demand = labor supply.
  • Monopsony (one buyer): MFC > wage; for supply W=a+bL, MFC = a + 2bL (twice the slope). Hire where MRP = MFC, pay wage off the supply curve (below MRP). A well-set minimum wage can raise both wage and employment.

Unit 6 · Market Failure & Government

  • Externalities: negative ⇒ overproduced (MSC > MPC); positive ⇒ underproduced (MSB > MPB). Efficient Q where MSB = MSC. Pigouvian tax = MEC; subsidy = MEB. DWL = ½ × (external per unit) × (Qmkt − Qeff).
  • Public Goods: non-rival + non-excludable ⇒ free-rider problem ⇒ underprovided (usually tax-funded).
  • Common Resources: rival + non-excludable ⇒ tragedy of the commons ⇒ overused.
  • Asymmetric Information: adverse selection (hidden info before), moral hazard (hidden action after). Fixes: signaling, screening.
  • Inequality: Lorenz curve; Gini (0 = equal, 1 = all to one). Progressive tax narrows the gap, regressive widens it; taxes & transfers lower measured inequality.
