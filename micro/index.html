Unit 1 — Basic Economic Concepts
1.1

Scarcity, Factors & Systems

Scarcity, the factors of production, economic systems, and positive vs. normative analysis.
1.2

Opportunity Cost & the PPF

Opportunity cost and the production possibilities curve — trade-offs, efficiency, and growth.
1.3

Comparative Advantage & Trade

Absolute vs. comparative advantage, output and input problems, and the gains from trade.
1.4

Marginal Analysis & Consumer Choice

Marginal benefit vs. cost, diminishing utility, and the consumer's utility-maximizing rule.

Unit 2 — Supply and Demand
2.1

Demand, Supply & Equilibrium

How markets set price and quantity, and how shortages and surpluses self-correct through the price mechanism.
2.2

Elasticities

Measuring how sensitive buyers and sellers are to price, income, and the prices of related goods.
2.3

Surplus & Deadweight Loss

Consumer, producer, and total surplus — and the welfare society loses when a market is pushed off equilibrium.
2.4

Government Intervention

Price controls, excise taxes & incidence, subsidies, tariffs, and quotas — analyzed with surplus and elasticity.

Unit 3 — Production, Cost & Perfect Competition
3.1

Production in the Short Run

The production function, total/marginal/average product, and the law of diminishing marginal returns.
3.2

Costs of Production

Fixed, variable, marginal, and average costs — their formulas, U-shapes, and how the curves relate.
3.3

Profit Maximization

Economic vs. accounting profit, the MR = MC rule, and measuring profit from P and ATC.
3.4

The Perfect Competition Model

Price-taking firms, the shutdown and break-even rules, long-run equilibrium, and efficiency.

Unit 4 — Imperfect Competition
4.1

Monopoly

Why MR < P, finding the monopoly price and quantity, profit, and the deadweight loss vs. competition.
4.2

Price Discrimination

The conditions and degrees of price discrimination, and why perfect price discrimination is efficient but captures all surplus.
4.3

Monopolistic Competition

Differentiated products, short-run profit, long-run zero profit, excess capacity, and inefficiency.
4.4

Oligopoly & Game Theory

Interdependence, payoff matrices, dominant strategies, Nash equilibrium, the prisoner's dilemma, and cartels.

Unit 5 — Factor Markets
5.1

The Demand for Labor (MRP)

Derived demand, marginal revenue product, marginal resource cost, and the MRP = MRC hiring rule.
5.2

Competitive Labor Markets & Shifts

Labor-market equilibrium, wage determination, and what shifts factor demand and factor supply.
5.3

Least-Cost & Profit-Max Inputs

The least-cost rule (MP per dollar equalized) and the profit-maximizing input condition.
5.4

Monopsony

A single buyer of labor: why MFC exceeds the wage, fewer hired at lower pay, and the minimum-wage twist.

Unit 6 — Market Failure & the Role of Government
6.1

Externalities

Private vs. social costs/benefits, over- and under-production, Pigouvian taxes and subsidies, and deadweight loss.
6.2

Public Goods & Common Resources

The rival/excludable grid, the free-rider problem, and the tragedy of the commons.
6.3

Asymmetric Information

Adverse selection, moral hazard, the lemons problem, signaling and screening, and the principal–agent problem.
6.4

Income Inequality

The Lorenz curve, the Gini coefficient, tax progressivity, and redistribution.
