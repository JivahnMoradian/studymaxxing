Elasticities

Read the theory → Drill 2.2 →

A. PED from Two Points

Problem 1. When the price of a sandwich falls from $10 to $8, quantity demanded rises from 100 to 140. Find PED using the simple (initial-value) method and classify it.

Show solution

%ΔQ = (140−100)/100 = +40%.
%ΔP = (8−10)/10 = −20%.
PED = 40 / −20 = −2
→ |PED| = 2 > 1 → elastic.

Problem 2. Same numbers as Problem 1, but use the midpoint method.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 40 / ((100+140)/2) = 40/120 = +33.3%.
%ΔP = −2 / ((10+8)/2) = −2/9 = −22.2%.
PED = 33.3 / −22.2 = −1.5
→ elastic.

Problem 3. When a coffee shop raises its latte price from $4 to $5, daily sales fall from 300 to 270. Midpoint PED, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = −30 / 285 = −10.53%.
%ΔP = 1 / 4.5 = +22.22%.
PED = −10.53 / 22.22 = −0.47
→ |PED| < 1 → inelastic.

Problem 4. A bus fare rises from $2 to $3 and daily riders fall from 1,000 to 900. Simple-method PED, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = −100/1000 = −10%.
%ΔP = 1/2 = +50%.
PED = −10/50 = −0.2
→ inelastic.

Problem 5. A streaming plan drops from $20 to $16 and subscribers rise from 500 to 650. Simple-method PED, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 150/500 = +30%.
%ΔP = −4/20 = −20%.
PED = 30/−20 = −1.5
→ elastic.

Problem 6. A gym raises membership from $40 to $50 and members fall from 200 to 170. Midpoint PED, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = −30/185 = −16.22%.
%ΔP = 10/45 = +22.22%.
PED = −16.22/22.22 = −0.73
→ inelastic.

B. PED from a Demand Function

Method reminder. Step 1: identify dQ/dP (the slope of demand). Step 2: compute the quantity Q at the given price. Step 3: PED = (dQ/dP) · (P/Q). If you're handed an inverse demand curve P = a − bQ, first rearrange it to Q = (a − P)/b.

(Use Q = 200 − 5P for Problems 7–9, where dQ/dP = −5.)

Problem 7. Find PED at P = 20.

Show solution

Q = 200 − 5(20) = 100.
PED = −5 · (20/100) = −1
→ unit elastic.
(This is the midpoint of the line.)

Problem 8. Find PED at P = 30.

Show solution

Q = 200 − 5(30) = 50.
PED = −5 · (30/50) = −3
→ elastic (upper-left portion).

Problem 9. Find PED at P = 10.

Show solution

Q = 200 − 5(10) = 150.
PED = −5 · (10/150) = −1/3 ≈ −0.33
→ inelastic (lower-right portion).

Problem 10. Demand is Q = 300 − 10P. (a) Identify dQ/dP. (b) Find PED at P = 10 and classify.

Show solution

(a) dQ/dP = −10.
(b) Q = 300 − 10(10) = 200.
PED = −10 · (10/200) = −0.5
→ inelastic.

Problem 11. Demand is Q = 80 − 4P. (a) Identify dQ/dP. (b) Find PED at P = 10 and classify.

Show solution

(a) dQ/dP = −4.
(b) Q = 80 − 4(10) = 40.
PED = −4 · (10/40) = −1
→ unit elastic.

Problem 12. Demand is Q = 150 − 3P. Find PED at P = 40 and classify.

Show solution

dQ/dP = −3.
Q = 150 − 3(40) = 30.
PED = −3 · (40/30) = −4
→ elastic.

Problem 13. Demand is Q = 500 − 25P. (a) What is dQ/dP, and what does it mean in words? (b) Find PED at P = 10.

Show solution

(a) dQ/dP = −25: quantity demanded falls by 25 units for every $1 increase in price (the slope of the demand curve).
(b) Q = 500 − 25(10) = 250.
PED = −25 · (10/250) = −1
→ unit elastic.

Problem 14. You're given the inverse demand curve P = 50 − 2Q. (a) Rearrange it so Q is a function of P. (b) Identify dQ/dP. (c) Find PED at P = 30 and classify.

Show solution

(a) Solve for Q: Q = (50 − P)/2 = 25 − 0.5P.
(b) dQ/dP = −0.5.
(c) Q = 25 − 0.5(30) = 10.
PED = −0.5 · (30/10) = −1.5
→ elastic.

Problem 15. You're given the inverse demand curve P = 120 − 4Q. (a) Rearrange to Q = f(P). (b) Identify dQ/dP. (c) Find PED at P = 40 and classify.

Show solution

(a) Q = (120 − P)/4 = 30 − 0.25P.
(b) dQ/dP = −0.25.
(c) Q = 30 − 0.25(40) = 20.
PED = −0.25 · (40/20) = −0.5
→ inelastic.

C. PED — Predict the Quantity Change

Rearrange the elasticity formula: %ΔQ = PED × %ΔP. With an old quantity, the new quantity is Q₁ = Q₀ × (1 + %ΔQ).

Problem 16. A good has PED = −0.5. Its price rises 10%. By what percent does quantity demanded change?

Show solution

%ΔQ = PED × %ΔP = (−0.5)(+10%) = −5%
→ quantity demanded falls 5%.
(Inelastic, so quantity moves less than price.)

Problem 17. A good has PED = −2.0. Its price falls 15%. By what percent does quantity demanded change?

Show solution

%ΔQ = (−2.0)(−15%) = +30%
→ quantity demanded rises 30%.
(Elastic, so quantity moves more than price.)

Problem 18. A good has PED = −1.5. Its price rises 8%. By what percent does quantity demanded change?

Show solution

%ΔQ = (−1.5)(+8%) = −12%
→ quantity demanded falls 12%.

Problem 19. A good has PED = −0.4 and current sales of 500 units. The price rises 20%. Find the new quantity demanded.

Show solution

%ΔQ = (−0.4)(+20%) = −8%.
New quantity = 500 × (1 − 0.08) = 460 units.

Problem 20. A good has PED = −3.0 and current sales of 200 units. The price falls 10%. Find the new quantity demanded.

Show solution

%ΔQ = (−3.0)(−10%) = +30%.
New quantity = 200 × (1 + 0.30) = 260 units.

Problem 21. A good has PED = −1.2 and current sales of 1,000 units. The price rises 25%. Find the new quantity demanded.

Show solution

%ΔQ = (−1.2)(+25%) = −30%.
New quantity = 1,000 × (1 − 0.30) = 700 units.

D. Total-Revenue Test

Problem 22. A toll road currently charges a price where demand is inelastic. It wants more revenue. Should it raise or lower the toll?

Show solution

Inelastic → price and revenue move together → raise the toll to raise revenue.

Problem 23. A concert promoter finds demand for tickets is elastic at the current price. To raise revenue, raise or lower price?

Show solution

Elastic → price and revenue move oppositely → lower the price to raise revenue.

Problem 24. At what elasticity is total revenue maximized?

Show solution

At unit elasticity, |PED| = 1 (the midpoint of a linear demand curve).

Problem 25. A bakery lowers its bread price and finds total revenue falls. Is demand elastic or inelastic over this range?

Show solution

Price and revenue moved together (both down)
→ inelastic.

Problem 26. A software firm raises its subscription price and total revenue rises. Elastic or inelastic?

Show solution

Price and revenue moved together (both up)
→ inelastic.

Problem 27. For Q = 200 − 5P, compute total revenue at P = 20 and at P = 30. Which price earns more, and how does that fit the elasticity at P = 20 (from Problem 7)?

Show solution

At P = 20: Q = 100, TR = 20 × 100 = $2,000.
At P = 30: Q = 50, TR = 30 × 50 = $1,500.
P = 20 earns more. P = 20 is the unit-elastic midpoint, where TR is maximized — so moving away from it (to P = 30) lowers revenue, exactly as expected.

E. Price Elasticity of Supply

Problem 28. When price rises from $5 to $6, quantity supplied rises from 100 to 130. Simple-method PES, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 30/100 = +30%.
%ΔP = 1/5 = +20%.
PES = 30/20 = +1.5
→ elastic.

Problem 29. A farmer's crop is already harvested and can't be increased this season regardless of price. What is PES in the very short run?

Show solution

Quantity can't respond to price → PES = 0, perfectly inelastic (vertical supply).

Problem 30. Price rises from $10 to $12 and quantity supplied rises from 200 to 260. Simple-method PES, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 60/200 = +30%.
%ΔP = 2/10 = +20%.
PES = 30/20 = +1.5
→ elastic.

Problem 31. Price rises from $8 to $10 and quantity supplied rises from 400 to 440. Simple-method PES, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 40/400 = +10%.
%ΔP = 2/8 = +25%.
PES = 10/25 = +0.4
→ inelastic.

Problem 32. Price rises from $20 to $24 and quantity supplied rises from 100 to 140. Midpoint PES, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔQ = 40/120 = +33.33%.
%ΔP = 4/22 = +18.18%.
PES = 33.33/18.18 = +1.83
→ elastic.

F. Income Elasticity of Demand

Problem 33. Income rises 10% and a household's restaurant spending rises 15%. Find YED and classify the good.

Show solution

YED = 15/10 = +1.5
→ positive and > 1 → normal, luxury good.

Problem 34. Income rises 10% and demand for instant noodles falls 5%. Find YED and classify.

Show solution

YED = −5/10 = −0.5
→ negative → inferior good.

Problem 35. Income rises 20% and demand for bread rises 6%. Classify.

Show solution

YED = 6/20 = +0.3
→ between 0 and 1 → normal, necessity.

Problem 36. A household's income rises from $40,000 to $44,000 and its demand for a good rises from 50 to 60 units. Simple-method YED, and classify.

Show solution

%ΔIncome = 4,000/40,000 = +10%.
%ΔQ = 10/50 = +20%.
YED = 20/10 = +2.0
→ normal, luxury.

Problem 37. Income rises 5% and demand for public-transit rides falls 10%. Find YED and classify.

Show solution

YED = −10/5 = −2.0
→ negative → inferior good (strongly).

Problem 38. A household's income rises from $50,000 to $55,000 and its demand for a good rises from 80 to 100 units. Find YED (simple method) and classify the good.

Show solution

%ΔIncome = 5,000/50,000 = 10%;
%ΔQ = 20/80 = 25%.
YED = 25%/10% = +2.5
→ a normal good, specifically a luxury (YED > 1).

Problem 39. A household's income rises from $30,000 to $36,000 and its demand for a good falls from 120 to 108 units. Find YED and classify.

Show solution

%ΔIncome = 6,000/30,000 = 20%;
%ΔQ = −12/120 = −10%.
YED = −10%/20% = −0.5
→ an inferior good (YED < 0).

Problem 40. A household's income rises from $60,000 to $63,000 and its demand for a good rises from 200 to 206 units. Find YED and classify.

Show solution

%ΔIncome = 3,000/60,000 = 5%;
%ΔQ = 6/200 = 3%.
YED = 3%/5% = +0.6
→ a normal good, specifically a necessity (0 <
YED < 1).

G. Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand

Problem 41. The price of Good B rises 10% and quantity demanded of Good A rises 4%. Find XED and state the relationship.

Show solution

XED = 4/10 = +0.4
→ positive → substitutes (weak, since |XED| < 1).

Problem 42. The price of Good B rises 10% and quantity demanded of Good A falls 8%. XED and relationship?

Show solution

XED = −8/10 = −0.8
→ negative → complements (weak).

Problem 43. XED between two goods is +2.5. Describe the relationship.

Show solution

Positive → substitutes; |XED| > 1
→ strong substitutes.

Problem 44. The price of coffee rises 20% and the quantity of tea demanded rises 10%. Find XED and classify.

Show solution

XED = 10/20 = +0.5
→ positive → substitutes.

Problem 45. The price of printers rises 15% and the quantity of ink cartridges demanded falls 30%. Find XED and classify.

Show solution

XED = −30/15 = −2.0
→ negative, |XED| > 1 → strong complements.

Problem 46. The price of Good B rises from $20 to $25, and the quantity of Good A demanded rises from 300 to 345 units. Find XED and state the relationship.

Show solution

%ΔP_B = 5/20 = 25%;
%ΔQ_A = 45/300 = 15%.
XED = 15%/25% = +0.6
→ positive, so A and B are substitutes.

Problem 47. The price of Good B rises from $10 to $12, and the quantity of Good A demanded falls from 500 to 440 units. Find XED and state the relationship.

Show solution

%ΔP_B = 2/10 = 20%;
%ΔQ_A = −60/500 = −12%.
XED = −12%/20% = −0.6
→ negative, so A and B are complements.

Problem 48. The price of Good B falls from $50 to $40, and the quantity of Good A demanded falls from 200 to 170 units. Find XED and state the relationship.

Show solution

%ΔP_B = −10/50 = −20%;
%ΔQ_A = −30/200 = −15%.
XED = −15%/−20% = +0.75
→ positive, so A and B are substitutes (B gets cheaper
→ buyers switch toward B and away from A).

H. Mixed / Conceptual

Problem 49. Name three factors that make the demand for a good more elastic.

Show solution

Any three of: more close substitutes available; the good is a larger share of income/budget; it's a luxury rather than a necessity; a longer time horizon to adjust; the good is narrowly defined (e.g. "Honeycrisp apples" vs. "food").

Problem 50. A demand curve is a vertical line. What is PED, and give a real-world example.

Show solution

Perfectly inelastic, PED = 0. Example: a life-saving medication with no substitute (e.g. insulin for a diabetic) in the short run.

Problem 51. On a downward-sloping straight-line demand curve, where is demand elastic, unit elastic, and inelastic?

Show solution

Elastic on the upper-left half (high price, low quantity), unit elastic at the midpoint, inelastic on the lower-right half (low price, high quantity).

Problem 52. You're given the inverse demand curve P = 80 − 5Q. Without computing a final number, describe the first two steps to find PED at a given price.

Show solution

Step 1 — rearrange to Q as a function of P: Q = (80 − P)/5 = 16 − 0.2P, so the slope dQ/dP = −0.2.
Step 2 — plug the given price into that equation to get Q.
Then apply PED = (dQ/dP)(P/Q) = −0.2 · (P/Q).
