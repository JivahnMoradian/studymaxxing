A. Negative Externality

(Problems 1–4: MPB = MSB = 100 − Q, private supply MPC = 10 + Q, and a marginal external cost MEC = 20.)

Problem 1. (a) Find the market quantity and the efficient quantity. (b) Graph it out (label D = MSB, MPC, MSC, and mark Qm, Qe, and the deadweight-loss triangle).

Show solution

(a) Market (MPB = MPC): 100 − Q = 10 + Q → 90 = 2Q → Qmarket = 45.
Efficient (MSB = MSC, where MSC = MPC + 20 = 30 + Q): 100 − Q = 30 + Q → 70 = 2Q → Qefficient = 35.
(b) This graph serves Problems 1–4:

Problem 2. Does the market over- or under-produce, and by how much?

Show solution

45 > 35 → the market overproduces by 10 units: the signature of a negative externality.

Problem 3. What per-unit Pigouvian tax corrects this externality?

Show solution

A tax equal to the marginal external cost: $20 per unit. It raises MPC up to MSC, pushing the market quantity down to the efficient 35.

Problem 4. Compute the deadweight loss of the uncorrected externality.

Show solution

At Qmarket = 45: MSC = 30 + 45 = 75;
MSB = 100 − 45 = 55;
gap = 20.
DWL = ½ × (45 − 35) × 20 = ½ × 10 × 20 = $100.

Problem 5. (a) Find the market and efficient quantities, and state whether the market over- or under-produces. (b) Graph it out (label D = MPB, MSB, S = MSC, and mark Qm and Qe).

Efficient (MSB = MSC): 100 − Q = 20 + Q → 80 = 2Q → Qefficient = 40.
The market underproduces by 10 units.
(b) This graph serves Problems 5–6:

Problem 6. What per-unit Pigouvian subsidy achieves the efficient quantity?

Show solution

A subsidy equal to the marginal external benefit: $20 per unit, which raises output from 30 to the efficient 40.

C. Concepts

Problem 7. Classify each as a positive or negative externality: (a) a factory polluting a river, (b) getting a flu vaccine, (c) a neighbor's loud late-night music, (d) a homeowner restoring a historic house.

Show solution

(a) Negative (production). (b) Positive (consumption). (c) Negative (consumption). (d) Positive (the neighborhood benefits).

Problem 8. Write and explain the relationship MSC = MPC + MEC.

Show solution

Marginal social cost equals marginal private cost plus marginal external cost. When MEC > 0 (a negative externality), MSC lies above MPC, so the private market — which only sees MPC — produces beyond the socially efficient quantity.

D. Production vs. Consumption Externalities

Problem 9. For each, state whether it is a production or consumption externality, whether it is positive or negative, and which curve (MPC/supply or MPB/demand) diverges from its social counterpart: (a) a steel mill emits soot that dirties nearby homes, (b) a student who gets a college education, (c) a person who plays loud music late at night, (d) a landowner who drains a wetland used by migrating birds.

Show solution

(a) Negative production — MSC lies above MPC (supply side diverges). (b) Positive consumption — MSB lies above MPB (demand side diverges). (c) Negative consumption — MSB lies below MPB (demand side diverges). (d) Negative production — MSC lies above MPC (supply side diverges).

Problem 10. A good generates a negative consumption externality: private demand is MPB = 100 − Q, the marginal external cost of consumption is MEC = 20 (so MSB = MPB − 20 = 80 − Q), and supply is MPC = MSC = 10 + Q. Find (a) the market quantity, (b) the efficient quantity, (c) the corrective Pigouvian tax, and (d) whether this is over- or under-production. (e) Graph it out.

Show solution

(a) Market (MPB = MPC): 100 − Q = 10 + Q → Qmarket = 45.
(b) Efficient (MSB = MSC): 80 − Q = 10 + Q → Qefficient = 35.
(c) A tax equal to the external cost, $20 per unit (here the externality sits on the demand side, but the corrective tax still equals MEC).
(d) 45 > 35 → overproduction, the signature of any negative externality.

Problem 11. Explain why a positive production externality (e.g. a firm's R&D that spills over to rivals) leads the market to underproduce, and name the curve that diverges.

Show solution

With a positive production externality the true cost to society is below what the firm pays, so MSC lies below MPC (the supply/cost curve diverges).
The firm sets output where MPB = MPC, which is less than the efficient quantity where MSB = MSC.
The market therefore underproduces, and the fix is a per-unit subsidy equal to the marginal external benefit.

Problem 12. A market has no externality on the supply side but a positive consumption externality. Which curve shifts to form the social version, and in which direction does the efficient quantity lie relative to the market quantity?

Show solution

The demand side diverges: MSB lies above MPB (supply MSC = MPC is unchanged).
The efficient quantity (MSB = MSC) lies to the right of the market quantity (MPB = MPC) — the market underproduces, as with any positive externality.
