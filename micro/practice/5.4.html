5.4

Monopsony

Read the theory → Drill 5.4 →

(Problems 1–5 use labor supply W = 2 + 2L and MRP = 50 − 2L.)

A. Marginal Factor Cost

Problem 1. Why is a monopsonist's marginal factor cost greater than the wage it pays?

Show solution

To hire one more worker it must raise the wage, and it must pay that higher wage to all workers, so the extra cost (MFC) is the new wage plus the raises to everyone already employed, which exceeds the wage itself.

Problem 2. Write the MFC function for the supply curve W = 2 + 2L.

Show solution

Same intercept, twice the slope: MFC = 2 + 4L.

B. The Monopsony Outcome

Problem 3. Find the number of workers the monopsonist hires and the wage it pays.

Show solution

Hire where MRP = MFC: 50 − 2L = 2 + 4L → 48 = 6L → L = 8.
Pay the wage from the supply curve: W = 2 + 2(8) = $18.

Problem 4. At that employment level, what is the workers' MRP, and how does it compare to the wage?

Show solution

MRP = 50 − 2(8) = $34, which is above the $18 wage: workers are paid less than the value of their marginal product.

C. Comparison & Policy

Problem 5. What would a competitive labor market produce (wage = MRP = supply)? Compare to the monopsony.

Show solution

Set MRP = supply: 50 − 2L = 2 + 2L → 48 = 4L → L = 12, wage = 2 + 2(12) = $26.
Versus the monopsony's 8 workers at $18, the competitive market hires more workers at a higher wage.

Problem 6. Explain how a minimum wage could increase employment in a monopsony.

Show solution

A monopsony restricts hiring to keep wages down. A minimum wage set above the monopsony wage (but at or below MRP) makes the wage fixed for the firm, so its marginal factor cost equals that wage: removing the incentive to under-hire. The firm then hires more workers and pays a higher wage.

D. MFC from a Labor-Supply Schedule

Problem 7. A monopsonist faces the labor-supply and MRP schedules below. (a) Compute total labor cost and the marginal factor cost (MFC) of each worker. (b) How many workers does it hire, and what wage does it pay? (c) A competitive labor market would hire where MRP = the supply wage: compare.

Workers (L) Wage (W) MRP
1 $4 $30
2 $6 $24
3 $8 $18
4 $10 $14
5 $12 $10
Show solution

(a) Total labor cost = W × L: $4, $12, $24, $40, $60.
MFC = ΔTLC: $4, $8, $12, $16, $20.
(Note MFC >
W at every step — paying the higher wage to all workers.) (b) Hire while MRP ≥ MFC: worker 1 ($30 ≥ $4), worker 2 ($24 ≥ $8), worker 3 ($18 ≥ $12); worker 4 fails ($14 < $16).
So hire 3 workers, paying the supply wage at L = 3
→ $8.
(c) Competitive rule MRP ≥ W: worker 3 ($18 ≥ $8), worker 4 ($14 ≥ $10); worker 5 fails ($10 < $12) → 4 workers at $10.
The monopsony hires fewer (3 vs 4) at a lower wage ($8 vs $10).

Problem 8. A monopsonist faces the schedule below. (a) Compute MFC for each worker. (b) Find monopsony employment and wage. (c) Compare to the competitive outcome.

Workers (L) Wage (W) MRP
1 $5 $20
2 $6 $16
3 $7 $12
4 $8 $8
5 $9 $4
Show solution

(a) Total labor cost = W × L: $5, $12, $21, $32, $45 → MFC = ΔTLC: $5, $7, $9, $11, $13.
(b) MRP ≥ MFC: worker 1 ($20 ≥ $5), worker 2 ($16 ≥ $7), worker 3 ($12 ≥ $9); worker 4 fails ($8 < $11) → 3 workers at a wage of $7.
(c) Competitive (MRP ≥ W): worker 3 ($12 ≥ $7), worker 4 ($8 ≥ $8); worker 5 fails ($4 < $9) → 4 workers at $8.
Again the monopsony hires fewer at a lower wage.

E. A Minimum Wage, Worked Numerically

Problem 9. As in Problems 1–5, a monopsonist faces supply W = 2 + 2L and MRP = 50 − 2L, so on its own it hires 8 workers at $18. The government sets a minimum wage of $26. (a) What is the firm's MFC for any worker it hires at or below the point where supply reaches $26? (b) Find the new employment and wage. (c) Compare to the no-minimum-wage monopsony outcome.

Show solution

(a) At a $26 floor the firm can hire any worker up to L = 12 (where supply gives W = 2 + 2(12) = $26) at a flat wage of $26, so MFC = $26 (constant) over that range — no more "raise everyone" penalty.
(b) Hire where MRP = MFC: 50 − 2L = 26 → L = 12, wage = $26.
(c) Versus 8 workers at $18 with no floor, the minimum wage raises both employment (8
→ 12) and the wage ($18 → $26) — here it exactly reproduces the competitive outcome.

More Practice — Monopsony Outcome

Problem 10. A monopsony faces labor supply W = 4 + 2L and MRP = 40 − 2L. (a) Write MFC. (b) Find the number of workers hired and the wage paid. (c) What would a competitive labor market produce for comparison?

Show solution

(a) MFC = 4 + 4L (twice the slope of supply).
(b) MRP = MFC: 40 − 2L = 4 + 4L → L = 6, wage = 4 + 2(6) = $16.
(c) Competitive (MRP = supply): 40 − 2L = 4 + 2L → L = 9, wage = $22 (the monopsony hires fewer at a lower wage).
