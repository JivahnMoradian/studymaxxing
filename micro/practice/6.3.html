A. Asymmetric Information

Problem 1. Classify each as adverse selection or moral hazard: (a) a person hides a health condition when buying insurance, (b) a driver with full coverage parks carelessly, (c) a used-car seller knows the car is a lemon, (d) a worker slacks off once their job is secure.

Show solution

(a) Adverse selection (hidden characteristic, before). (b) Moral hazard (hidden action, after). (c) Adverse selection. (d) Moral hazard.

Problem 2. Explain the "lemons problem" and name one way markets address it.

Show solution

Because buyers can't tell a good used car from a bad one, they aren't willing to pay the high price a genuinely good car is worth — they'll only offer a price based on average quality. At that average price, owners of good cars refuse to sell (their car is worth more than buyers will pay), so they pull their cars off the market. That leaves a higher share of bad cars ("lemons"), which drags average quality down further, so buyers offer even less, pushing still more good cars out. In the extreme, the good cars disappear entirely and the market can unravel. This is a form of adverse selection — the hidden characteristic (quality) is known before the sale. Remedies restore information: signaling (warranties, certifications, dealer reputation), screening (independent inspections), or disclosure regulation.

Problem 3. Classify each as adverse selection or moral hazard: (a) a person who knows they're a reckless driver buys the most comprehensive auto coverage, (b) once insured, that same driver stops bothering to lock their car, (c) an all-you-can-eat buffet mostly attracts customers with huge appetites, (d) a salaried worker starts taking long breaks now that pay isn't tied to output.

Show solution

(a) Adverse selection (hidden characteristic — being reckless — known before the deal). (b) Moral hazard (hidden action after being insured). (c) Adverse selection (big eaters self-select into the buffet). (d) Moral hazard (hidden action after the pay deal).

Problem 4. Classify each: (a) only homeowners in flood-prone areas buy flood insurance, (b) a ride-share passenger eats messily in the car because it isn't theirs, (c) a job applicant conceals that they were fired from three previous jobs, (d) a contractor on a fixed-price job cuts corners once hired.

Show solution

(a) Adverse selection (high-risk homeowners self-select before buying). (b) Moral hazard (hidden action after, someone else's car). (c) Adverse selection (hidden characteristic before hiring). (d) Moral hazard (hidden action after being hired).

Problem 5. Classify each: (a) after buying travel insurance, a tourist attempts a dangerous hike, (b) a used-phone seller hides that the battery is failing, (c) a person signs up for generous dental insurance right before getting braces they had already planned, (d) an employee with guaranteed tenure stops preparing for meetings.

Show solution

(a) Moral hazard (riskier action after getting insured). (b) Adverse selection (hidden quality known before the sale — a mini lemons problem). (c) Adverse selection (hidden characteristic — the known, planned dental need — before enrolling). (d) Moral hazard (hidden action after tenure is secured).
