4.3

Monopolistic Competition

Read the theory → Drill 4.3 →

A. Short-Run Outcome

(Problems 1–2: a firm faces demand P = 60 − Q with constant MC = 20.)

Problem 1. Find the short-run profit-maximizing quantity and price.

Show solution

MR = 60 − 2Q.
Set MR = MC: 60 − 2Q = 20 → 40 = 2Q → Q = 20.
Price = 60 − 20 = $40.

Problem 2. If ATC = $30 at that output, find the firm's short-run profit.

Show solution

π = (P − ATC) × Q = (40 − 30) × 20 = $200 (short-run profit).

B. Long Run

Problem 3. The firm in Problems 1–2 earns a short-run profit. What happens in the long run, and why?

Show solution

The profit attracts entry by new differentiated competitors, which pulls demand away from this firm (its demand curve shifts left) until economic profit falls to zero (P = ATC).
Low barriers to entry drive the adjustment.

Problem 4. In long-run equilibrium, what is the relationship between price and ATC, and is the firm producing at minimum ATC?

Show solution

P = ATC (zero economic profit), but at a point where ATC is still declining: not at minimum ATC.
The firm therefore operates with excess capacity.

C. Efficiency

Problem 5. Is a monopolistically competitive firm allocatively efficient in the long run? Productively efficient? Explain each.

Show solution

Neither. Allocatively inefficient because P > MC (a markup). Productively inefficient because it produces below the minimum-ATC output (excess capacity).

Problem 6. Name one benefit consumers receive from monopolistic competition that perfect competition does not provide.

Show solution

Product variety / differentiation: a range of brands, styles, and qualities to choose from, which is the trade-off for the inefficiency.

D. Comparing the Four Market Structures

Problem 7. For each market structure — perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly — state whether P = MC or P > MC in long-run equilibrium.

Show solution

Perfect competition: P = MC (allocatively efficient).
Monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and monopoly: all P >
MC — each faces a downward-sloping demand curve and marks price up over marginal cost.

Problem 8. In which market structures is long-run economic profit driven to zero, and why do the other two keep profits?

Show solution

Perfect competition and monopolistic competition reach zero economic profit in the long run because entry and exit are easy: profits attract entrants until they vanish. Oligopoly and monopoly can keep positive economic profit because high barriers to entry block new competitors.

More Practice — Short-Run Quantity, Price & Profit

(In part (c)/(d) the "perfectly competitive" benchmark is the allocatively efficient output where P = MC. In monopolistic competition this markup triangle is partly offset by the value of product variety, so read it as the allocative inefficiency, not a verdict that the market is as wasteful as a monopoly.)

Problem 9. A monopolistically competitive firm faces demand P = 75 − 3Q with MC = 15. (a) Find its short-run quantity and price. (b) If ATC = $40 there, find its profit. (c) Find the perfectly competitive quantity and price and the deadweight loss.

Show solution

(a) MR = 75 − 6Q = 15 → Q = 10, P = 75 − 3(10) = $45.
(b) π = (45 − 40) × 10 = $50 (profit).
In the long run, entry/exit drives economic profit to zero.
(c) Perfect competition sets P = MC = $15, where demand meets MC: 75 − 3Q = 15
→ Qc = 20.
DWL = ½ × (P − MC) × (Qc − Q) = ½ × (45 − 15) × (20 − 10) = $150.

Problem 16. A monopolistically competitive firm faces demand P = 75 − 3Q and has total cost TC = 250 + 15Q. (a) Find its MC and ATC functions. (b) Find the short-run profit-maximizing quantity and price. (c) Find its profit. (d) Find the perfectly competitive quantity and price and the deadweight loss.

Show solution

(a) MC = dTC/dQ = 15;
ATC = TC/Q = 15 + 250/Q.
(b) MR = 75 − 6Q; set MR = MC: 75 − 6Q = 15 → Q = 10, P = 75 − 3(10) = $45.
(c) ATC = 15 + 250/10 = $40;
profit = (P − ATC) × Q = (45 − 40) × 10 = $50 (profit).
(d) Perfect competition sets P = MC = $15, where demand meets MC: 75 − 3Q = 15
→ Qc = 20.
DWL = ½ × (P − MC) × (Qc − Q) = ½ × (45 − 15) × (20 − 10) = $150.
