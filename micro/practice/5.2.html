5.2

Competitive Labor Markets & Shifts

Read the theory → Drill 5.2 →

A. Labor-Market Equilibrium

(Problems 1–2: labor demand Qd = 100 − 2W, labor supply Qs = 3W − 20, where W is the wage.)

Problem 1. Find the equilibrium wage and quantity of labor.

Show solution

Set Qd = Qs: 100 − 2W = 3W − 20 → 120 = 5W → W = $24.
Quantity = 100 − 2(24) = 52 workers.
(Check supply: 3(24) − 20 = 52 ✓.)

Problem 2. At a wage of $30, is there a shortage or surplus of labor, and how large?

Show solution

Qd = 100 − 60 = 40;
Qs = 90 − 20 = 70.
Qs >
Qd → a surplus of 30 workers (unemployment): the wage is above equilibrium.

B. Shifts

Problem 3. The price of the product these workers make rises sharply. What happens to labor demand, the wage, and employment?

Show solution

Higher product price raises MRP → labor demand shifts right
→ wage up, employment up.

Problem 4. A wave of immigration increases the number of available workers. Effect on wage and employment?

Show solution

Labor supply shifts right
→ wage down, employment up.

Problem 5. A firm adopts new technology that makes each worker more productive. Which curve shifts, and which way?

Show solution

Higher MP raises MRP → labor demand shifts right.

C. Concepts

Problem 6. Why is a single competitive firm called a "wage taker," and what does that imply about its MRC?

Show solution

It's too small to affect the market wage, so it can hire any quantity at that wage: it faces a horizontal labor-supply curve.
Each extra worker costs the same wage, so MRC = wage.

D. Determinants — Other Input Prices

Problem 7. Machines are a substitute for the workers in this market. The price of machines falls sharply, and firms treat the two as substitutes. Predict the most likely effect on the demand for labor, the wage, and employment.

Show solution

Cheaper substitute input → firms replace workers with machines → labor demand shifts left
→ wage down, employment down. (The substitution effect dominates.)

Problem 8. Delivery drivers and the trucks they operate are complements. The cost of trucks rises. Predict the effect on the demand for drivers, the wage, and employment.

Show solution

Costlier complement input → firms use fewer trucks and therefore need fewer drivers → labor demand shifts left
→ wage down, employment down.

Problem 9. Consumers' demand for the product these workers make collapses. Trace the effect through to the labor market.

Show solution

Lower product demand → lower product price → MRP falls
→ labor demand shifts left
→ wage down, employment down. This is derived demand: labor demand moves with demand for the output.

Problem 10. Industrial robots are a substitute for these workers. The price of robots rises sharply, and firms treat the two as substitutes. Predict the effect on the demand for labor, the wage, and employment.

Show solution

A costlier substitute input makes labor relatively cheaper, so firms shift back toward workers → labor demand shifts right
→ wage up, employment up. (The substitution effect works in reverse of a cheaper substitute.)

Problem 11. These machine operators and the electricity that powers their machines are complements. Electricity prices spike. Predict the effect on the demand for these workers, the wage, and employment.

Show solution

A costlier complement input raises the cost of running the machines, so firms run them less and need fewer operators → labor demand shifts left
→ wage down, employment down.

Problem 12. Warehouse workers and the forklifts they operate are complements. The price of forklifts falls sharply. Predict the effect on the demand for these workers, the wage, and employment.

Show solution

A cheaper complement input leads firms to use more forklifts, which raises the need for the workers who operate them → labor demand shifts right
→ wage up, employment up.
