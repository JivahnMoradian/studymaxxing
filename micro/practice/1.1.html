A. Identify the Factor of Production

Problem 1. Classify each as land, labor, capital, or entrepreneurship: (a) a delivery truck, (b) an iron-ore deposit, (c) a software engineer's work, (d) a founder who launches a startup.

Show solution

(a) Capital (a manufactured tool). (b) Land (a natural resource). (c) Labor. (d) Entrepreneurship.

Problem 2. A bakery uses flour, an oven, the baker's effort, and the plot of land it sits on. Match each to a factor of production.

Show solution

Flour comes from land (natural resources); the oven is capital; the baker's effort is labor; the plot is land.

Problem 3. True or false: the money a firm uses to buy machines is "capital" in the economic sense.

Show solution

False. Economic capital means physical manufactured tools (the machines themselves), not money. Money used to buy them is "financial capital," a different concept.

Problem 4. Classify each as land, labor, capital, or entrepreneurship: (a) a wind turbine, (b) a farmer plowing a field, (c) crude oil underground, (d) an inventor who starts a company around a new product.

Show solution

(a) Capital (a manufactured tool). (b) Labor. (c) Land (a natural resource). (d) Entrepreneurship.

Problem 5. A coffee shop owner takes a risk opening the shop, hires baristas, installs espresso machines, and rents the storefront's land. Identify the factor of production for each.

Show solution

The owner's risk-taking initiative is entrepreneurship; the baristas are labor; the espresso machines are capital; the storefront's land is land.

B. Positive vs. Normative

Problem 6. Label each statement positive or normative: (a) "Raising tariffs reduces imports." (b) "The government ought to protect domestic jobs with tariffs."

Show solution

(a) Positive (a testable cause-and-effect claim). (b) Normative ("ought": a value judgment).

Problem 7. Label each statement positive or normative: (a) "Rent control reduces the quantity of available housing." (b) "Housing should be affordable for everyone."

Show solution

(a) Positive. (b) Normative ("should").

Problem 8. Label each statement positive or normative: (a) "A $15 minimum wage would reduce teen employment." (b) "A $15 minimum wage is the fair thing to do."

Show solution

(a) Positive (a testable prediction). (b) Normative ("fair": a value judgment).

Problem 9. Label each statement positive or normative: (a) "Cutting income taxes increases consumer spending." (b) "The rich should pay higher tax rates than the poor."

Show solution

(a) Positive. (b) Normative ("should").

Problem 10. Label each statement positive or normative: (a) "The government ought to spend more on public schools." (b) "Smaller class sizes raise student test scores."

Show solution

(a) Normative ("ought"). (b) Positive (a testable cause-and-effect claim).

C. Economic Systems & Scope

Problem 11. In which economic system does a central authority decide what to produce and who receives it?

Show solution

A command (planned) economy.

Problem 12. Name the three basic questions every economy must answer because of scarcity.

Show solution

What to produce, how to produce it, and for whom to produce it.

Problem 13. Classify as micro or macro: (a) a study of pricing in the coffee market, (b) a study of the national unemployment rate.

Show solution

(a) Micro (a single market). (b) Macro (the whole economy).

Problem 14. Explain why even a "free" concert ticket given to you still has an opportunity cost.

Show solution

The opportunity cost is the next-best use of the time spent attending (and of the seat, which could have gone to someone else). Cost is what you forgo, not just money paid, so nothing with a time or resource commitment is truly free.
