The Perfect Competition Model

Read the theory → Drill 3.4 →

A. Produce or Shut Down?

Problem 1. The market price is $12. At the firm's best output, minimum AVC is $15. Should it produce or shut down?

Show solution

P ($12) < AVC ($15) → shut down. Producing wouldn't even cover variable costs, so the loss would exceed fixed cost.

Problem 2. Price is $20. The firm's minimum AVC is $15 and minimum ATC is $22. Produce or shut down, and profit or loss?

Show solution

P ($20) ≥ AVC ($15), so keep producing; but P < ATC ($22), so the firm operates at a loss (it minimizes the loss by producing rather than shutting down).

Problem 3. Price is $25, minimum ATC is $22. Describe the firm's situation.

Show solution

P ($25) > ATC ($22) → the firm produces at an economic profit.

B. Short-Run Profit / Loss

Problem 4. A competitive firm produces Q = 100 where P = MC = $18, with ATC = $15 at that output. Find its profit.

Show solution

π = (P − ATC) × Q = (18 − 15) × 100 = $300 profit.

Problem 5. A firm has FC = $400. At its best output, P = $14 but AVC = $16. What should it do, and what is its loss?

Show solution

P ($14) <
AVC ($16) → shut down.
Its loss is limited to fixed cost = $400 (producing would lose more).

C. Long Run & Efficiency

Problem 6. In long-run perfect-competition equilibrium, what is each firm's economic profit, and what does price equal? Why?

Show solution

Economic profit is zero and P = minimum ATC.
Free entry competes away profits (and exit erases losses) until no firm has an incentive to enter or leave.

Problem 7. State the two efficiency conditions perfect competition satisfies in long-run equilibrium, with the matching equality for each.

Show solution

Productive efficiency: production at minimum ATC.
Allocative efficiency: P = MC, so the value of the last unit to buyers equals its marginal cost.

Problem 8. Explain why a perfectly competitive firm's marginal revenue equals the market price.

Show solution

The firm is a price taker facing a horizontal demand curve: it can sell each additional unit at the same market price, so the extra revenue per unit (MR) is just that price.
Hence P = MR = AR.

D. Market + Firm (Side-by-Side) & Long-Run Adjustment

Problem 9. A perfectly competitive market has demand Qd = 1000 − 20P and supply Qs = 200 + 30P. A typical price-taking firm produces where P = MC at q = 50 units, where its ATC = $13. (a) Find the market price. (b) Find the firm's short-run profit or loss. (c) What happens in the long run, and why?

Show solution

(a) Set Qd = Qs: 1000 − 20P = 200 + 30P → 800 = 50P → P = $16.
(b) The firm takes P = $16. π = (P − ATC) × q = (16 − 13) × 50 = $150 profit.
(c) Positive economic profit attracts entry; new firms shift market supply right, pushing price down until P = minimum ATC and economic profit returns to zero.

Problem 10. A perfectly competitive market has demand Qd = 800 − 10P and supply Qs = 200 + 20P. A typical firm produces where P = MC at q = 40 units, where its ATC = $26 and AVC = $18. (a) Find the market price. (b) Should the firm produce or shut down? (c) Find its short-run profit or loss. (d) What happens in the long run?

Show solution

(a) 800 − 10P = 200 + 20P → 600 = 30P → P = $20.
(b) P ($20) ≥ AVC ($18), so keep producing (revenue covers variable cost and part of fixed cost).
(c) P ($20) <
ATC ($26), so a loss: π = (20 − 26) × 40 = −$240.
(d) Losses cause exit; market supply shifts left, price rises until P = minimum ATC and economic profit = zero.

Problem 11. A competitive market is in long-run equilibrium at a price equal to each firm's minimum ATC of $25. A typical firm produces where P = MC at q = 30 units. (a) Find each firm's economic profit. (b) Is there any incentive for firms to enter or exit? (c) What is the firm's accounting situation called at this point?

Show solution

(a) π = (P − ATC) × q = (25 − 25) × 30 = $0 economic profit.
(b) No, with zero economic profit no outside firm gains by entering and no existing firm gains by leaving, so the market is at rest.
(c) The firm earns a normal profit (it exactly covers all costs, including the owner's opportunity cost).

Problem 12. A constant-cost competitive industry starts in long-run equilibrium with price = $10 = minimum ATC. Market demand then rises permanently. In the short run the new market price is $14, and a typical firm now produces where P = MC at an output where its ATC = $11. (a) Find the firm's short-run profit per unit and state profit or loss. (b) Trace the long-run adjustment and give the new long-run price.

Show solution

(a) Profit per unit = P − ATC = 14 − 11 = $3 profit (each firm earns short-run economic profit).
(b) Positive profit draws entry; market supply shifts right, driving price back down.
In a constant-cost industry input prices don't change, so entry continues until price returns to $10 (= minimum ATC) and economic profit is again zero: at a larger industry quantity.

E. Profit Maximization with a Cost Function (Optimization)

(The full competitive-firm problem: the firm takes the market price P (given directly, or found from market demand = supply), derives MC by differentiating total cost, MC = dTC/dq, sets P = MC to find the optimal quantity q*, and computes profit as π = TR − TC = P·q* − TC(q*). Finally it checks the shutdown rule: if P < AVC at that output, it produces 0 and loses only its fixed cost. To get AVC, take TC, drop the fixed component — the term with no q — to leave variable cost, then divide by q; e.g. for TC = q² + 10q + 400, variable cost is q² + 10q, so AVC = q + 10.)

Problem 27. A competitive firm faces price P = $50 with total cost TC(q) = q² + 20q + 90. Find the profit-maximizing quantity and the firm's profit.

Show solution

MC = dTC/dq = 2q + 20.
Set P = MC: 50 = 2q + 20 → q* = 15.
TR = 50 × 15 = $750;
TC = 15² + 20(15) + 90 = 225 + 300 + 90 = $615.
π = 750 − 615 = $135. (Check: AVC = (q² + 20q)/q = q + 20 = $35 < $50, so producing is correct.)

Problem 28. A competitive firm faces price P = $30 with total cost TC(q) = q² + 10q + 400. Find the profit-maximizing quantity and the firm's profit, and say whether it should keep producing.

Show solution

MC = 2q + 10.
Set P = MC: 30 = 2q + 10 → q* = 10.
TR = $300;
TC = 100 + 100 + 400 = $600.
π = −$300 (loss). Shutdown check: AVC = q + 10 = $20 <
P = $30, so the firm keeps producing — the $300 loss beats the $400 fixed cost it would lose by shutting down.

Problem 29. A competitive firm faces price P = $18 with total cost TC(q) = 2q² + 24q + 60. Find the optimal quantity and profit.

Show solution

MC = 4q + 24, and AVC = 2q + 24, whose minimum (as q
→ 0) is $24.
Since P = $18 < min AVC = $24, the firm shuts down: q* = 0.
(Setting P = MC gives 18 = 4q + 24 → q = −1.5, confirming no positive output is worthwhile.) Profit = −$60, the fixed cost.

Problem 30. The market has demand Qd = 1200 − 20P and supply Qs = 40P − 600. A price-taking firm in it has TC(q) = q² + 6q + 80. Find the market price, then the firm's profit-maximizing quantity and profit.

Show solution

Market price: 1200 − 20P = 40P − 600 → 1800 = 60P → P = $30.
MC = 2q + 6; set 30 = 2q + 6
→ q* = 12.
TR = $360;
TC = 144 + 72 + 80 = $296.
π = $64. (AVC = q + 6 = $18 < $30, produce.)

Problem 32. A competitive firm faces price P = $24 with total cost TC(q) = 0.5q² + 6q + 40. Find the optimal quantity and profit.

Show solution
