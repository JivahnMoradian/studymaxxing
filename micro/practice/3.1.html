3.1

Production in the Short Run

Read the theory → Drill 3.1 →

(Problems 1–5 use this short-run production schedule: capital is fixed.)

Labor (L) Total Product (TP)
0 0
1 10
2 24
3 36
4 44
5 48
6 48

A. Marginal & Average Product

Problem 1. Compute the marginal product of each of the 6 workers.

Show solution

MP = ΔTP: 10, 14, 12, 8, 4, 0 (for workers 1 through 6).

Problem 2. Compute the average product of labor at L = 2, 4, and 6.

Show solution

AP = TP/L.
L=2: 24/2 = 12.
L=4: 44/4 = 11.
L=6: 48/6 = 8.

B. Diminishing Returns

Problem 3. At which worker do diminishing marginal returns begin?

Show solution

MP rises from 10 (1st) to 14 (2nd), then falls to 12 (3rd). Diminishing marginal returns begin with the 3rd worker (the first whose MP is lower than the previous worker's).

Problem 4. At what quantity of labor is total product maximized, and what is the marginal product there?

Show solution

TP peaks at L = 6 (TP = 48), where MP = 0.
Adding a 7th worker would not raise output.

C. MP–AP Relationship

Problem 5. Between L = 1 and L = 2, is average product rising or falling? Explain using MP.

Show solution

AP goes 10 → 12, so it's rising. This happens because the 2nd worker's MP (14) is above the current AP (pulling the average up): the rule "MP > AP ⟹ AP rising."

Problem 6. In your own words, state the difference between the short run and the long run.

Show solution

In the short run at least one input is fixed (e.g. factory size), so output changes only by varying labor. In the long run all inputs — including scale/capital — are variable, so the firm can change everything.

Labor Total Product
1 6
2 14
3 21
4 25
5 28
6 30
Show solution

(a) MP of worker 3 = 21 − 14 = 7.
(b) AP = 21/3 = 7.
(c) MP first falls at the 3rd worker.
