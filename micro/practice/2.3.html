A. Consumer, Producer & Total Surplus

(Market M1 for Problems 1–3: Qd = 100 − P, Qs = P.)

Problem 1. Find the equilibrium, then compute consumer surplus in market M1.

Show solution

Equilibrium: 100 − P = P → P* = 50, Q* = 50.
Demand intercept (Q=0): P = 100.
CS = ½ × 50 × (100 − 50) = ½ × 50 × 50 = $1,250.

Problem 2. Compute producer surplus in market M1.

Show solution

Supply intercept (Q=0): P = 0.
PS = ½ × 50 × (50 − 0) = $1,250.

Problem 3. Compute total surplus in market M1. Then graph it out.

Show solution

TS = CS + PS = 1,250 + 1,250 = $2,500.

(Market M2 for Problems 4–6: Qd = 120 − 2P, Qs = 2P.)

Problem 4. Find the equilibrium and the demand/supply intercepts.

Show solution

120 − 2P = 2P → P* = 30, Q* = 60.
Demand intercept: 0 = 120 − 2P → P = 60.
Supply intercept: 0 = 2P → P = 0.

Problem 5. Compute CS and PS in market M2.

Show solution

CS = ½ × 60 × (60 − 30) = ½ × 60 × 30 = $900.
PS = ½ × 60 × (30 − 0) = $900.

Problem 6. Compute TS in market M2. Then graph it out.

Show solution

TS = 900 + 900 = $1,800.

B. Conceptual

Problem 15. In one or two sentences, explain why the competitive equilibrium maximizes total surplus.

Show solution

At equilibrium, every buyer whose willingness to pay exceeds the price trades, and every seller whose cost is below the price trades, so every mutually beneficial trade happens and no wasteful one does. Any move away from Q* either skips a valuable trade or forces a wasteful one, lowering total surplus.

Problem 16. A tax and a price ceiling both create deadweight loss. In surplus terms, what does the DWL triangle actually represent?

Show solution

It represents the value of mutually beneficial trades that no longer occur: surplus that was being created at equilibrium but is destroyed (not transferred) once quantity falls below Q*.

Problem 17. True or false: a tax transfers surplus to the government and destroys some surplus. Explain.

Show solution

True. The tax-revenue rectangle (T × Q) is a transfer from consumers and producers to the government (still part of total surplus), but the DWL triangle is surplus that is destroyed: lost to everyone because those trades stop happening.
