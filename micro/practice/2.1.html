Demand, Supply & Equilibrium

A. Qualitative Shifts — Determine the Effect on Equilibrium P and Q

Problem 1. Pizza is a normal good. Average household income rises. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of pizza? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Higher income → demand for a normal good shifts right. Along an upward-sloping supply curve, that raises both: P ↑ and Q ↑.

Problem 2. A new oven technology cuts the cost of baking bread. Effect on the bread market? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Better technology lowers production cost → supply shifts right. Price falls, quantity rises: P ↓, Q ↑.

Problem 3. Tea and coffee are substitutes. The price of tea rises sharply. Effect on the coffee market? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Tea pricier → consumers switch toward coffee → demand for coffee shifts right: P ↑, Q ↑.

Problem 4. Printers and ink cartridges are complements. The price of printers rises. Effect on the ink-cartridge market? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Printers pricier → fewer printers bought → fewer cartridges needed → demand for cartridges shifts left: P ↓, Q ↓.

Problem 5. Autoworkers win a large wage increase. Effect on the car market? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Higher wages = higher input cost → supply shifts left: P ↑, Q ↓.

Problem 6. Consumers expect the price of gasoline to jump next month. Effect on this month's gasoline market? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Expecting higher future prices, buyers fill up now → current demand shifts right: P ↑, Q ↑.

Problem 7. Several new firms enter the smartphone-case market. Effect? Then graph it out.

Show solution

More sellers → supply shifts right: P ↓, Q ↑.

B. Solving for Equilibrium from Equations

Problem 8. Demand: Qd = 120 − 3P. Supply: Qs = 2P − 30. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Set Qd = Qs: 120 − 3P = 2P − 30 → 150 = 5P → P* = 30.
Q* = 120 − 3(30) = 30 units.
(Check supply: 2(30) − 30 = 30 ✓)

Problem 9. Demand: Qd = 50 − P. Supply: Qs = 2P − 10. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

50 − P = 2P − 10 → 60 = 3P → P* = 20.
Q* = 50 − 20 = 30 units.
(Check: 2(20) − 10 = 30 ✓)

Problem 10. Demand: Qd = 200 − 5P. Supply: Qs = 50 + 10P. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

200 − 5P = 50 + 10P → 150 = 15P → P* = 10.
Q* = 200 − 5(10) = 150 units.
(Check: 50 + 10(10) = 150 ✓)

Problem 11. Demand: Qd = 90 − 2P. Supply: Qs = P. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

90 − 2P = P → 90 = 3P → P* = 30.
Q* = 30 units.

Problem 12. Demand: Qd = 100 − 2P. Supply: Qs = 3P − 50. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

100 − 2P = 3P − 50 → 150 = 5P → P* = 30.
Q* = 100 − 2(30) = 40 units.
(Check: 3(30) − 50 = 40 ✓)

Problem 13. Demand: Qd = 240 − 4P. Supply: Qs = 6P − 60. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

240 − 4P = 6P − 60 → 300 = 10P → P* = 30.
Q* = 240 − 4(30) = 120 units.
(Check: 6(30) − 60 = 120 ✓)

Problem 14. This time you're given curves in inverse form (price as a function of Q): inverse demand P = 80 − 3Q, inverse supply P = 20 + Q. Find Q* and P*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Set the two prices equal: 80 − 3Q = 20 + Q → 60 = 4Q → Q* = 15.
Then P* = 20 + 15 = $35.
(Check demand: 80 − 3(15) = 35 ✓)

Problem 15. Demand: Qd = 500 − 10P. Supply: Qs = 20P − 100. Find P* and Q*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

500 − 10P = 20P − 100 → 600 = 30P → P* = 20.
Q* = 500 − 10(20) = 300 units.
(Check: 20(20) − 100 = 300 ✓)

Problem 16. Inverse demand P = 100 − 4Q, inverse supply P = 20 + Q. Find Q* and P*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Set prices equal: 100 − 4Q = 20 + Q → 80 = 5Q → Q* = 16.
Then P* = 20 + 16 = $36.
(Check demand: 100 − 4(16) = 36 ✓)

Problem 17. Inverse demand P = 80 − 2Q, inverse supply P = 20 + 2Q. Find Q* and P*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

80 − 2Q = 20 + 2Q → 60 = 4Q → Q* = 15.
Then P* = 20 + 2(15) = $50.
(Check demand: 80 − 2(15) = 50 ✓)

Problem 18. Inverse demand P = 60 − 2Q, inverse supply P = 12 + Q. Find Q* and P*. Then graph it out.

Show solution

60 − 2Q = 12 + Q → 48 = 3Q → Q* = 16.
Then P* = 12 + 16 = $28.
(Check demand: 60 − 2(16) = 28 ✓)

C. Shortage / Surplus at a Given Price

(Problems 19–21 use the market from Problem 8: Qd = 120 − 3P, Qs = 2P − 30, equilibrium P* = 30.)

Problem 19. At a price of $20, is there a shortage or surplus, and how large? Which way does price move? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Qd = 120 − 60 = 60;
Qs = 40 − 30 = 10.
Qd >
Qs → shortage of 50 units.
Price is below equilibrium → it's bid up toward $30.

Problem 20. At a price of $35, shortage or surplus, how large, and which way does price move? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Qd = 120 − 105 = 15;
Qs = 70 − 30 = 40.
Qs >
Qd → surplus of 25 units.
Price is above equilibrium → it falls toward $30.

Problem 21. At a price of $28, shortage or surplus, and how large? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Qd = 120 − 84 = 36;
Qs = 56 − 30 = 26.
Qd >
Qs → shortage of 10 units.
Price rises toward $30.

Show solution

Qd >

Show solution

Qs >

Problem 24. At the equilibrium price of $20, describe quantity demanded vs. quantity supplied and the pressure on price. Then graph it out.

Show solution

At the equilibrium price, Qd = Qs (both equal 30).
There is no pressure on the price: the market clears.

D. Double Shifts (Identify What's Ambiguous)

Problem 25. A good experiences both an increase in demand and an increase in supply. What can you say about P* and Q*? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Quantity definitely rises (both shifts push Q up). Price is ambiguous: it depends on which shift is larger.

Problem 26. Demand increases while supply decreases. P* and Q*? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Price definitely rises. Quantity is ambiguous: depends on the relative magnitudes.

Problem 27. A severe frost destroys half the orange crop and a new study reports oranges prevent illness (raising demand). Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Frost → supply left; study → demand right. Demand ↑ & Supply ↓ → P rises for certain; Q is ambiguous.

Problem 28. A recession lowers incomes (oranges are normal) and the same frost destroys supply. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Demand left (lower income) & supply left (frost). Both shifts lower Q → Q falls for certain; P is ambiguous.

Problem 29. The market for electric cars: a viral review makes EVs far more popular and, at the same time, a new battery plant sharply increases production capacity. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

The viral review raises demand (shifts right); the new plant raises supply (shifts right). Both shifts raise quantity
→ Q rises for certain; P is ambiguous (it depends on which shift is larger).

Problem 30. The market for laptops: a recession lowers household incomes (laptops are a normal good) and cheaper microchips cut production costs. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Lower income for a normal good lowers demand (shifts left); cheaper chips raise supply (shifts right). Both shifts push price down
→ P falls for certain; Q is ambiguous.

Problem 31. The market for coffee: a new study links coffee to better health and a drought wrecks the harvest in a major growing region. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

The health study raises demand (shifts right); the drought lowers supply (shifts left). Both shifts push price up
→ P rises for certain; Q is ambiguous.

Problem 32. The market for beef: new health warnings about red meat are published and a feed shortage raises ranchers' costs. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

The health warnings lower demand (shifts left); higher costs lower supply (shifts left). Both shifts lower quantity
→ Q falls for certain; P is ambiguous.

Problem 33. The market for a streaming service: it adds several hit shows and upgrades its servers so it can serve more subscribers at lower cost. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

The hit shows raise demand (shifts right); the cheaper, higher-capacity servers raise supply (shifts right). Both shifts raise quantity
→ Q rises for certain; P is ambiguous.

Problem 34. The market for solar panels: a government subsidy for buyers expires and manufacturing improvements make panels cheaper to produce. Predict P and Q. Then graph it out.

Show solution

Losing the buyer subsidy lowers demand (shifts left); cheaper production raises supply (shifts right). Both shifts push price down
→ P falls for certain; Q is ambiguous.
