6.4

Income Inequality

Read the theory → Drill 6.4 →

A. Lorenz Curve & Gini

Problem 1. Two countries have Lorenz curves; Country A's lies closer to the 45° line than Country B's. Which country has more equal income distribution?

Show solution

Country A: the closer the Lorenz curve is to the diagonal (perfect-equality line), the more equal the distribution.

Problem 2. What does a Gini coefficient of 0 mean? Of 1?

Show solution

0 = perfect equality (everyone has the same income;
Lorenz curve is the 45° line).
1 = perfect inequality (one household has all the income).
Higher Gini = more inequality.

Problem 3. On a Lorenz curve, the point (0.60, 0.30) lies on the curve. Interpret it.

Show solution

The bottom 60% of households earn 30% of total income. (Under perfect equality they would earn 60%, so this shows inequality.)

Problem 4. If a country's Gini rises from 0.38 to 0.45 over a decade, what happened to inequality?

Show solution

Inequality increased: a higher Gini means the Lorenz curve bowed further from the diagonal.

B. Taxes & Redistribution

Problem 5. Classify each as progressive, proportional, or regressive: (a) a graduated income tax with rates rising from 10% to 37%, (b) a flat 15% tax on all income, (c) a sales tax that takes a larger share of a low-income household's income.

Show solution

(a) Progressive. (b) Proportional (flat). (c) Regressive.

Problem 6. Name one source of income inequality and one government tool used to reduce it.

Show solution

A source: differences in education/human capital (also ability, inheritance, discrimination, or market power). A tool: progressive taxation or transfer payments (welfare, food assistance, social insurance).

Problem 7. Country X has a Gini of 0.28; Country Y has a Gini of 0.52. Which has more income inequality, and whose Lorenz curve bows farther from the 45° line?

Show solution

Country Y has more inequality (higher Gini), and its Lorenz curve bows farther from the diagonal.

Problem 8. On a Lorenz curve the point (0.80, 0.50) lies on the curve. Interpret it, and state what the same point would be under perfect equality.

Show solution

The bottom 80% of households earn 50% of total income. Under perfect equality that point would be (0.80, 0.80): the bottom 80% would earn 80%.

Problem 9. A tax takes 25% of a $20,000 income but only 10% of a $500,000 income. Is it progressive, proportional, or regressive?

Show solution

The average rate falls as income rises (25%
→ 10%), so it is regressive.

Problem 10. Explain why a sales tax on groceries is often considered regressive.

Show solution

Lower-income households spend a larger share of their income on necessities like groceries, so a flat-rate grocery tax takes a bigger percentage of their income than of a high earner's: the hallmark of a regressive tax.

Problem 11. Briefly, what is the equality–efficiency trade-off in redistribution?

Show solution

Redistribution can reduce inequality but may weaken incentives to work, save, and invest, and lose some resources to administration ("leaky bucket"). So more equality can come at some cost to total output: a trade-off society must weigh (a normative judgment).

Problem 12. A household earning $40,000 pays $4,000 in income tax; a household earning $120,000 pays $18,000. Compute each household's average tax rate and classify the system as progressive, proportional, or regressive.

Show solution

Low earner: 4,000 ÷ 40,000 = 10%.
High earner: 18,000 ÷ 120,000 = 15%.
The average rate rises with income (10%
→ 15%), so the system is progressive.

Problem 13. A country's five income quintiles (poorest to richest) earn 5%, 10%, 15%, 25%, and 45% of total income. (a) Give the cumulative income share at the top of each quintile. (b) Write the Lorenz-curve points for the bottom 40% and bottom 60%. (c) Is this distribution equal or unequal, and how can you tell from those points?

Show solution

(a) Cumulative shares: bottom 20% → 5%, 40%
→ 15%, 60%
→ 30%, 80%
→ 55%, 100%
→ 100%. (b) Points: (0.40, 0.15) and (0.60, 0.30). (c) Unequal — the bottom 40% earn only 15% (perfect equality would be 40%) and the bottom 60% earn only 30% (vs. 60%), so the Lorenz curve sags well below the 45° line.
