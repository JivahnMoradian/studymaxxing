Comparative Advantage & Trade

Read the theory → Drill 1.3 →

A. Output Method

(Problems 1–6: in one hour, each country can produce the following.)

Wheat Cloth
Country A 10 5
Country B 8 2

Problem 1. Who has the absolute advantage in wheat? In cloth?

Show solution

Country A produces more of both (10 > 8 wheat; 5 > 2 cloth), so A has the absolute advantage in both goods.

Problem 2. Find each country's opportunity cost of 1 wheat (in cloth).

Show solution

Output method = other ÷ this good.
A: 5/10 = 0.5 cloth.
B: 2/8 = 0.25 cloth.

Problem 3. Find each country's opportunity cost of 1 cloth (in wheat).

Show solution

A: 10/5 = 2 wheat.
B: 8/2 = 4 wheat.

Problem 4. Who has the comparative advantage in wheat? In cloth?

Show solution

Wheat: B's opp cost (0.25) < A's (0.5) → B. Cloth: A's opp cost (2) < B's (4)
→ A. So B specializes in wheat, A in cloth.

Problem 5. Give the range of terms of trade (price of 1 wheat, in cloth) that benefits both countries.

Show solution

Between the two opportunity costs of wheat: between 0.25 and 0.5 cloth per wheat.

Problem 6. Is "1 wheat for 0.4 cloth" mutually beneficial? Explain.

Show solution

Yes: 0.4 lies between 0.25 and 0.5. B (the wheat exporter) gets 0.4 cloth, more than its 0.25 cost; A (the importer) pays 0.4 cloth, less than its 0.5 cost to make wheat itself. Both gain.

B. Input Method

(Problems 7–10: hours required to produce one unit of each good.)

Bread (hrs) Shirts (hrs)
Alice 2 4
Bob 3 12

Problem 7. Who has the absolute advantage in each good?

Show solution

Fewer hours = better.
Alice needs fewer hours for both (2 < 3 bread; 4 < 12 shirts) → Alice has the absolute advantage in both.

Problem 8. Find each person's opportunity cost of 1 bread (in shirts).

Show solution

Input method = this good ÷ other.
Alice: 2/4 = 0.5 shirt.
Bob: 3/12 = 0.25 shirt.

Problem 9. Who has the comparative advantage in bread? In shirts?

Show solution

Bread: Bob's opp cost (0.25) <
Alice's (0.5) → Bob.
Shirts: opp cost of 1 shirt is Alice 4/2 = 2 bread vs.
Bob 12/3 = 4 bread → Alice lower → Alice.
Bob specializes in bread, Alice in shirts.

Problem 10. Give the range of terms of trade (price of 1 shirt, in bread) that benefits both.

Show solution

Between the two opportunity costs of a shirt: between 2 and 4 bread per shirt.

C. Conceptual

Problem 11. Can one producer have a comparative advantage in both goods? Explain.

Show solution

No. Opportunity costs are reciprocals: having a lower opp cost in one good necessarily means a higher opp cost in the other, so each producer has a comparative advantage in exactly one good.

Problem 12. Alice is absolutely better at making both bread and shirts. Should she still trade with Bob? Why?

Show solution

Yes. Gains from trade come from comparative, not absolute, advantage. If Alice specializes in shirts (her lower-opp-cost good) and Bob in bread, total output rises and both can consume more than by self-producing, so trade still benefits Alice despite her absolute superiority.
