A. Marginal Revenue & the Monopoly Outcome

(Problems 1–5 use demand P = 100 − 2Q and constant MC = 20.)

Problem 1. Write the marginal revenue function.

Show solution

Same intercept, twice the slope: MR = 100 − 4Q.

Problem 2. Find the monopoly's profit-maximizing quantity and the price it charges.

Show solution

Set MR = MC: 100 − 4Q = 20 → 80 = 4Q → Qm = 20.
Price from demand: P = 100 − 2(20) = $60.

Problem 3. If ATC = $30 at that output, find the monopoly's profit.

Show solution

π = (P − ATC) × Q = (60 − 30) × 20 = $600.

Problem 4. What quantity and price would a perfectly competitive industry (P = MC) produce with the same demand and cost?

Show solution

P = MC: 100 − 2Q = 20 → Qc = 40, and P = $20.
(More output, lower price than the monopoly.)

Problem 5. Compute the deadweight loss created by the monopoly. Then graph it out.

Show solution

DWL = ½ × (Pm − MC) × (Qc − Qm) = ½ × (60 − 20) × (40 − 20) = ½ × 40 × 20 = $400.

B. A Second Market

(Problems 6–7 use demand P = 80 − Q and constant MC = 20.)

Problem 6. Find the monopoly quantity and price.

Show solution

MR = 80 − 2Q.
Set MR = MC: 80 − 2Q = 20 → 60 = 2Q → Qm = 30.
Price = 80 − 30 = $50.

Problem 7. What is the competitive quantity for comparison, and by how much does the monopoly restrict output? Then graph it out.

Show solution

Competitive: 80 − Q = 20 → Qc = 60.
The monopoly produces 30, so it restricts output by 30 units (half the competitive amount).

C. Concepts

Problem 8. Explain why a monopolist's marginal revenue is less than the price.

Show solution

To sell an extra unit the monopolist must cut the price on all units sold (it faces the whole downward-sloping demand curve). The added revenue is the new unit's price minus the revenue lost on previous units, so MR < P.

Problem 9. Name three barriers to entry that can create or protect a monopoly.

Show solution

Any three of: economies of scale (natural monopoly), control of a key resource, patents or licenses, government franchise/legal barriers, or high start-up/network advantages.

Problem 10. What is a natural monopoly?

Show solution

A market where economies of scale are so large that one firm can supply the entire market at a lower average cost than two or more firms could: ATC keeps falling over the relevant range (e.g. water or electricity distribution).

D. Monopoly Profit

Problem 11. A monopolist faces demand P = 120 − 2Q and has total cost TC = 0.5Q² + 20Q + 300. (a) Find its marginal cost function. (b) Find the profit-maximizing quantity and price. (c) Find the firm's profit.

Show solution

(a) MC = dTC/dQ = Q + 20. (b) MR = 120 − 4Q; set MR = MC: 120 − 4Q = Q + 20
→ 100 = 5Q → Qm = 20, price P = 120 − 2(20) = $80.
(c) TR = 80 × 20 = 1,600;
TC = 0.5(20²) + 20(20) + 300 = 200 + 400 + 300 = 900.
Profit = 1,600 − 900 = $700.

Profit = 1,400 − 750 = $650.

E. Natural-Monopoly Regulation

Problem 15. Explain why forcing a natural monopoly to price at marginal cost (P = MC) causes the firm to lose money.

Show solution

A natural monopoly has declining ATC (large economies of scale), so MC lies below ATC throughout the relevant range.
Setting P = MC therefore makes P <
ATC, so the firm loses money on each unit and needs a subsidy to keep operating.

Problem 16. A natural monopoly faces demand P = 50 − Q and has total cost TC = 400 + 10Q. (a) Find its MC and ATC functions. (b) Under marginal-cost (socially optimal) pricing, find the price, quantity, and the firm's profit or loss. (c) Under average-cost (fair-return) pricing, find the price and quantity. Then graph it out.

Show solution

(a) MC = dTC/dQ = 10 (constant);
ATC = TC/Q = 10 + 400/Q (declining — the hallmark of a natural monopoly).
(b) P = MC: 50 − Q = 10 → Q = 40, P = $10.
Profit = TR − TC = 10(40) − (400 + 10·40) = 400 − 800 = −$400 (a loss equal to the fixed cost; it needs a $400 subsidy).
(c) P = ATC: 50 − Q = 10 + 400/Q → Q² − 40Q + 400 = 0 → (Q − 20)² = 0 → Q = 20, P = $30 (check: ATC = 10 + 400/20 = $30).
The firm earns zero economic profit.

Problem 17. A natural monopoly faces demand P = 80 − 2Q and has total cost TC = 450 + 20Q. (a) Find its MC and ATC functions. (b) Under socially optimal (P = MC) pricing, find the price, quantity, and the firm's loss. (c) Under fair-return (P = ATC) pricing, find the price and quantity. Then graph it out.

Show solution

(a) MC = dTC/dQ = 20;
ATC = TC/Q = 20 + 450/Q (declining).
(b) P = MC: 80 − 2Q = 20 → Q = 30, P = $20.
Loss = TR − TC = 20(30) − (450 + 20·30) = 600 − 1,050 = −$450 (needs a $450 subsidy).
(c) P = ATC: 80 − 2Q = 20 + 450/Q → Q² − 30Q + 225 = 0 → (Q − 15)² = 0 → Q = 15, P = $50 (check: ATC = 20 + 450/15 = $50).
Zero economic profit.

Problem 23. A monopolist faces demand P = 80 − 3Q with constant MC = 20. Find (a) the monopoly quantity and price, and (b) the deadweight loss vs. the competitive outcome. Then graph it out.

Show solution

(a) MR = 80 − 6Q; set MR = MC: 80 − 6Q = 20 → Qm = 10, Pm = 80 − 3(10) = $50.
(b) Competitive: P = MC → Qc = 20.
DWL = ½ × (50 − 20) × (20 − 10) = $150.

F. Antitrust & More Natural-Monopoly Regulation

Problem 37. What is the purpose of antitrust law? Give two example actions.

Show solution

To promote competition by limiting market power. Examples: blocking anticompetitive mergers, breaking up or restraining monopolies, and prohibiting collusion/price-fixing.

Problem 38. A regulated natural monopoly has MC = $10 and ATC = $25 at the relevant output. (a) What is the socially optimal (marginal-cost) price? (b) What problem does it create? (c) What price lets the firm break even? Then graph it out.

Show solution

(a) Socially optimal price = MC = $10 (allocatively efficient).
(b) Since $10 <
ATC ($25), the firm loses money and needs a subsidy to stay open.
(c) Fair-return (average-cost) pricing: P = ATC = $25, where the firm earns zero economic profit.

Problem 39. Why is an unregulated natural monopoly inefficient?

Show solution

Like any monopoly it sets P > MC and restricts output below the efficient level, creating deadweight loss, so regulation (or public provision) is used to push price and quantity toward the efficient outcome.
